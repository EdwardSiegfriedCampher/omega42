# Hindu tradition (the Upanishads in the translation of F. Max Mueller, Sacred Books of the East 1 and 15, 1879 and 1884; the Bhagavad Gita in the translation of Kisari Mohan Ganguli, the Mahabharata, Bhishma Parva, 1883-1896; public domain; wording checked against sacred-texts.com on 21 October 2026) -- frozen atlas, Core M309 step 1; chosen at the Originator's request of 21 October 2026 and written before any statement was struck; quoted, with the passage cited, the translators' parenthetical glosses, Greek glosses and vocatives removed
HIN 1 | In the beginning there was that only which is, one only, without a second.
HIN 2 | It thought, may I be many, may I grow forth.
HIN 3 | It is the Self, and thou art it.
HIN 4 | That from whence these beings are born, that by which, when born, they live, that into which they enter at their death, try to know that.
HIN 5 | That is Brahman.
HIN 6 | What is here, the same is there; and what is there, the same is here.
HIN 7 | He goes from death to death who sees any difference here.
HIN 8 | As pure water poured into pure water remains the same, thus is the Self of a thinker who knows.
HIN 9 | It is never born, nor doth it ever die.
HIN 10 | Weapons cleave it not, fire consumeth it not.
HIN 11 | Whenever loss of piety and the rise of impiety occurreth, on those occasions do I create myself.
HIN 12 | I am born age after age.
HIN 13 | On attaining to me there is no re-birth.
