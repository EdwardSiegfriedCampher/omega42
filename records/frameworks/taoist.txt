# Philosophical Taoism (the Tao Te Ching, in the translation of James Legge, The Tao Teh King, Sacred Books of the East 39, 1891, public domain; wording checked against Project Gutenberg eBook 216 and classics.mit.edu on 22 October 2026) -- frozen atlas, Core M309 step 1; chosen at the Originator's request of 22 October 2026 (records/frameworks/philosophy_selection.txt) and written before any statement was struck; quoted, the translator's parenthetical words removed and clauses taken alone
TAO 1 | The Tao that can be trodden is not the enduring and unchanging Tao.
TAO 2 | The Tao is like the emptiness of a vessel.
TAO 3 | The thirty spokes unite in the one nave; but it is on the empty space, that the use of the wheel depends.
TAO 4 | This returning to their root is what we call the state of stillness.
TAO 5 | There was something undefined and complete, coming into existence before Heaven and Earth.
TAO 6 | Passing on, it becomes remote. Having become remote, it returns.
TAO 7 | The movement of the Tao by contraries proceeds.
TAO 8 | The Tao produced One; One produced Two; Two produced Three; Three produced All things.
TAO 9 | The Tao in its regular course does nothing, and so there is nothing which it does not do.
