┌─ THE ANSWER ─┐
A human being is the place where the whole holds still long enough to be someone. The question lands on the still point of its world, the one kind of place in the cycle that stays itself while everything around it moves, and that is the first thing it says about a person: what makes you you is not any one of your states, which change at every step, but what holds through them. In the arithmetic this is exact: a state changes every step, and its world and its name, the orbit it travels and the number that labels that orbit, are kept forever. A human life is the turning; the human being is what the turning keeps. That stillness rests on its seed, the place of causes and origins: a person stands on what made them, a body, a lineage, a first condition that every later step carries forward. It holds time out of view, because a person lives in sequence without being made of sequence: time runs through a life, and the one living it is never found at any single moment of it. It is heading for a second still point, nine steps on, so the stillness that a person begins with is not the stillness they arrive at: the first is given, the second is made, a self that has become itself on purpose. And it is completed at a gap, an opening: what completes a human being is the place that no description fills, the room where judgment and meaning are made, which no rule and no reading can enter. Like the hub of a turning wheel, which is the one part that does not travel and the part every spoke depends on, a person is the still centre of their own motion, and the hub is hollow: the opening at its middle is what lets the wheel turn.

Conventional Connection: the long answers of philosophy run close to this. The persistence of personal identity through change is the oldest puzzle about selves, from the river that cannot be stepped in twice to the modern accounts that ground identity in continuity rather than in any fixed part; developmental psychology describes a self given at the start and a self formed later; and the thinkers of freedom place the human being where a gap opens between what has happened and what is chosen.

Practical Bridge: you are not your worst day, nor your best one; you are what holds across them, and that is the part worth tending. The second stillness is built, not found: the habits, commitments and relationships that a person chooses are how the given self becomes a made one. And the gap is not a flaw to close: the place in you that no label fits is where your judgment lives, and it is the one place no one else can decide for you.

Historical Lineage: the still point at the centre of motion is an old image, in the turning world with its unmoving centre of the poets, in the unmoved mover of Aristotle and in the hub of the wheel of the Daodejing, whose usefulness lies in its empty centre. This reading reaches the same figure by arithmetic: the still point, the kept orbit and the opening.

Implications: if a human being is what holds through change, then no single act, failure or label defines a person, and a person can change deeply without ceasing to be themselves. If the second stillness is made, then a life is a work and not only an inheritance. If the completion is an opening, then a human being is never finished from outside: the final word on what a person is belongs to that person.

**Takeaway: A human being is not a thing that changes but the stillness that changes keeps. You begin from what made you, you live through time without being made of it, you become a second stillness you choose, and what completes you is an opening that no description can fill and no one else can enter.**
