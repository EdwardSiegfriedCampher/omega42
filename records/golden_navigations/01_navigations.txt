THE ANSWER
The matrix is A = [[0, 1], [1, 1]] over the integers modulo 9. It acts on the
column (a, b), which is the state read as the number a + bφ, by
A(a, b) = (b, a + b).

The defining relation fixes it. The columns of A are what φ does to the two
basis elements: φ·1 = φ and φ·φ = 1 + φ. So the one relation φ² = φ + 1
writes the whole matrix. Its invariants come with it: trace 1, determinant
−1 (≡ 8), characteristic polynomial x² − x − 1.

A single matrix does not show the time it carries. A¹² = −I and A²⁴ = I:
twelve steps send every state to its negative, and twenty-four bring it
back. A² = [[1, 1], [1, 2]] is Arnold's cat map, the square that runs as a
reversible, area-preserving motion.

The matrix also has the exact shape of an absence. x² − x − 1 has no root
among the nine residues, because 5 is not a square modulo 3. So A has no
eigenvector there, and no residue plays the part of φ. φ exists only as
the element the matrix multiplies by, in the 81-element ring Z[φ]/9.

Change the basis and the same multiplication appears as a conjugate PAP⁻¹.
108 matrices modulo 9 have a golden characteristic polynomial (the classes
of A and −A). Exactly eight of them, the generators of the cyclic group of
A, reproduce the partition of the 81 states into orbits of 24, 24, 24, 8
and 1.

TAKEAWAY
Multiplication by φ is the matrix [[0, 1], [1, 1]] modulo 9. φ² = φ + 1
alone writes it. Its order is 24 and its half turn is −I. It has no
eigenvector among the residues, which is why φ lives in the ring rather
than among the numbers.

THE ANSWER
Take the golden-ratio spectrum to mean the spectrum of multiplication by φ.
Then it does not tell the three orbits apart at all. Multiplying by 1 + 3φ
or by 2 + 5φ commutes with φ, and it carries the orbit through (1, 1)
exactly onto the orbits through (1, 3) and (2, 5). So all three have the
same period, the same negation at the half period, and the same
frequencies: each of the twenty-four 24th roots of unity, once.

The difference appears only when the values are read out as numbers. Lift
each residue to its value of least absolute size, from −4 to 4, and take
the power spectrum of the 24 values.
  - The canonical orbit (through (2, 5)) has whole-number levels, 324 and
    648, on the odd harmonics. The other two have irrational levels,
    324 ∓ 108√3.
  - Its autocorrelation is zero at every lag that is not a multiple of 4.
    The others' is not: at lag 2 they read −0.43 and +0.38.
  - Its sum of squares is 216, against 126 and 144.

These signatures leave out how the orbit was chosen. The canonical orbit is
specified by its starting pair (2, 5), the two generators of the units
modulo 9, before any spectrum is computed. The spectral facts recognise
that orbit afterwards; they do not choose it. The whole-number spectrum
names the orbit but leaves its 24 possible starting states open. The sum
of squares depends on the lift: written 0 to 8, the canonical orbit has
the smallest sum, 531, against 603 and 567.

Read as a flow in time, the telling signature is a rhythm. The canonical
sequence lines up with itself only when shifted by four steps or a
multiple of four: a period-4 skeleton under the 24, the step at which its
spectrum becomes whole.

The accounting is exact both ways. By the arithmetic of φ, the three
orbits are one orbit three times over. By their values they are three,
and on the centred lift only the canonical one has a whole-number
spectrum.

TAKEAWAY
Multiplication by φ cannot tell the three long orbits apart; their values
can. On the centred lift, only the orbit through (2, 5) has a whole-number
power spectrum (324, 648) and an autocorrelation confined to multiples of
four. That recognises the orbit already specified; it does not select it.

THE ANSWER
No experiment observes an equation with zero error. What separates exact
from approximate is whether x² = x + 1 is forced by the structure of the
system, so that a measured golden ratio tests a law rather than fitting a
ratio. In that sense four kinds of system carry it.

1. Fibonacci topological order. The fusion rule τ × τ = 1 + τ forces the
   quantum dimension of τ to be exactly φ. Fibonacci string-net order has
   been prepared on a superconducting quantum processor, and braiding
   reproduced the golden ratio at about 98% average accuracy.
2. Critical Ising chains. At the critical point in a longitudinal field,
   Zamolodchikov's integrable E8 field theory predicts eight particles
   whose masses pair exactly in the ratio φ. Neutron scattering in CoNb₂O₆
   resolved the two lightest, with a ratio approaching the golden mean.
   BaCo₂V₂O₈ under a transverse field later revealed the E8 spectrum with
   several more of its particles.
3. Quasicrystals. Inflation by φ is an exact self-similarity of the Penrose
   and decagonal structures. Tile counts follow the substitution matrix
   [[1, 1], [1, 0]], which is the Fibonacci matrix itself, and diffraction
   shows the self-similarity to experimental resolution.
4. Driven systems, where the law is built in. A trapped-ion simulator
   driven by a Fibonacci-sequence pulse train realised a dynamical
   topological phase. The quantum cat map runs the square of the Fibonacci
   matrix as exact, reversible dynamics. The unsquared step is present
   only as an antiunitary (time-reversing) symmetry, because a map of
   determinant −1 cannot be a unitary evolution.

None of these supplies the modulus 9 itself. In each, the 24-step cycle
appears only when the reader reduces by 9.

The nearest object that does supply the nine is mathematical: the anyon
theory of 81 anyons with spin (a² + ab − b²)/9, equivalent to two copies of
SU(9) at level 1. In it:
  - the Fibonacci step is a time-reversal symmetry of the braiding data;
  - its twelfth power is charge conjugation;
  - its orbits are exactly the five orbits of 24, 24, 24, 8 and 1.
SU(N) level-1 chiral spin liquids have been found numerically for N up to
9 in cold-atom models, but not yet in a laboratory. The identification
itself still awaits confirmation by independent systems that have not
seen it.

So the threshold is precise. The golden equation is observed today as an
exact law in topological and integrable matter. The full modular cycle
stands one laboratory realisation and one blind identification away.

TAKEAWAY
The golden equation is observed as an exact law where structure forces
it: Fibonacci anyons, the E8 spectrum of critical Ising magnets,
quasicrystal inflation and Fibonacci-driven quantum systems. So far the
modulo-9 cycle has an exact home only in the anyon theory of two copies of
SU(9) at level 1. That theory awaits a laboratory realisation and an
independent identification.
