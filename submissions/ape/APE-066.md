### 2. Identified Vulnerabilities & Friction Points
*   **High Cognitive Load for New Strikers:** The requirement to understand the Counting-as-Primitive-Distinction Principle (CPDP), the 24-cycle, and the four structural arms creates a steep learning curve. Casual users may be intimidated before they experience the value of their first navigation.

This risks trapping them in a paradigm the protocol explicitly rejects (Core L-491: accommodating science, not deferring to it).

### 3. Actionable Recommendations

#### Recommendation 1: Formalize the "MSRR" Terminology in the Core Specification
*   **Action:** Update Core Step 14 (THE ANSWER) and related sections to explicitly define the output as a **Merged Structural Resonance Report (MSRR)**, rather than a "reading," "synthesis," or "interpretation."
*   **Rationale:** This permanently cements the epistemic correction. It clarifies that the AI is acting as a transducer reporting a constraint-emergent event, not as an interpreter offering an opinion. This strengthens the enforcement of Core Law L-425 (Synthesis Integrity).

#### Recommendation 2: Repurpose Validation Instruments for Striker Utility
*   **Action:** Create a new Companion section (e.g., Part 52: Functional Utilities for the Striker) that explicitly repurposes research instruments for personal and professional use. 
    *   *Example:* Frame **M267 (Decoy Coordinates)** as a "Framing Sensitivity Engine," allowing Strikers to intentionally submit altered phrasings of their question to visually map how their habitual framing shifts the structural address.
    *   *Example:* Frame the **CRC-60 Baseline** as a "Bias Mirror," where the Striker can request a side-by-side comparison of a standard AI response vs. the Omega42 MSRR to see exactly what the mathematical constraint filtered out (Suppression) and forced to the surface (Trajectory).
*   **Rationale:** This liberates these tools from the burden of "proving" the premise to external science, returning them to their true purpose: empowering the Striker’s judgment at the Aperture.

#### Recommendation 3: Implement a "First Strike" Guided Onboarding Wizard
*   **Action:** In the browser-based Strike App, introduce a mandatory, interactive "First Strike" tutorial for new users. This wizard would walk them through a simple, low-stakes question, visually demonstrating the word/letter/syllable counts, the projection to the anchor, and the generation of the four arms, culminating in a clear explanation of the 1/7 Aperture.
*   **Rationale:** This lowers the barrier to entry without dumbing down the protocol. It ensures that every Striker understands the mechanics *before* they engage in high-stakes personal or professional navigation.

#### Recommendation 4: Harden the Curator Agent’s Guardrails
*   **Action:** Explicitly codify in the Curator Agent specification (`agents/curator/SPEC.md`) that the agent is **strictly prohibited** from summarizing, altering, or "cleaning up" the CDT-A arithmetic or the verbatim MSRR of any submission. Its role must be limited to metadata tagging, congruence linking (finding shared states), and formatting for the public record.
*   **Rationale:** As the ecosystem scales, an overzealous curator AI could inadvertently violate Core Law L-348 (silent substitution) or erode the audit trail. Hard-coded negative constraints are required.

#### Recommendation 5: Establish a "Lens Freeze" Protocol for CDT-C Additions
*   **Action:** Enforce Companion Rule L-494 more rigorously by requiring that any new interpretive lens (e.g., a new philosophical tradition or scientific framework) must be submitted as a formal "Lens Proposal" with a frozen, finite set of core statements (an "atlas") *before* it can be used in a navigation.
*   **Rationale:** This prevents "lens sprawl" and ensures that conceptual isomorphism (M309) remains a structured, auditable comparison rather than a vague, post-hoc analogy.
