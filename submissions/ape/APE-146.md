This is ordinary discussion of the protocol, not a strike: no domain is put to the cycle, no Substrate Lock is issued (Rule 4, L-502). Part 59 already gives the seventeen avenues. What follows is not a restatement of them — it is a ranking of the concrete investigations inside them by the only axis that matters: can the avenue come back negative, and would we know?

An avenue that cannot fail cannot inform. That single test removes most of the tempting list, and it is why the ranking below starts where it does.

---

The ranking axis

Every investigation is judged on four things: is there a null; is the threshold fixed before the data; can the result be negative; and does a second party recompute it. An avenue with all four is worth running. An avenue with none is a way of generating prose.

TIER 1 — The avenues that could change the protocol's self-understanding

These are cheap, decisive, and the protocol currently does not know their answers. Every one of them can come back null, and a null in any of them would be a real result.

1.1 The decoy-coordinate control (M267) — the principal one. Does the coordinate contribute anything to a reading beyond the question's own words? The design exists and is frozen (records/m267_pilot_01/): twenty unexposed domains, canonical/decoy/no-coordinate conditions, a sealed key, a sign test fixed at 40 pairs. What would be interesting: the canonical readings preferred beyond chance with a stated margin. What would be null and equally interesting: canonical and decoy indistinguishable — which would show the value lies in the four-direction form, not the coordinate, and would move the whole project's centre of gravity without touching the premise (L-490). This is the only avenue in the entire protocol that can show the coordinate adds nothing. It needs writers and raters, which is why it has not run.

1.2 The within-Navigator baseline (E07). A model's text varies across runs at temperature 0 with a fixed seed. Nobody has measured how much one Navigator differs from itself on the same domain in two fresh sessions. Without that number, SR-CAS cross-architecture agreement cannot be interpreted: if within-Navigator divergence equals cross-architecture divergence, SR-CAS measures nothing about architecture. Cost: two sessions, one domain. This is the cheapest decisive experiment in the documents.

1.3 M225 reliability. T1.5-S verdicts are judgments. Is Cohen's κ ≥ 0.60 between two adjudicators of different architectures on the same quoted statements? If not, every "T1.5" number in every SR-CAS report is a single reader's impression wearing a decimal point.

1.4 The role-blind re-reading of the framework atlases. This session produced five religious and five philosophical readings, all scoring 0.90–1.00. A score that returns 0.90 for four frameworks out of five is a score of the scorer. The investigation: give a second Navigator the same frozen frameworks and the five M309 roles, blind to the first assignments, and measure agreement on concept-to-role filling. Interesting if: agreement is high (the roles are structural). Null if: agreement is at chance (the roles are the Navigator's habit) — and either answer tells you what every atlas in Parts 49, 50.14 and 75 is worth.

1.5 M279 ground sensitivity and M280 erasure. Both are pre-registered with frozen protocols (records/EPR_M279.txt, EPR_M280.txt) and both are level-3 discriminative validity tests. Change the ground and hold the procedure fixed: does the reading change systematically? Erase the coordinate where reconstruction is possible: does the reading survive? Interesting if: the reading tracks the ground. Null if: the reading follows the domain text regardless of ground — which is the single most informative null available, because it would relocate the entire mechanism claim.

1.6 The page forecast (U57). Before a new reading is made at a state that already has readings, freeze a forecast of what that page already shares. Outcome C of Companion 58.4 put to work. Cheap, cumulative, and it can fail cleanly.

TIER 2 — The avenues that could yield new structure (computable now, no panel needed)

These need no human raters. They are mathematical or computational, and each has a natural null.

2.1 Offset-space uniqueness — a computable gap I can name. The four arm offsets (+5, +18, +8, +12) are CDT-B constructions. The documents prove their generator orders are 24, 4, 3, 2 and that 4×3×2 = 24 equals Yang's order (FP-GeneratorOrder). They do not show the quadruple is unique under the stated design constraints. Enumerated by hand here: order-24 offsets mod 24 are {1,5,7,11,13,17,19,23} (8); order-4 are {6,18} (2); order-3 are {8,16} (2); order-2 is {12} (1). Impose the mod-4 classes (Yang ≡ 1, Yin ≡ 2, 4D ≡ 0, Antipode ≡ 0): 4 × 2 × 2 × 1 = 16 quadruples survive. The stated constraints leave sixteen candidates, not one. The investigation: enumerate all 24⁴ = 331,776 quadruples under progressively richer constraint sets and find which set is exactly tight enough to leave one survivor. RECONSTRUCTED by hand, needs script verification — and either way it is a real result about how under-determined the interface is, which bears directly on "the components are known; the configuration is the experiment" (L-508).

2.2 Identity mining with a rarity count. E01 (Anchor × Antipode − Yin² ≡ N) was found by searching 105 differences of arm products; three take at most two values and one equals the norm exactly. The investigation: search the full space of small algebraic identities among {anchor, yang, yin, 4d, antipode, norm, antipodal sum} — products, differences, powers — and ask how many hold on all 81 states, against a null of randomly drawn offsets. Interesting if: the count is what chance gives (then E01's interest is its rarity, computed). Interesting if: the count exceeds chance (then the arm system is algebraically richer than one identity). Either way it is exact, computable, and it has a null.

2.3 Reconstruction window scaling (M298). The rival-ground comparison found that six consecutive centred values single out the ground and the state among all orbits of the eleven grounds. The investigation: how does the minimal window scale with the modulus? Is six minimal for mod 9, and what is it for mod 6, 10, 16? A computable, publishable-shaped result about the reconstruction code (Part 37).

2.4 Cross-adaptor meetings at long word counts. The Korean finding — six of eight languages at P1 — is a word-count artifact, and the documents say so: two content words give r1 = 1 and the anchor stays at P1 with chance 8/81. The investigation: long, varied questions (15+ content words) across the nine adaptors, each meeting counted against its reference chance given word count (L-501). Interesting if: long questions meet beyond their reference. Null if: they do not — which would be the honest statement of what the adaptors add and do not add.

2.5 The Atlas page-by-page reading (G, U57). Nineteen domains on 15 of 81 states. The investigation: as the Register grows, read one page at a time and classify each page by Outcome A, B or C — never the whole record at once. Interesting if: a page shows agreement beyond the reference chance, the contrast and the echo. This is the only route by which the "picture of everything" question can be answered at all, and it is currently unanswerable because the record is 19 domains.

2.6 The homogenization check (E08). Once the record holds ≥3 readings at each of ≥3 states: are readings at different states as alike as readings at the same state? If so, the Navigator's template is speaking, not the coordinate. Cheap, and it can fail.

2.7 Extending the rival grounds. The Moduli study is exhaustive for period-24 moduli (a theorem: π(m) | 24 ⟺ m | 144). It is not exhaustive for other structural families (second-order cellular automata, graph dynamical systems, substitution systems). The investigation: extend the class and re-run the Minimal-Richness criterion. Interesting if: a rival meets all six yield properties. Null if: none does — which strengthens the ground's standing within its class while changing nothing about the premise.

The avenues that look interesting and are not

Named so they are not run by accident.

Adding traditions or lenses. Every framework fills five roles and scores 0.90–1.00. The instrument saturates; a sixth tradition adds a row, not a finding. What would be interesting is the blind re-read (1.4), not the addition.

Hunting 24s, 9s and 7s in nature. The number 24 turns up everywhere; the protocol's own depth scheme records it at depth 2 and does not count it (L-465). The look-elsewhere arithmetic is brutal: five frozen depth-2 values over counts 1–48 give a base rate of 5/48 = 0.1042, so six post-hoc tests fire at least once with probability 1 − (43/48)⁶ = 0.483 — nearly one in two. Post-hoc property hunting on 24 positions is worse still, because the per-property base rate is often a quarter or more.

Reading anchor concentrations in an atlas as resonance. The P1 concentration across seventeen framework statements is not a pattern among traditions. For any statement of two content words, r1 = dr(10) = 1 is fixed, and the anchor equals r1 with chance 73/81 = 0.901. Short definitional sentences land at P1 nine times in ten. The atlas is a map of word counts.

Treating a placement as a finding. Every countable thing has a position; a placement discriminates nothing (L-510, FC-02.8).

Extending the Bashar/Seth/Ra lenses. Registered, frozen, selectable by relevance — and adding a fourth atlas is a curation act, not an investigation.
