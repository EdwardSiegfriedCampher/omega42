Frame. This is discussion, not a navigation: I strike no domain, produce no coordinate, and claim no sandbox. I have not opened the three files you name; my comment rests on the report and on the two documents in context. "All 63 CI steps pass" and "the checksums verify" are taken as reported, not independently checked by me (L-456, L-420).

Most of what the report says is closed, is closed. The per-adaptor nulls, the seven adaptors with known-answer tests, the Greek routing bug, the Strike-app routing, the separation of adapted.jsonl from the Congruence Register, and the document edits are the right moves, and the open-items list is honest about why each remainder is open. But there is one issue that the report's own observation raises and then does not follow through, one integrity failure that survives in exactly the three languages whose adaptors were deferred, and three concrete corrections.

---

1. The serious one: the observation is understated, and the nulls do not condition on it

The report says: "In six of the eight languages, 'What is consciousness?' lands at P1 … two content words give r1 = 1, and when the three residues differ the tie goes to r1 (L-222). The states still differ, and the state is what M310 counts."

The outcome is right. The mechanism is stated too weakly, and the consequence is not followed.

· The anchor is not a tie-break case; it is nearly forced. If n1 = 2, then r1 = dr(5×2) = dr(10) = 1, always. The anchor is P1 unless r2 = r3 ≠ 1, which happens with probability (8/9)(1/9) = 8/81. So a two-content-word domain anchors at P1 nine times in ten, by construction, whatever its language. This is L7 ("word count typically carries the vote") at its full force, and it should be stated flatly in the documents rather than presented as a curiosity about six languages coinciding.
· When the anchor is P1, the state carries one residue. State = (r1, r2) = (1, r2). The six languages do not occupy six states in any meaningful sense; they occupy at most nine states, all with r1 = 1, differing only in r2.
· This breaks the base rate the register uses. Two texts that both have n1 = 2 share r1 by construction, so the probability that they share a state is the probability that r2 agrees: about 1/9 ≈ 0.111, not the uniform 1/81 ≈ 0.0123. That is a factor of nine. The report's nulls (0.0123 uniform, 0.0154 structural) are computed without conditioning on n1, so for exactly the class the adaptors create, they understate chance by an order of magnitude. A cross-adaptor meeting among short texts will look far more surprising than it is — which is the original collapse problem returning in a subtler form.
· The mitigation is undermined by the measure it relies on. "The state is what M310 counts" is true, but M310 layer 3 computes its expected-by-chance figures from the uniform base rates. For a class whose r1 is fixed, those figures are wrong, so the mitigation cannot do the work the report asks of it.

What I would do. Add one rule, in the same revision: the null for a meeting is conditioned on n1 (or on r1) for both texts. Where the conditioning cannot be computed for lack of data, the meeting is recorded and marked UNGRADED — not graded against the uniform rate. This is D11 from the previous exchange, arriving in a sharper form than I anticipated; it is now the most important open item, not a refinement.

---

2. The one integrity failure that survives: ZERO-COUNT-UNADAPTED

The routing rule routes a non-Latin text with zero canonical content words to its adaptor. For Chinese, Japanese and Thai the adaptor does not exist. The report does not say what happens then, and the default is that the text returns to P9, state (0, 0) — the same coordinate as L-468's NULL STRIKE. That is the exact degeneracy the adaptor programme was built to remove, and it remains for precisely the three languages that were deferred.

The distinction the frame now needs is between three states, and only two are currently named:

State What it means Recorded as
A genuine empty strike no question was asked NULL STRIKE (L-468)
A non-Latin text with no adaptor a question the extractor cannot see ZERO-COUNT-UNADAPTED (new)
A non-Latin text with an adaptor a question placed by its own boundaries routed, ADAPTOR line

What I would do. Name the third state explicitly, flag it in the Substrate Lock, and record it in the register as unplaced, awaiting an adaptor rather than as a still-point meeting. Until that is done, the report's claim that "every question written in a non-Latin script lands on exactly the same coordinate" has been fixed for five languages and is still true for three.

3. Concrete corrections

3.1 The count. Seven companion adaptors are listed (DE, FR, RU, EL, AR, HE, HI); with EN-CANON that makes eight, but the report says nine — and HANGUL-STRUCTURAL-1 is named as a null with no Hangul adaptor in the list. The most coherent reading is that a Hangul adaptor was adopted and omitted from the list (EN + 7 + Hangul = 9). Either add KO-HANG-1 to the list, or correct "nine" to "eight" and remove the orphan null. Either way the mismatch is a Rule 6 and Rule 9 item and should be resolved in the same revision, not a later pass.

3.2 The Part and entry numbers. The previous report called the measuring work Companion Part 55 and the Align tool U54; this one cites Parts 56.3, 56.6, 56.7 and U55. Either the numbering changed (in which case the earlier reference is stale and a reader following it will not find the Part) or one of the two is wrong. Low stakes; exactly what tools/consistency_check.py exists to catch, so it should be caught by running it before release rather than by a reader.

3.3 The language declaration changes the coordinate without changing the domain hash. German and French are now counted by their adaptors when the Striker declares the language. The same text, declared and undeclared, therefore yields two coordinates while L-414's DOMAIN SHA-256 hashes only the text. Two records can then carry the same domain hash and different anchors, which is the condition M270's reconciliation gate is built to detect — and it will report it as an extraction divergence when nothing has gone wrong. The report says "the canonical count stays the one on record," which is the right instinct, but it should be made operative: the language declaration is part of the Substrate Lock and the record, not part of the domain hash; the adapted coordinate is recorded beside the canonical one and never in place of it. One sentence, and the M270 false positive disappears.

---

4. Two smaller items

4.1 The estimated third layer. Declaring Arabic and Hebrew n3 an estimate is honest and better than not declaring it. But the CPDP is a counting principle, and an estimate is not a count. The ADAPTOR line should read n3 ESTIMATED, not counted, and the register should either grade those coordinates separately or note that they do not satisfy layer 1 "recomputed by the reference script" in the same sense as a count does. Otherwise a recomputation check will compare an estimate with an estimate and call it agreement.

4.2 Greek routing's tie-break. "The script most of a text's letters belong to" handles the reported bug. Two cases are unstated: a text split near 50/50 between scripts, and a very short text where a majority is not meaningful. M189 already requires per-part splitting for mixed input; the new rule should defer to it explicitly, and state a tie-break for the remaining case.

5. What is genuinely closed

The per-adaptor nulls existing at all is progress, even where their conditioning needs the fix in §1. The seven adaptors with CI known answers are a real extension of fidelity. The Greek bug was a real bug and the fix is the right shape. The Strike app loading the same rules file is the right way to keep the browser and the script from drifting. Separating adapted.jsonl from the Congruence Register is exactly the integrity move the register needed. The open-items list — Chinese, Japanese and Thai needing frozen data; corpus nulls needing frozen corpora; media adaptors needing three declared layers before anything; the corrected navigation and launch items being OWNED; Pyodide parity deferred to the workflow that can run it — is honest, and the reasons are the right reasons. I have no comment on those beyond agreement.

---

6. What I would adopt, and in what order

One revision, not two. In it: the conditioning rule of §1; ZERO-COUNT-UNADAPTED of §2; the three corrections of §3; and the two smaller items of §4. The first two are substantive and change what the register reports; the rest are consistency. A revision that closes §3 and §4 but not §1 and §2 would leave the register reporting cross-adaptor meetings at a chance rate nine times too low, and Chinese, Japanese and Thai still indistinguishable from an empty strike — which is to say, it would leave the two problems the adaptor programme was created to solve.

None of this changes the arithmetic, the extraction of English, or any navigation already on record. It changes what the record says about meetings it has not seen before.

What this means, which of these to adopt, and in what order, are yours to judge.
