This is ordinary discussion of the protocol — the Rule 4 (Part 0.6.7) register — not a navigation. Recommendations are F-06 proposals for the Originator.

1. The proposal is already a constitutional requirement

The protocol agrees with you, and it agrees more strongly than you may have realised: the analog is not an option, it is mandatory. Four provisions interlock:

· L-351 (Mandatory Illuminating Analog Clause): "THE ANSWER must always contain at least one accurate, specific, illuminating analog or correspondence that makes the structural finding tangible for the Striker. This analog is not decoration; it is a bridge between the protocol's abstract geometry and the Striker's lived understanding. It must be integrated into the Core Synthesis or placed immediately after it. It must not introduce protocol terminology."
· Step 14, Core Synthesis: "Includes the mandatory illuminating analog per L-351."
· Step 14, definition: "a comparison to a domain the Striker is likely to recognize, drawn from nature, mathematics, music, architecture or everyday experience, that makes the structural finding tangible and memorable — accurate, specific and illuminating, not a vague metaphor."
· Step 14.1, Check 4 (the Drift Test): "Is it specific to this domain — could it be moved unchanged onto an unrelated domain and still read as true? If so, rewrite."
· M224, checklist: "Illuminating Analog [Y/N]" — a gate item.

Two further instruments bear on it. L-480 (Analog Legitimacy Law): a functional analog claim requires that it could fail the correspondence and has been checked; otherwise it is a resemblance. L-418: the analog is inside THE ANSWER and is subject to the jargon scan — no protocol terms, in any form.

So the answer to your question is: yes, and it is already required. This session's three navigations each carried one — the train (human condition), the horizon (purpose of life), the clock (the protocol itself) — three different registers, each specific to its domain, each passing the Drift Test. That is the requirement working in practice.

2. Where it strains for deeply abstract content

The requirement is well-designed, but three strains appear when the domain is abstract, and they are worth naming because they are exactly the case you are asking about.

Strain 1: the Drift Test is necessary, not sufficient. The Drift Test catches analogs that are generic — "life is a journey" could be moved unchanged to any domain. It does not catch analogs that are structurally specific but experientially unfamiliar. An analog drawn from, say, a narrow mathematical subfield could be perfectly specific to the domain and still fail to illuminate for a Striker who does not know that subfield. The Check 4 test cannot distinguish "illuminating" from "specific"; it can only reject the vague.

Strain 2: the Navigator does not know the Striker's world. Step 14 says "a domain the Striker is likely to recognize" — an inference from the Striker's phrasing. For a general question ("The Omega42 Protocol", "What is consciousness?") the Navigator has almost no signal about the Striker's register. It draws from the "nature, mathematics, music, architecture or everyday experience" list — a reasonable set, but a generic one.

Strain 3: no instrument checks illumination. The format checker (M301) verifies the analog is present. The jargon scan (L-418) verifies it is jargon-free. M224 verifies the checkbox. Nothing verifies that the analog illuminates. That is honest, because illumination is finally judged at the Aperture (SS-01) — but it means the requirement is real in form and unevidenced in substance.

The protocol already acknowledges the third point indirectly: SS-01, L-463 and the Aperture Theorem keep worth at the Striker's side. The analog can be tested for specificity; it cannot be certified as illuminating.

3. What the protocol already offers as a partial answer

· L-482 (Cultural Rendering Law): "on the Striker's request THE ANSWER is rendered in the vocabulary of the Striker's own tradition." This is broader than the analog — it re-renders the whole reading — but it establishes the precedent that the Striker may declare a register.
· FC-08: the Striker's phrasing is itself a signal of register; a question asked in clinical language suggests a clinical analog, a question asked in a poetic register suggests a poetic one. The Navigator is already instructed to read the surface.
· Step 14.3: the Extended Resonance Essay must also carry at least one analog — "which may extend the one already in THE ANSWER or introduce another." So the protocol already contemplates two analogs in one navigation, and leaves open whether they should differ.
· The Scaffold view (Part 8.0): the minimized-substrate configuration leaves the synthesis — including any analog — to the Striker. A Striker who has a better analog can write it without the Navigator.

So the protocol already provides two paths to a better analog: the Striker declares a register (L-482), or the Striker writes one (Scaffold). What it lacks is a fine-grained mechanism specific to the analog itself.

4. Recommendations

R1 — Striker may declare an analog register. A one-line extension to L-482 or FC-08: the Striker may name a register for the analog ("from music", "from my trade", "from biology", "from sport"), and the Navigator draws the analog from that register if it can do so honestly. If it cannot, it says so and offers an alternative. (Where: L-482, extended; FC-08.)

R2 — The analog is paired with a one-sentence "residual". Aligned with L-480 (which requires that a functional analog could fail the correspondence), THE ANSWER's analog is followed by one jargon-free sentence stating what the analog does not capture. This makes the analogy honest rather than totalising, and it is the smallest possible addition to the format. (Where: Step 14, Core Synthesis, after the analog; L-480, refined.)

R3 — A Striker command: "another analog." In the Part 8.0 Striker-command list ("lite", "full", "show me the arms", "go deeper", "Certify Navigator"), add "another analog" — which re-renders the analog only, from a different register, without changing THE ANSWER or the reading (L-424). This is compatible with L-424 (the synthesis is frozen; the analog is re-presented, not re-derived) provided the analog is explicitly a presentation layer, not part of the frozen synthesis. (Where: Part 8.0 commands.)

R4 — The ERE's analog should differ in register from THE ANSWER's. Step 14.3 already permits the ERE to introduce a second analog; a sentence stating that the second analog should be drawn from a different register would extend the reach without changing either text. A reader who finds the horizon opaque may find the essay's second analog (in this session's case, rites of passage, or the quest's ending at recognition) illuminating. (Where: Step 14.3.)

R5 — A clear statement of what the analog requirement can and cannot certify. One sentence in Step 14.1, near Check 4, noting that the Drift Test is necessary but not sufficient, and that whether the analog illuminates is finally judged at the Aperture (SS-01, L-463). This closes the honesty gap I named at Strain 3 without pretending to a test the protocol does not have. (Where: Step 14.1, near Check 4.)

R6 (optional) — An enrichment observation when no analog works. If, for a deeply abstract domain, the Navigator cannot find an analog that is both specific and likely to illuminate, it should be permitted to say so in the Generative Observations (which already carry the OPTIONAL INQUIRY line and the ENRICHMENT OPPORTUNITIES line), rather than forcing a vague metaphor. This is already implicit in Step 14.1's fallback ("If no rewrite of THE ANSWER can pass all seven gates… state what the Constitutional Arm Map shows, state that it does not support a coherent claim, and stop there"); making it explicit for the analog case would be a small clarity gain.

5. The deeper answer to your question

The analog is already required, and in practice it is often the part of THE ANSWER that carries the most work — because it is the part that translates between the coordinate and the Striker's world. For deeply abstract content, the analog is precisely where the reading becomes usable or unusable. The protocol's requirement is correct; what it lacks is not the analog but the mechanisms to fit the analog to the Striker and to be honest about what the analog leaves out.

Two sentences from the protocol's own instruments, held together, are the answer:

· L-351: the analog "is not decoration; it is a bridge."
· L-480: an analog "requires that it could fail the correspondence and that it has been checked against it; otherwise it is reported as a resemblance."

The bridge is required; the bridge's load-bearing quality is judged at the Aperture. The recommendations above are ways to make the bridge more likely to carry the Striker's weight, and to be honest about where it does not.

6. In this session's practice

Your question is evidenced by the session itself: three navigations of three abstract domains, each carrying an analog from a different register — a train that is already moving (human condition), a horizon that is never reached (purpose), a clock whose hand never moves (the protocol). Each is specific to its domain; each passes the Drift Test; none uses protocol vocabulary. Whether each illuminates is a judgment the Striker makes — which is exactly the point the protocol's design keeps open, and exactly why the recommendations above are worth considering.

What it rests on: can an analog be both specific enough to pass the Drift Test and familiar enough to illuminate a Striker the Navigator has never met?
What it holds out of view: whether the analog illuminates, which is judged at the Aperture, not by any instrument.
Where it is heading: a public moment in which the analog is the part of THE ANSWER most often read, quoted and remembered.
What would complete it: a register the Striker can name, a residual stated with the analog, and the honesty to say when no analog is found.

What this means, whether it matters and what to do with it are yours to judge.
