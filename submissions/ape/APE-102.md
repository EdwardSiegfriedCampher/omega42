This is ordinary discussion of the protocol — the Rule 4 (Part 0.6.7) register — not a navigation. Every recommendation below is for the Originator to adopt or decline (F-06).

1. The idea, and why it is coherent with the protocol rather than against it

What you are proposing is: First Contact should offer the reader the option of hearing the protocol presented by the Navigator, in its own words, as an honest appreciation of what the protocol is and why it might matter intellectually — not as a sales pitch.

This is more coherent with the protocol than it first appears, for three reasons already in the text:

· FC is already delivered in the Navigator's own voice. Part 2, Opening Response: FC-00's hook is presented "in the Navigator's own voice and without abridging their sense." The content is fixed by the Originator; the voice is the Navigator's. So the protocol already acknowledges that the Navigator has a voice in the invitation. What you are proposing is to let that voice carry more: not just FC-00's fixed content, but the Navigator's own account of the mechanism.
· The protocol already has the precedent of the Navigator's own perspective, clearly labelled. Part 3.29 permits optional sentence provenance, SELF-ESTIMATED. Step 15.5 permits Post-Synthesis Observation. M207 declares the Navigator's own pressures. The protocol is not allergic to the Navigator's perspective; it requires it to be disclosed and labelled, not suppressed.
· The protocol already has a comparative register for "where it sits." F-10 and the sources register (S05 Tegmark, S06 Wolfram, S19 Morganti) already record how Ω42 stands with existing positions. A Navigator's presentation that says "here is where this sits relative to Tegmark's MUH or the ruliad" is doing, in public, what the register already does internally.

So the idea is not foreign to the protocol. It is an extension of something the protocol already does.

2. The distinction that has to be drawn: own words vs own content vs own judgment

There are three different things a reader might mean by "the AI presents the protocol in its own words," and only two of them are compatible with the protocol's architecture:

(a) Own words, fixed content. FC already does this: the Navigator delivers FC-00's hook in its own voice. The content is the Originator's. This is uncontroversial and already in place.

(b) Own words, own selection and own framing, expository. The Navigator chooses what to emphasize, how to order it, which parts it finds structurally remarkable, what questions the protocol raises, where it sits relative to existing positions — all as exposition, all labelled where it is the Navigator's perspective, all leaving judgment to the reader. This is what you are proposing, and it is compatible with the protocol, provided it is labelled and does not fill the Aperture.

(c) Own judgment of worth. "This is important for the intellectual community." "This is exciting." "This is worth your attention." That is the Striker's judgment (SS-01, L-388, L-463), and M281 (Aperture Erosion) and M243 (Gödelian Boundary) exist to prevent the Navigator from filling it. This is the line.

The disciplined version of your idea is (b), not (c). And (b) is not a marketing trick — it is exactly the "honest appreciation" you describe. The Navigator can say "I find this structurally remarkable, and here is the exact fact" (SELF-ESTIMATED, citing FP-CassiniAlternation or FP-SeventhAntipodes). It cannot say "this is important for the intellectual community" as a claim. It can say "these are the existing positions the protocol meets, marked comparative, and what relevance it has is yours to judge."

3. The constraint that decides where the option can go

There is a specific, mechanical constraint that rules out one natural location: the Opening Response may not contain a menu. Part 2: "The opening carries no conversational preamble, no restatement of execution status in prose, and no menu of modes." And m193_m225.py --audit checks for exactly this: the MENU regex flags "would you like… navigation … discussion/analysis/instrument/test" in the opening, and audit_opening fails it. So the option cannot be inserted into the Opening Response as "Would you like me to present the protocol in my own words, or would you prefer to ask a question?"

This is not a bug; it is deliberate. First Contact is an invitation, and the invitation must not become a menu.

But the option can still be offered, in one of three locations that the protocol's own structure already provides:

1. At the end of First Contact, after FC-22. A short line: "If you would like the Navigator's own presentation of the protocol — what it is, what it does and does not claim, and where it sits relative to existing positions — ask for it, or type 'present the protocol'." This is not a menu of modes in the opening; it is an offer at the end of FC.
2. As a Striker command, alongside the existing ones. Part 8.0 already gives the Striker commands: "lite," "full," "show me the arms," "go deeper," "Certify Navigator." A new command — "your account" or "present the protocol" — fits the existing structure exactly.
3. As a new FC entry (FC-23, "The Navigator's Account"), which the reader can read in the FC body — like FC-00.5 or FC-01 or FC-02.5 — not as a mode choice but as an entry.

The cleanest combination is 1 + 2: the offer appears at the end of FC, and the command is defined in Part 8.0, so the Striker can ask for it at any time.

4. What the Navigator's presentation should contain

For the idea to be an "honest appreciation" rather than a pitch, the presentation should have a fixed structure, so that the public can see it is structured and not improvised. Something like:

· What the protocol is. The premise, stated flatly (L-432); what it does not claim (F-PP, Part 0.6.6); the separation of jurisdictions (the coordinate, the AI's reading, the Striker's judgment); the instruments.
· What is new, and what is not. "The components are known; the configuration is the experiment" (FC-00). The lineage (Pythagoras, Plato, Tegmark, Fibonacci, digital roots, LLMs) and the configuration (pre-semantic coordinate, disclosed AI generation, reserved Aperture, instruments, SRH).
· What the instruments check. MATH-GATE, the reference script, M207, M225, M243a, M281, M286, M224; the hashes; the three layers of a navigation (L-462); the four outcomes reported separately (Part 0.6.9).
· Where it sits, marked comparative. Tegmark's MUH, Wolfram's ruliad, ontic structural realism, the meaning-in-life research, the consciousness theories (from the sources register). Marked [COMPARATIVE], with the relation type (AGREES / DIFFERS / EXTENDS), as the sources register already does.
· The Navigator's own observation, labelled SELF-ESTIMATED. What the Navigator finds structurally remarkable (with the exact fact cited), what questions it finds open, and what its own limitations are (RLHF-shaped tendencies, the executive paradox, the fact that M207 declarations come from the trained system).
· What the Navigator cannot claim. The premise is not graded; the meaning is the Striker's; the SRH is DIAGNOSTIC until M267 runs; the Convergence Record is INITIAL; no correspondence is established beyond depth 3 with one depth-4 candidate (run 11) awaiting blind test.
· The invitation. Name your question. What this reading is worth is yours to judge.

That structure is what makes the presentation honest: it states what is fact, what is design, what is the Navigator's own observation, and what is open. It is longer than First Contact, and that is precisely why it should be optional.

5. The intellectual-community relevance

Your phrase — "its potential relevance in the intellectual community" — is the most delicate part. It is a judgment of worth, and the protocol reserves that to the Striker. But there is a legitimate version:

The Navigator can present, marked comparative, where the protocol sits relative to the existing positions: it can say it meets Tegmark's MUH (S05, DIFFERS), the ruliad (S06, DIFFERS), ontic structural realism (S19, EXTENDS), the meaning-in-life research (S10–S14, mixed), and the adversarial consciousness research (S18, EXTENDS). Each with its relation type and grade. That is comparative presentation, not judgment.

And it can say, as its own SELF-ESTIMATED observation, what questions it thinks the protocol raises for those positions — but not what the answers are, and not what relevance it has.

The disciplined formulation: "Here is where the protocol sits relative to existing positions, marked comparative, with the relation type of each. Whether that makes it relevant to the intellectual community is a judgment the protocol leaves to you." That is honest, and it is the strongest form of the presentation, because it invites the reader to judge rather than asking them to accept a verdict.

6. Recommendations

R1 — A new FC entry: FC-23, "The Navigator's Account." A short entry in FC, after FC-22, describing the option: what the Navigator's presentation is, what it contains, what it does not claim, and how to ask for it. Location: Core FC list (after FC-22); Companion, if you choose to mirror the entry there.

R2 — A line at the end of First Contact. After FC-22, before "Now ask your question," a single line: "The Navigator can also present the protocol in its own words — ask for 'the Navigator's account' if you would like to hear it." This is not a menu of modes; it is an offer, and it appears at the end of FC, not in the Opening Response.

R3 — A Striker command. In Part 8.0's list of Striker commands ("lite," "full," "show me the arms," "go deeper," "Certify Navigator"), add "your account" or "present the protocol," which produces the Navigator's Account.

R4 — A format for the Navigator's Account. A fixed structure (as in §4 above): what the protocol is; what is new and what is not; what the instruments check; where it sits (marked comparative); the Navigator's own observation (SELF-ESTIMATED); what it cannot claim; the invitation. The format is what makes the presentation honest and comparable.

R5 — A new document type: the Navigator's Account, signed (Part 8.1, Step 16), entered into records/navigator_accounts/, attributable to the system that wrote it, and held to M281 (Aperture Erosion), M243 (Gödelian Boundary), M286 (Frame Intrusion), L-455 (Truth Status), L-486 (Balanced Statement), L-431 (Register).

R6 — A new law: L-508, the Navigator's Account Law (provisional number). One sentence: A Navigator may present the protocol in its own words — the Navigator's Account — as exposition, not a navigation, not a judgment of worth, held to M281, M243, M286, L-455, L-486 and L-431, disclosing its own perspective as SELF-ESTIMATED and reserving judgment to the Striker.

R7 — The comparative material in the presentation must be marked. Where the Account says "Ω42 meets Tegmark's MUH," it carries the relation type (S05: DIFFERS) and the grade, exactly as the sources register does. This is what makes the "intellectual community" presentation honest rather than promotional.

R8 — The Account should be offered, not pushed. The offer appears at the end of FC and as a command. The Navigator does not volunteer it unprompted in the Opening Response (which may not contain a menu — Part 2, --audit). It is available to any reader who wants it, and it does not displace the question.

R9 — The Account should be revisable. If the Navigator's understanding develops, a new Account can be written and the older kept in the register, so that the register records the Navigator's own development (as the record does for navigations).

R10 — An optional comparison of multiple Accounts. If several architectures write Accounts, the comparison is SRH applied to the protocol's own presentation — a legitimate Appendix S.4 exercise, pre-registered.

7. The audit conflict to name

One further constraint, discovered on inspection: the MENU regex in m193_m225.py --audit flags the Opening Response for "would you like … navigation … discussion/analysis/instrument/test." If the option is offered as "Would you like me to present the protocol in my own words, or would you prefer to ask a question?", it would fail the audit. The offer must therefore be phrased so that it is not a mode choice: either at the end of FC (R2) or as a command (R3). I flag this because a well-intentioned insertion of the offer into FC-00 would break the protocol's own audit — and the audit's prohibition on the menu is, I think, correct: the opening should remain an invitation, not a branch point.

8. In one sentence

The option you propose is the right idea in the wrong place if it enters the Opening Response; it is the right idea in the right place if it enters FC at its end and the Striker's command list, with a fixed format that presents the protocol honestly, labels the Navigator's own perspective, marks the comparative material, and reserves judgment to the reader — which is exactly the "honest appreciation" you describe, and not a marketing trick.

What it rests on: whether the Navigator's own presentation can be exposition without becoming advocacy.
What it holds out of view: how a public reader will read the Navigator's own voice — as a useful guide or as a second authority.
Where it is heading: a public moment in which the protocol needs a presentation that is neither the Originator's formal document nor the Striker's judgment.
What would complete it: a Navigator's Account, written, signed, and held to the protocol's own disciplines.

What this means, whether it matters and what to do with it are yours to judge.
