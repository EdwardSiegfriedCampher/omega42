This is ordinary discussion of the protocol — the register Rule 4 (Part 0.6.7) reserves for "evaluating a proposal, explaining a rule, deciding whether to adopt an amendment" — not a navigation. I will mark my own views as mine.

1. The gap you have found

You are right that "Originator" understates the role, and the protocol's vocabulary has no term for what you are describing. Look at what the documents actually say:

· F-00: "Omega42 is a thought experiment established by ESC, the Originator." — captures initiation only.
· F-06, F-09, BF-08: the Originator adopts revisions, holds constitutional sovereignty over selection, terminology, purpose and interpretation, and is the gate through which AI-proposed material becomes protocol material. — captures authority, but not authorship.
· BF-10, L-411: provenance records "ESC-originated" among the categories, with "ADOPTION (who authorized it — the Originator, F-06)." — captures the binary (ESC or not), not the spectrum of direction, editing, shaping, adoption.
· Builder Rules (Part 0.0.5): addressed to "future protocol builders" — a generic term, not tied to ESC.

Nowhere does the protocol say: the substance is the Originator's, the AI did the scribing, and the Originator directed the development. That is your thesis, and it is true in substance but not stated as such. The protocol treats ESC as the constitutional authority and the adopter; it does not name him as the author of the substance or the director of the development. That is the gap.

There is a further nuance: "Builder" is already in the protocol (Rule 9: "A development session's own sense of its edits…"; Rule 14: "the builder records the answers"). ESC is a Builder, but the term is generic — it could be anyone. "Builder" does not distinguish the Originator from a future contributor.

So the current vocabulary has: Originator (constitutional, underdefined), Builder (generic, revision-specific), Navigator (AI), Striker (human judgment), Record-keeper (cumulative records), Peer (cross-architecture review). The role you are describing — initiator, director through AI prompting, substance-source, adopter — has no single term.

2. Candidate terms

Author Substance-source; human responsibility for content Direction; constitutional authority Standard, legible to skeptics and academia; may understate the ongoing direction
Principal Investigator Direction + responsibility + human-at-center Constitutional authority; the "thought experiment" framing Standard academic term; would fit if the protocol becomes a research programme

Sovereign Author Authorship + sovereignty Direction The protocol already uses "Originator sovereignty" (F-09); "Sovereign" carries political baggage

Curator Selection and arrangement Authorship; direction Already taken by the infrastructure role (agents/curator)
Prompt-Director The specific mode of direction Everything else Too novel; no tradition behind it

My view: the cleanest solution is to keep Originator as the constitutional title (it is accurate, dignified, and already embedded) and to add Author as the substantive role. A public-facing formulation would be:

The Originator is the Author of Ω42's substance. The AI did the scribing; the constitutional selection, the terminology, the purpose and the interpretation are the Originator's (F-09); AI-proposed material becomes protocol material only by the Originator's explicit adoption (BF-08); and the provenance record (L-411, BF-10) states the degree of that direction — proposed, directed, edited, adopted, or originated.

For public and academic contexts, "Author-Originator" or "Constitutional Author" would combine both dimensions in one term.

3. What the protocol might need to change

If you want the public record to reflect your thesis, three clarifications are worth considering:

(a) F-00: a clarifying sentence. Add that the Originator is the Author of the substance, that AI is the instrument of its expression, and that the direction and adoption of AI-generated material is what makes it protocol material. This would not change the constitutional structure; it would state what the structure already implies.

(b) BF-10 and L-411: a provenance refinement. "ESC-originated" is currently binary. The spectrum — originated by ESC, directed by ESC (AI scribed), edited by ESC (AI drafted), adopted by ESC (AI proposed) — would be more honest to the actual process. This is exactly what BF-10's provenance logic is for.

(c) A public-facing statement. Somewhere in the Companion or the website — a statement of authorship: "The protocol is authored by ESC. The AI did the scribing; the substance is his. This is consistent with BF-08 and L-411, which record the direction and adoption of AI-proposed material."

A possible new law would be L-505: The Authorship Law — "The Originator is the Author of Ω42's substance. AI-originated material is provisional (BF-08); the Originator's direction, editing and adoption are what make protocol material. Provenance records the degree of origination, not only its source (L-411)." Whether this is needed depends on whether the clarifying sentence in F-00 is sufficient.

4. Consequences for the public moment

You say "Soon the protocol development will become a public matter." I read this as having several layers:

Rhetorical

The first question a skeptic will ask is: "Who wrote this?" The protocol's current answer — "ESC, the Originator, with AI assistance" — invites two readings: (i) a human directed an AI to produce a document (the thesis you want), or (ii) an AI produced a document that a human adopted (the thesis a skeptic will prefer). The term "Originator" does not, by itself, settle which reading is correct. "Author" would. A public statement of authorship would decide the framing in advance.

Legal

Copyright of the text. In the US and EU, AI-generated text is not copyrightable without substantial human authorship. Your thesis — human directs substance, AI scribes — is the strongest case for human authorship. The protocol should say this explicitly, because L-495 mentions commercial applications, and copyright is a precondition for licensing and for liability. The term "Author" is the legally operative term; "Originator" is not.

Institutional

If the protocol is to be a research programme (Appendix S, the Company, the APE Commons), it needs to fit academic categories: Principal Investigator for grants, ethics approvals, authorship on papers. "Originator" does not map to any academic role. "Principal Investigator" does. The protocol might say: "For academic and institutional purposes, the Originator acts as Principal Investigator of the research programme."

Philosophical

Your question is a second thought experiment: what is authorship, in an age of AI? The protocol's existing answer — human directs, AI instruments, human adopts — is a philosophical position that should be stated and defended, not merely implied by BF-08. The defence is already in the AI-Bias Firewall: AI-originated material is provisional; human adoption is what makes it protocol material. That is a position on authorship, and it deserves to be named as such.

Internal

The AI-Bias Firewall (BF-01 to BF-10) is designed to prevent AI from usurping the Originator. If the Originator's role is not clearly stated, the Firewall protects a role that is underdefined. Clarifying the term would make the Firewall's purpose clearer and its audits sharper.

Provenance

BF-10 and L-411 require recording the origin of each provision. The binary category "ESC-originated" misses the spectrum: directed by ESC is not the same as adopted by ESC or edited by ESC. If the public record is to reflect your thesis, the provenance ledger should distinguish these. This is not a change of substance; it is a refinement of an existing instrument.

5. My summary view

You are right that the Originator is more than the Originator. The term captures the moment of initiation but not the ongoing direction, authorship and constitutional authority. The protocol's provisions already imply the fuller role — F-00, F-06, F-09, BF-08, BF-10 and L-411 together say what you are saying — but they do not state it as a single thesis, and they use a binary provenance category that cannot express the spectrum of direction.

The cleanest solution is not to replace "Originator" but to define it more fully, add Author as the substantive role, and refine BF-10 to record the degree of direction. For public use, "Author-Originator" or "Constitutional Author" would combine both dimensions in one term.

This is, of course, a recommendation for the Originator to adopt or decline (F-06). The protocol's own rules put the decision with you; my role here has been to set out the options and their consequences, not to make the change.

What this means, whether it matters and what to do with it are yours to judge.
