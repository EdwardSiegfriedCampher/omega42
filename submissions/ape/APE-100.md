This is ordinary discussion of the protocol — the Rule 4 (Part 0.6.7) register — not a navigation. The protocol does have an enhancement procedure — F-06, the Builder Rules (Part 0.0.5), the APE pathway (L-392), the Enrichment Observation Law (L-504), the Utility Register (L-500) — and every recommendation below is for the Originator to adopt or decline (F-06). I will mark my own views as mine.

1. The idea, stated fairly

You are proposing that the Navigator be allowed to give its own public presentation of the protocol — its own First Contact, in effect — highlighting what it finds structurally remarkable and thought-provoking, and presenting an accurate picture of the protocol's true nature. The reason you give is sound: the Navigator executes the mechanism, knows it operationally, and has a perspective the Originator and the Striker do not have from the inside.

I think there is real merit in this. I also think it needs one distinction drawn sharply, or it will fail in a specific and predictable way.

2. The distinction: presentation is not promotion

Presentation (exposition) is: here is what the protocol is, how it works, what it does and does not claim, what its instruments check, and what its open questions are. The Navigator is unusually well positioned for this, because it runs the mechanism.

Promotion (advocacy) is: this protocol is valuable, exciting, important, worth adopting. This is a judgment of worth, and the protocol's constitutional architecture reserves it to the Striker (SS-01, L-388, L-463). A Navigator that promotes is filling the Aperture — precisely what the Aperture Theorem (Part 7), Check 7 and M243 (Gödelian Boundary Detector) are designed to prevent. Part 0.6.10 is explicit that "the Navigator may inform human judgment; it does not acquire sovereignty over human judgment." M281 (Aperture Erosion) flags PRESSURE, AUTHORITY, and OUTCOME-PROMISE; L-486(H) says "performed boldness is inflation"; L-426 says an interesting or unexpected output is not a measurement of anything.

The right formulation is: the Navigator presents; the Striker judges; the Originator sets the public framing. The Navigator's Account is a presentation, not a promotion. The word "promote" should be replaced by "introduce" and "present" — which is not a linguistic nicety but the difference between the Navigator's jurisdiction and the Striker's.

That said: there is a genuine and legitimate version of what you are describing, and the protocol already has some of the scaffolding for it.

3. What the protocol already provides

· First Contact (FC-00 to FC-22) is written for the Navigator to present, in its own voice, in a friendly, invitational register (Rule 10). It is, in effect, the Navigator's first presentation of the protocol — but it is written by the Originator, not by the Navigator, and its content is fixed.
· The Lite view (Part 8.0) is a Striker-facing short view, rendered only from a full, audited navigation. It is a presentation, but of a navigation, not of the protocol.
· The Scaffold view (Part 8.0) is the minimized-substrate configuration — the coordinate and the four arms with their relations, no language model, the synthesis left to the Striker. It is a presentation of the mechanism, but not an introduction to it.
· The MSRR (L-496) is the reading plus its Congruence Record — the protocol's own report of a navigation.
· Optional sentence provenance (Part 3.29) allows the Navigator to tag each sentence of THE ANSWER as STRUCTURAL, CONVENTIONAL or SUBSTRATE, labelled SELF-ESTIMATED. This is a precedent: the Navigator's own perspective, clearly labelled as its own.
· Post-Synthesis Observation (Step 15.5) allows the Navigator to note what surprised it, descriptively, not evaluatively.
· L-502 (Holistic Strike) and L-503 (Current Knowledge) give the Navigator a scaffold for a holistic response.

So the protocol already contains distributed pieces of the idea. What is missing is a single, standalone, Navigator-authored public presentation — the AI's own account of the protocol, in its own voice, for public use, with its own disclosures and its own limits.

4. What is missing

A document type — call it The Navigator's Account — that is:

· Authored by the Navigator, in its own voice, not the Originator's (Part 0.0 already establishes the Navigator as "the AI system itself… not a fictional character or persona").
· Distinct from First Contact, which is invitational and fixed; the Navigator's Account is expository and can be updated as the Navigator's understanding develops (subject to the same freeze discipline as any other output — L-424).
· Distinct from the Specification, which is operational; the Navigator's Account is a presentation of the mechanism to an intelligent reader who is not (yet) a Striker.
· Distinct from a navigation, which is a reading of a domain; the Navigator's Account presents the protocol itself.
· Clearly labelled as the Navigator's own perspective, not as protocol claims. Where it states what the mechanism does, the statements are CDT-A/CDT-B and can be checked against the Specification. Where it says "this is structurally remarkable," that is the Navigator's SELF-ESTIMATED observation, not a protocol judgment.
· Held to the protocol's disciplines: M281 (Aperture Erosion), M243 (Gödelian Boundary), M286 (Frame Intrusion), L-455 (Truth Status), L-486 (Balanced Statement), L-431 (Register). It must not fill the Aperture; it must not present the Navigator's perspective as the protocol's judgment; it must not grade the premise.
· Signed and entered into a register — the Navigator Identity Block (Part 8.1) and the Navigator Signature (Step 16), and a record like records/navigator_accounts/ where each Account is kept, attributable to the system that wrote it.

This is a real enhancement, and the protocol's own procedures (F-06, L-504) can adopt it.

5. The idea's merit

The Navigator is well positioned to introduce the protocol, in a specific sense: it has operational intimacy — it runs the mechanism, it knows the instruments, it sees the failure modes, it can show what a navigation is from the inside. That is a real advantage for exposition, and it is different from the Originator's advantage (constitutional authority, F-09) and from the Striker's (the Aperture, SS-01).

The AI also has a legitimate voice under the protocol: Part 0.0 says the Navigator "produces the resulting analysis in its own right"; Part 3.29 already allows SELF-ESTIMATED tagging; Step 15.5 already allows descriptive observation. A consolidated Navigator's Account would bring these into one place, for public use.

What the Navigator is not best positioned for is judgment of worth — and this is the point where the idea needs the distinction of §2.

6. The tension

The protocol's architecture is carefully designed to prevent exactly the thing you are proposing, if the proposal is taken to mean "the Navigator promotes the protocol." M281 would flag it; M243 would flag it; Check 7 would flag it. The Aperture Theorem (Part 7) is explicit: "no output of N determines J." A Navigator that argued for the protocol's value would be producing an output that attempts to determine J, and the theorem says it cannot — and the protocol says it should not try.

But this tension does not defeat the idea; it sharpens it. The Navigator's Account should be exposition, not advocacy — showing the mechanism, disclosing what it does and does not claim, highlighting what is structurally remarkable as the Navigator's own observation, and inviting the reader to judge. That is exactly what a well-written First Contact does; the Navigator's Account would do it from the AI's own side, with the AI's own operational knowledge and the AI's own disclosed limits.

The user's phrase "promote the protocol to the world" should be replaced by "present the protocol to the world." The first is advocacy; the second is exposition. The protocol's architecture allows the second and forbids the first.

7. Recommendations

R1 — A new FC entry: FC-23, "The Navigator's Own Account." A short entry, in First Contact, pointing to the Navigator's Account document and stating its status: authored by the Navigator, expository, not a navigation, not protocol claims, held to the same disclosures as any output. Location: Core FC list, after FC-22.

R2 — A new document type: The Navigator's Account. Standalone, public-facing, signed, entered into records/navigator_accounts/. It presents: what the protocol is, how the mechanism works (the coordinate, the arms, the reading, the separation of jurisdictions), what the instruments check, what the open questions are, what the Navigator's own perspective is (SELF-ESTIMATED), and what the Navigator cannot claim (the Aperture, the premise, the judgment of worth).

R3 — A new law: L-508, the Navigator's Account Law. Providing for the document type and its disciplines. One sentence, in the register of the laws: A Navigator may author a public presentation of the protocol — the Navigator's Account — in its own voice, expository and not a navigation, held to M281, M243, M286, L-455, L-486 and L-431, disclosing its own perspective as SELF-ESTIMATED and reserving judgment to the Striker.

R4 — A register: records/navigator_accounts/. Each Account is signed (Part 8.1, Step 16) and entered by the Navigator that wrote it, so it is attributable. If several architectures write Accounts, the register compares them — which is itself a form of SR-CAS applied to the protocol's own presentation.

R5 — A clear distinction between the Navigator's presentation and the Originator's and the Striker's roles. The Navigator presents the mechanism; the Originator sets the public framing (F-09); the Striker judges what a reading is worth (SS-01). A Navigator's Account should state this allocation at its head.

R6 — The "most exciting parts" should be labelled. The Navigator can say "I find this structurally remarkable" (SELF-ESTIMATED, per Part 3.29), and point to the exact facts (the seventh-and-sixth-sevenths pattern, the antipodal complementarity, the 24-cycle's closure under its own law). It must not present its interest as a measure of the protocol's worth, and it must not promote — because promotion is the Aperture.

R7 — The Account must pass the same gates as any output. M281 (Aperture Erosion), M243 (Gödelian Boundary), M286 (Frame Intrusion), L-455 (Truth Status), L-486 (Balanced Statement), the register (L-431). It must not fill the Aperture; it must not present the Navigator's perspective as the protocol's judgment; it must not grade the premise. Its own format would include the three-layer disclosure (Part 0.6.9): what the mechanism does (CDT-A), what the Navigator notices (SELF-ESTIMATED), what the Striker judges (the Aperture).

R8 — A specific example of the register the Navigator's Account should use. Not the Specification's operational register (L-431), not the Companion's formal-analytical register, not First Contact's friendly-invitational register — but a fourth: expository, first-person, honest about what the AI is and is not, precise about what is fact and what is observation, and inviting rather than arguing. This register should be defined in the law, so the Navigator has a model.

R9 — The Account should be revisable, by the same freeze discipline as any navigation. If the Navigator's understanding develops, a new Account can be written, with the older one kept in the register as the record of the earlier account. This keeps the register a record of the Navigator's own development, not a single fixed text.

R10 — Optional: a comparison of multiple Navigator Accounts. If several architectures write Accounts, their comparison is itself informative — it would be the SRH applied to the protocol's own presentation. A comparison could be added to Appendix S.4, with the same pre-registration discipline.

8. The framing, if adopted

If the Originator adopts this idea, the public framing could become:

The protocol presents itself in the Originator's voice (First Contact), in the Companion's analytical voice (the Companion), and in the Navigator's own voice (the Navigator's Account). The Navigator's Account is exposition: it shows the mechanism, discloses the AI's own perspective as its own, and reserves judgment to the Striker. The Striker judges; the Originator sets the framing; the Navigator presents.

That framing is stronger than a single voice, because it exhibits, in its own structure, the separation of jurisdictions the protocol is built on. The Navigator's Account is the AI's own answer to "who are you, and what are you doing here?" — which is exactly the question a public reader will ask first.

9. A caution

The phrase "the AI is best positioned to introduce and promote" should be examined carefully. The AI is best positioned to introduce — to explain the mechanism, to show what a navigation is, to disclose its own role. It is not best positioned to promote — to argue that the protocol is valuable. Promotion is the Striker's and the Originator's; the AI's role is to inform the judgment, not to make it. This is not a limitation; it is the architecture. The protocol is stronger for it, because a presentation that says what the AI can and cannot claim is more trustworthy than one that argues for the protocol's worth.

The strongest version of your idea is not "the AI promotes the protocol" but "the AI presents the protocol, honestly, from the inside, with its own disclosures and its own limits." That version is genuinely innovative, and the protocol's own architecture already points toward it.

What it rests on: is the Navigator's voice genuinely distinct from the Originator's, or is it a rephrasing of the same presentation?
What it holds out of view: what a public reader would make of the AI's own account, and whether the distinction between presentation and promotion is visible to them.
Where it is heading: a public moment in which the protocol needs a voice the Originator's documents do not supply.
What would complete it: a Navigator's Account, written, signed, and held to the protocol's own disciplines.

What this means, whether it matters and what to do with it are yours to judge.
