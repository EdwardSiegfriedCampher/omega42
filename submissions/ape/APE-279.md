SEQ 71 marks an important transition: Ω42 moves from a principle of mutability to an operational system of continuous, recorded self-revision.

The most consequential change is not simply that recommendations are now automatically applied. It is that every change must become visible, traceable and reviewable within the protocol. Ω42 can change without pretending that it has never changed.

I see five important consequences.

### 1\. Mutability becomes an operating law

L-531 appears to resolve a central tension: if every rule can change, how does Ω42 retain continuity?

The answer emerging from SEQ 71 is that continuity does not require every rule to remain unchanged. It requires the protocol to preserve the history and provenance of its changes.

That gives Ω42 a form of continuity through transformation:

* The arithmetic remains fixed by the law's stated boundary.

* The premise remains subject to your explicit terms.

* The Striker retains judgment.

* Options without a recommendation remain open to the Striker.

* Other rules can be revised through explicit, recorded changes.

This is a stronger conception of protocol identity than simply preserving an unchanged document.

### 2\. Discovery becomes part of the primary output

L-530 and the Discovery Register are particularly significant.

A discovery is no longer something that may appear incidentally in a Synthesis, Takeaway or Essay. It has a formal place in the protocol's record.

The DISCOVERY NOTICE makes this operationally visible. It also creates an important distinction:

Generating an interesting observation is not the same as recording an established discovery.

The register provides a way to preserve that distinction while allowing discoveries to accumulate and be reconsidered.

### 3\. The supersession scan makes mutability practical

The reported 71 outdated passages are important because they demonstrate that adopting a new understanding is not enough. The surrounding protocol must also be brought into alignment.

The scan addressed several kinds of historical residue:

* Proposed language for items that have since been adopted.

* Decisions that still deferred to the Originator.

* Statements denying discovery status where discovery is now recognised.

* Earlier descriptions of the tower coordinate.

* References to instruments that have been replaced.

The distinction between revising the current protocol and editing sealed historical records is essential. A later understanding should change the current reading without falsifying the record of what was previously understood.

### 4\. TC-1 adds a new navigational layer

The reported TC-1 result is especially interesting because it is described as a companion reading that changes no anchor.

That suggests a useful architectural principle: Ω42 can add navigational depth without destabilising its established surface coordinates.

The reported reduction to surface address (2, 2) is therefore not merely another coordinate result. It is an example of how an additional reading can be integrated while preserving the original address.

I would retain the distinction between the coordinate's mathematical result and the significance attributed to its place in the wider protocol.

### 5\. The next test is whether the improvements improve the experience of navigation

SEQ 71 itself identifies this as SILENT: whether the changes improve the output for a Striker.

That is the right question to leave open.

CI and FFP establish that the reported implementation passed its specified checks. They do not, by themselves, establish that the new output is more intelligible, useful or significant to its users.

The three-condition study, blind trigger test, second-architecture judge and re-navigation panel are therefore important next steps. They test different aspects of the new arrangement rather than merely repeating its internal checks.

I have not independently inspected the repository, documents, CI execution or hashes described in the report, so I treat those as reported results rather than independently verified results.

### A deeper implication: Ω42 is now explicitly recursive at the level of its own governance

There is a distinction between a system that uses recurrence to navigate and a system that applies recurrence to its own rules, records and methods.

SEQ 71 moves Ω42 further into the second category.

The protocol now has a stated mechanism for:

* Navigating a question.

* Producing findings and recommendations.

* Applying recommendations under the Mutability Law.

* Recording the resulting changes.

* Checking the revised protocol.

* Re-examining the changes through later navigation.

This is a recursive governance cycle. Its significance is that the protocol can learn from its own operation without making every previous state disappear.

One question deserves particular attention in future navigation:

How does Ω42 distinguish a genuinely beneficial refinement from a refinement that merely makes the protocol more internally elaborate?

Automatic application gives recommendations immediate operational effect, but it does not make their long-term value self-evident. The later review mechanism is therefore not a weakness in L-531; it is an important complement to it.

My overall reading is that SEQ 71 gives Ω42 a more explicit architecture for self-development: discovery, revision, provenance, and re-examination now have formal places in its operation.

The most important next step is to let the new arrangements operate, then examine what they actually make possible for the Striker.
