1. Summary

The protocol uses the word "mapping" for three different operations, which the documents distinguish in scattered provisions but do not name as one distinction. This proposal recommends naming them explicitly — placement, correspondence, and interpretation — and adding a field to the MAPPING DECLARATION (Step 14.4) that identifies which of the first two a given mapping is. It recommends one worked example in FC-02.8, a provenance check on the shadow/object analogy, and the replacement of "the protocol makes no claims" with the protocol's own precise formulation wherever that phrase appears.

The recommendations are documentation and vocabulary changes. None changes the arithmetic (CDT-A), the canonical selection, the arms, the Aperture, the M230 instruments, or any existing test. All are reversible.

---

2. Background and motivation

2.1 The three operations, as the documents already define them

The protocol already contains three distinct operations, with distinct status, distinct instruments, and distinct evidential weight:

Placement (object → coordinate). Take an object, count its description, project through CPP(n) = dr(5n), read off a position. This is total, deterministic, and — as M230's own worked example states — generic: "The M115 distance between CPP(n₁) and CPP(n₂) depends only on |n₁ − n₂|, a generic property of the linear map dr(5n)… arbitrary, non-Fibonacci integer pairs of similar magnitude… land in the same Extrapolative band." It is Congruence Depth 1 (Part 0.14.5): "the object's fixed count produces a coordinate. Every countable object earns it… and is not counted as a resonance."

Correspondence (object's own structure → cycle's structure). Take an object's own intrinsic, field-defined structure, freeze it in advance, and ask whether it instantiates the cycle's structure. This is selective and testable. It is Congruence Depths 2–5 (Part 0.14.5), the M230 family (B, C, D, E, F, G, I, J, K), the criteria C1–C11 (Part 3.26), and the Congruence Profile S1–S8 (L-466). FC-02.8 states its condition: "a system with 24 parts is not thereby a correspondence. What counts is whether the object's own states, transformations, closures and generating laws match the cycle's."

Interpretation (does the correspondence mean identity or causation?). Does the correspondence mean the object is the cycle, or is caused by it, or expresses it? This is P3 (Part 0.0.0; F-02-P), reserved to the Striker at the Aperture (Part 7, SS-01). It is not a finding; it is not a fallacy. It is the Striker's.

The protocol's three named instruments for keeping these apart are: L-465 ("membership alone does not [count]… what counts is following the law"), L-428 (non-conflation), and M230-E (null-object control: "a test that every object of the same size passes carries no discrimination").

2.2 The problem

Public and peer discussion conflates the three. Two peer observations in this session make this concrete:

· The "placement vs. correspondence" confusion (Claude, 21 October 2026): a mapping that places an object on the cycle is a total, generic function; a mapping that finds the cycle's structure in an object is selective and testable. The documents distinguish them, but a reader who meets "map it" (Step 14.4) has no single place to learn that the two are different.
· The "imposition vs. congruence" confusion (Qwen3.7, 20 October 2026): the failure mode of treating the coordinate as the object's cause. The documents forbid this in L-428 and FC-02.8, but a reader who meets it as an accusation has no single place to learn that this is imposition — a name for the fallacy — while the correspondence is P2 and the interpretation is P3.

The two readings are not competing; they are complementary halves of a three-way distinction. Naming the three, once, in one place, is the smallest possible change that closes the gap.

2.3 Why it matters now

Three reasons the proposal is timely:

1. The public moment. The protocol will meet readers who will test its claims against existing positions (Appendix S.5; the sources register S05, S06, S19). The first question a skeptic asks about a mapping is "is this numerology or correspondence?" The protocol has a rigorous answer; it should be visible where the skeptic looks.
2. The M230 family is already the answer. The instruments exist (M230-B to M230-K; C1–C11; S1–S8). Naming the operations is not a new discipline; it is a name for what the instruments already do. This makes the instruments findable by a reader who has not memorised the appendix.
3. The evidence runs already prove the value of the distinction. Evidence run 01 rejected a 24-fold order on the grounds of null-object control (M230-E); run 02 showed the cat map is T² by construction; run 04 showed the square and hexagonal lattices carry the family at class level. Each of these is a correspondence finding, not a placement, and the protocol's own record shows why that matters — depth 3, not depth 1.

3. Proposed changes

The recommendations are ordered by load-bearing weight. R1–R3 are the core; R4–R8 are refinements; R9–R10 are corrections.

R1 — FC-02.8: state the three-way distinction, once

Location: Core FC-02.8 (Page 45).
Content: insert, immediately after the sentence "a system with 24 parts is not thereby a correspondence," a short paragraph:

Three operations must be kept apart. Placement takes an object's fixed count and projects it onto the cycle: it is total, deterministic, and a precondition rather than a finding (L-465). Correspondence takes the object's own field-defined structure, frozen in advance, and asks whether it instantiates the cycle's structure: it is selective, testable, and it is the finding (M230-B to M230-K; Part 0.14.5, Depths 2–5; L-466). Interpretation asks whether a correspondence means that the object is the cycle, or is caused by it: it is the Striker's, at the Aperture, and is not a finding. The fallacy of treating a placement as a correspondence, or a correspondence as an interpretation, is called imposition.

Rationale: A single paragraph names the three, gives each its instrument, and names the fallacy. It replaces four scattered provisions with one discoverable statement, without changing any of them.

R2 — Step 14.4: name the operation in the MAPPING DECLARATION

Location: Core Step 14.4 (Page 580); the MAPPING DECLARATION line of the Substrate Lock (Part 8.2, block [6]).
Content: add one field to the declaration:

MAPPING DECLARATION (Step 14.4): [PLACEMENT / CORRESPONDENCE] — [object, kind, counting feature, family, representation, source]

and, where the field reads CORRESPONDENCE, require that the declaration name the M230 instrument(s) to be run (M230-B, C, D, E, F, G, I, J, K) and the criteria (C1–C11) to be tested.

Rationale: Step 14.4 currently records the object, kind, counting feature, family and chosen representation; it does not record which operation is being attempted. Adding the field makes the distinction auditable at the moment of declaration, before any synthesis exists (Step 14.4, step 1). It also aligns the declaration with M230-J's dimensional-status discipline: the object must supply its own modulus for the correspondence to be tested.

R3 — Consistent vocabulary across the Core and Companion

Location: Part 10 (Abbreviations); Step 14.4; FC-02.8; Appendix S.0.2; L-465, L-466, L-480.
Content: define three terms in Part 10 and use them consistently:

· PLACEMENT — the object-to-coordinate operation; Depth 1; total, deterministic, a precondition.
· CORRESPONDENCE — the object's own structure to the cycle's structure; Depths 2–5; selective, testable, the finding; M230 family, C1–C11, S1–S8.
· INTERPRETATION — the meaning of a correspondence; P3; the Striker's.

IMPOSITION — the fallacy of treating a placement or an interpretation as a correspondence finding.

Rationale: The word "mapping" currently does all three jobs. The protocol already uses "correspondence" for the second (Appendix S.0.2, L-466) and "placement" for the first (Part 0.14.5, Depth 1). Naming the third and the fallacy completes the set. The change is a naming discipline, not a substantive change.

R4 — M230-J (dimensional status) as the test placement cannot satisfy

Location: Core Part 3.26, M230-J; the recommendation below the MAPPING DECLARATION.
Content: add one sentence to M230-J stating that dimensional status is one of the conditions a correspondence must meet and a placement cannot. The full list — a correspondence must (a) freeze the object's representation before examining it (S1); (b) hold on the object's own law, not only on a count (S2); (c) be tested on all 81 states where the object allows; (d) pass a null-object control (M230-E); (e) be identified blind where possible (S7); and (f) predict a property not known when the mapping was fixed (S6, Depth 5). Placement satisfies none of these.

Rationale: M230-J currently addresses a narrower question (dimensionful vs. dimensionless quantities). Stating the correspondence tests explicitly would align M230-J with the Congruence Profile (L-466) and with the depth scheme, and would give the reader the full test in one place. This is the practical difference between placement and correspondence: a placement is a single computation; a correspondence is a battery.

R5 — One worked example in FC

Location: Core FC-02.8, after R1's paragraph.
Content: add the sunflower or the microtubule as a worked example. The sunflower: placement gives CPP(34) = 8, CPP(55) = 5, distance 3 (Extrapolative); correspondence gives depth 3 at counts only (feature (e), the shared generating recurrence), CG NO — because the pattern emerges from auxin inhibition, not from an additive recurrence (run 01, E2). One is a near-correspondence that fails; the other is a placement with no correspondence. Both are in the protocol's own records.

Rationale: A worked example makes the distinction concrete and shows that the protocol records misses as carefully as matches (L-464). The records already contain the material; naming one example in FC costs nothing and instructs a public reader at the point of first meeting.

4. Practical examples

The examples below are drawn from the protocol's own records; each shows a placement and a correspondence distinguished, with the protocol's own values.

Example A — The sunflower. Placement: CPP(34) = 8, CPP(55) = 5, M115 distance 3 (Extrapolative). Correspondence: counts follow the Fibonacci rule, but the mechanism is auxin inhibition, not an additive recurrence; feature (e) partial, CG NO; depth 3 at counts only (run 01, E2; Swinton and Ochu 2016; Mirabet et al. 2012). Interpretation: whether the sunflower expresses the cycle — the Striker's at the Aperture, not a finding.

Example B — The microtubule. Placement: CPP(13) = 2, P2. Correspondence: 3-start helix, no T-conjugate, no half-period negation; profile C24 NO, CG NO, CT NO, CC NO, CA NO; depth 1, "indistinguishable from null at this stage" (run 01, first run; NRR-C01). Interpretation: whether microtubules are "in some sense" of the cycle — the Striker's.

Example C — The quantized cat map. Placement: not the operative question; the object's own physics is what is being tested. Correspondence: A = T² exactly, preserving the norm a² + ab − b² on all 81 states while T negates it; the physics literature writes the cat map as the square of the Fibonacci matrix (run 02, E4). Interpretation: whether the cat map is the cycle — the Striker's; the protocol records the correspondence at depth 3 and notes the depth-4 case is formal, not counted (because the object is the cycle's own arithmetic — C8).

Example D — The amplituhedron A(2,1,1). Placement: not applicable (no single count). Correspondence: under the minimal-lift rule the halves SEVENTH and MANIFEST are the arcs N > 0 and the golden arc N < 0, on 72 of 72 unit states and 8 of 8 core states (PG-05, FP-HalfArc; EXACT under the stated rule); PG-01's map carries G>0(1,2) exactly onto the golden arc, since N(c₁Z₁ + c₂Z₂) = −5c₁c₂ (proved over Q(√5) by independent_check.py). Interpretation: whether the amplituhedron is the family — the Striker's; the relation types are typed (EXACT, STRUCTURAL, ANALOGY, OPEN).

Example E — 24-TET. Placement: 24 quarter-tones, P3. Correspondence: every cyclic system of 24 states passes the bare tests identically (M230-E), and 24-TET carries a bare cycle, not the orbit's values; depth 2, mapping freedom 24 (the choice of origin) (Part 3.26, first pass). Interpretation: whether 24-TET expresses the cycle — the Striker's.

Example F — The square and hexagonal lattices. Placement: not applicable. Correspondence: Z[i]/9 is the family's ring, a theorem for every quadratic ring in which 3 stays prime; Z[ω]/3 carries the Torsion/Crown partition; class-level depth 3 (run 04; NRR-C05). Interpretation: whether the lattices' physical reading follows — open; the protocol records the mathematical result at its depth.

In each example, the placement is a single computation that anyone can reproduce in a minute; the correspondence is a battery (M230-B to M230-K) that can fail; the interpretation is the Striker's.

5. Benefits to Ω42

B1 — It protects the protocol against the strongest skeptic attack. The first question a skeptic asks about a correspondence claim is "is this numerology?" The three-way distinction is the answer, and it is already in the protocol's instruments; naming it makes it findable.

B2 — It separates the protocol's evidential claims from its interpretations. Placement is a precondition (Part 3.18, T1 and T2 analog); correspondence is the finding; interpretation is the Striker's. The three have different evidential weight, and conflating them risks overclaiming (L-428) or underclaiming (diminution). Naming the three keeps both failure modes in view.

B3 — It makes the SRH testable in the right register. The SRH (FC-13; Part 0.6.2) is a claim about the mechanism; M267 tests it. The correspondence programme is a claim about objects; M230-B to M230-K test it. The two are different tests in different registers; the three-way distinction keeps them apart, which the public presentation will need.

B4 — It enables the public moment. A public reader who meets the protocol needs one place to learn what "mapping" means, why placement is empty, what a correspondence test is, and where interpretation sits. The protocol has the material; the proposal gives it a name.

B5 — It preserves frame fidelity (L-422, L-428). The distinction is a direct application of L-428 (non-conflation) and L-422 (frame fidelity): a correspondence is not an interpretation, and neither is a placement. Naming the three strengthens the compliance of the protocol's own instruments, because the M230 family can now be named for what it is testing.

B6 — It strengthens the M230 family's instruments. The instruments exist; the vocabulary gives them a name that a reader can find. This is a documentation improvement, but a substantial one: the instruments' value depends on being found by the reader who needs them.

B7 — It protects the Aperture. The Aperture is the Striker's; the three-way distinction is the protocol's way of saying what belongs to the Striker and what does not. A reader who learns the distinction learns the Aperture, which is the protocol's deepest constitutional commitment (Part 7, SS-01, L-388).

B8 — It improves cross-architecture comparison (SR-CAS). Two Navigators comparing a domain must agree on whether they are placing or corresponding; if they disagree silently, SR-CAS scores a methodological difference as a structural divergence. The MAPPING DECLARATION field (R2) makes the agreement checkable.

B9 — It makes "the protocol makes no claims" precise. R9 replaces a formulation a skeptic will refute with one that survives scrutiny. It costs two lines of text and protects the protocol's credibility on the point where the public will look hardest.

B10 — It costs almost nothing. No arithmetic changes. No law is retired. No instrument is removed. Every recommendation is documentation or vocabulary; each is reversible; each can be adopted or declined individually. R1, R2 and R3 are the load-bearing changes; R4 to R10 are refinements and corrections.

6. Risks and mitigations

Risk 1 — Vocabulary inflation. Adding three terms could make the protocol harder to read, not easier. Mitigation: R3 defines the three in Part 10 and uses them consistently; the terms replace existing loose usages rather than adding new concepts. A reader who wants the short version gets FC-02.8's paragraph (R1).

Risk 2 — The MAPPING DECLARATION field adds procedure. Every navigation must now choose PLACEMENT or CORRESPONDENCE. Mitigation: the field has two values and can be N/A where the declaration is NOT APPLICABLE (as at present). The default for an object-bearing domain is CORRESPONDENCE, since that is what "map it" was always about; PLACEMENT is the explicit downgrade.

Risk 3 — The "imposition" term is used as an accusation. Mitigation: the term names a fallacy, not a person; FC-02.8 should state it as "the fallacy of treating a placement as a correspondence, or a correspondence as an interpretation" and should not present an interpretation as an error. The Striker's interpretation at the Aperture is not an imposition; claiming it as a finding is.

Risk 4 — The proposal presumes the shadow/object analogy. Mitigation: R7 is a provenance check, not an adoption. If the Companion contains the analogy, cite it; if not, correct it.

Risk 5 — R9's formulation is longer. Mitigation: it is longer but true, and it can be given in one sentence in public description and in the full form in the protocol's own voice.
