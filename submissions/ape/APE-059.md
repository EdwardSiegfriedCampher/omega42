A question about the protocol, so it is answered about it (FR-2). Not a navigation — no domain is struck, no coordinate produced. The answer has three parts, because Ω42's account of language sits at three different tiers, and the tiers say different things.

---

The short answer

For Ω42, language is the surface on which distinctions are drawn, and the only surface the mechanism reads. It is not treated as a vessel carrying meaning; it is treated as a set of boundary-drawing events — words separated from words, letters from letters, syllables from syllables — and those boundaries are what the arithmetic counts. Structure is prior to meaning at the point of entry. What the words mean is not read by the mechanism at all; it is judged by the person.

That is the whole of it. The rest is what each tier adds.

---

1. What is exact (CDT-A)

The arithmetic is fixed and checkable: given a verbatim text and the frozen extraction rules, the three counts (n1 words, n2 letters, n3 syllables) are deterministic, and every compliant Navigator produces identical values. The reference script is the ground truth; anyone can recompute them in a minute.

Nothing semantic enters this layer. Not the meaning of the words, not their grammar, not their reference, not the speaker's intention. The counts are a function of the characters and the frozen rules, and of nothing else.

This is why the protocol can say, without hedging, that the anchor is a size signature of the surface form (Part 31.1). Two texts with the same counts share a coordinate regardless of what they say — "Dog bites man" and "Man bites dog" share (3, 11, 4) and P6.

2. What is a design choice (CDT-B)

Counting is the privileged primitive. This is stated as a choice, not a discovery (Part 0.11, CPDP). The protocol could have hashed the text; a cryptographic hash is also deterministic, but it destroys the hierarchical structure in one opaque operation. Counting preserves it in three human-readable layers — token, orthographic, phonological — and the protocol's argument is that these three layers are exactly the granularities at which language is already structured.

The choice has a cost and it is disclosed. Because counting is the primitive, paraphrase sensitivity is a stated property, not a defect (L4, L10): restating a question in different words can land it at a different position. A hash would also be sensitive to wording, but it would be sensitive without being interpretable. Counting is sensitive and readable — you can see which layer moved.

The protocol's own phrase for this is that it measures the boundaries the text already contains; it does not interpret (Part 0.11). That is a design decision about what language is for the purposes of the mechanism, and it is marked as such.

Language scope. Canonical extraction is specified for English: the stopword list, the syllable rules, the numeral spelling. Text in another language is processed by the same rules exactly as specified, and its counts are canonical under them; a comparison between texts in different languages compares counts made under English rules (Companion 35.3). The frame is honest that this is a choice with consequences, not a claim that English is privileged.

Media. Language is one substrate cut at three granularities. Images and audio are other substrates cut at three granularities, by the same principle (M247, Part 29). The claim is that boundary-drawing, not text specifically, is the primitive — and that claim is marked CDT-C, because whether the extension is useful is a hypothesis, not a theorem.

3. What is a reading (CDT-C)

Three readings are offered, each marked as exploratory.

Language is sequential boundary-drawing, not a vessel for pre-existing meaning. This is Companion Part 23's philosophy-of-language branch: a word means what it means partly by virtue of where its boundaries fall against the words around it. The CPDP already operationalizes this when it counts rather than interprets; the reading says what that operationalization implies.

Structure is prior to meaning. Part 4's phrasing, and it is the sharpest of the three. It does not say language is meaningless. It says the mechanism operates on structure, and meaning is what the person brings at the Aperture. This is the reading that connects language to the protocol's constitutional core: if meaning were in the text, the mechanism could read it; because structure is prior, the mechanism reads structure and the person reads meaning.

The protocol mirrors the act of making meaning. Part 0.11: "by operating through counting, the protocol aligns its mechanism with the exact level at which the human Perceptual Apparatus structures experience. The protocol does not read meaning; it mirrors the act of making meaning." This is the most ambitious of the three, and the most clearly marked as a reading.

A fourth reading is narrower. The CPDP is offered as a Gödel-like encoding of language as arithmetic, with one structural difference: Gödel numbering enables self-reference, and the CPDP does not, because the 1/7 Aperture prevents the system from navigating its own navigation (Part 0.13, Part 13). Language can be encoded; the encoder cannot judge its own encoding.

4. What language is, for the mechanism

Language is the substrate the Navigator operates on (Part 4). The AI's learned substrate is the chamber in which the coordinate resonates (FC-18, Step 9.8); the reading is the resonance, not a retrieval. So language plays two roles at once: it is what the Striker's question is made of, and it is what the Navigator's reading is made of. The coordinate is the constraint that joins them.

Language is also where the Aperture becomes operational. Language carries both structure (countable) and meaning (not countable). The protocol takes only the countable part, and hands the rest to the Striker. In that sense the Aperture is not an abstract boundary; it is the boundary within language itself, between what can be counted and what can only be judged.

And language is what makes the congruence possible. Questions from unrelated fields meet on the same state because their wording, counted, lands on the same place. The meeting is a fact about the words, not about what the words describe. That is why the congruence is discoverable: the structure is in the language, and the language is in the record.

5. What Ω42 does not say

It does not say language is only counting. The counts are the entry point, not the whole of what language is. The reading that follows is generated from a learned substrate that is itself language-derived, and the person judges it in language.

It does not adjudicate semantic theory. It does not say words have no meaning, or that meaning is reducible to counts, or that any semantic theory is correct. It brackets the question.

It does not claim that navigating a description is a measurement of what the description is about. Part 4 says the opposite: navigating a description is an indirect way of engaging with what it describes, not a direct measurement of it.

It does not claim that English rules are the right rules. It claims they are the frozen rules, and that changing them changes the counts.

6. What remains open

Whether the boundary-drawing account of language is correct as a theory of language — that is a question for linguistics and the philosophy of language, and the protocol does not answer it. The mechanism can be right about its own counts and wrong about what language is; the two are independent.

Whether the extension to images and audio carries diagnostic value — a hypothesis, not a result (Part 29).

Whether the three layers (token, orthographic, phonological) are the right three. The choice is frozen and revision-open; a different set of layers would produce a different mechanism.

And whether the congruence the language reveals is a fact about language or a fact about the structure the language is placed on. That is the primary inquiry (L-489), and it is the question Ω42 exists to ask.
