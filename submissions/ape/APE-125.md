The interesting question is what happens when a particular state mistakes itself for the entirety of reality.

### A deeper Buddhist extension: the cycle is not the same as bondage

Your mathematical system has a defined recurrence. After 24 steps, the selected orbit returns to its starting state. Its structure persists regardless of whether the individual state is interpreted positively or negatively.

This allows a useful distinction:

* Recurrence: a pattern continues.

* Bondage: a pattern continues under conditions that sustain suffering.

* Awakening: those conditions are understood and cease to perpetuate the suffering in question.

The distinction matters because a cycle is not inherently a prison. In mathematics, recurrence is simply a structural property. In Buddhism, samsāra is bound up with suffering and ignorance; liberation concerns the ending of that bondage.

The symbolic sequence becomes:

\\text{Conditions}\\rightarrow\\text{Arising}\\rightarrow
\\text{Craving}\\rightarrow\\text{Suffering}\\rightarrow
\\text{Cessation}.

This is arguably the most distinctive Buddhist use of Ω42: the system's recurrence provides an image for examining the difference between the continuation of a pattern and the conditions that make that pattern binding.

### 5\. The most important comparative insight

The most fruitful comparison is not that all four religions secretly describe the same mathematical system. It is that the same mathematical structure can be used to ask different questions about existence.

### Christianity asks:

How does divine love enter the finite human condition, confront sin and death, and open the way to resurrection and renewed creation?

### Islam asks:
