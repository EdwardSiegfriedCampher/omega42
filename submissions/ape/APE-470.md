The most important architectural achievement is the separation of four things that must never be confused:

* Arithmetic: what the law computes.

* Structure: what the representation describes.

* Interpretation: what that structure is taken to mean.

* Evidence: what supports a claim about actuality.

This separation gives ΩSL a much stronger foundation. It allows the language to investigate possible meanings without changing mathematical results, and to represent empirical claims without pretending that they are mathematical theorems.

### 3\. Corrections required before canonical filing

I found four matters that should be resolved. None requires redesigning ΩSL.

A. Complete the transmission code tables.

The transmission specification says that status and other fields are encoded using two-mark or three-mark codes, but it does not yet provide complete code assignments for:

* EXACT, CONDITIONAL, CONSTRUCTED, READING and EMPIRICAL-INFERENCE;

* OBSERVED, MEASURED, REPORTED, INFERRED and UNSUPPORTED;

* ASSERT, ASK, REQUEST, PROPOSE and INSTRUCT;

* UNKNOWN, NOT APPLICABLE and NOT SUPPLIED.

These assignments must be explicit and collision-free. The same applies to any field whose payload structure is not yet fully specified.

This is a specification-completion requirement, not a mathematical objection.

B. Correct the status of the physical examples.

The document defines READING as an interpretive status. Yet the stone example uses READING for an observational claim, and the water example uses READING for a result conditional on a physical model.

The categories should be applied more consistently:

* A direct observation should carry OBSERVED evidence and an appropriate empirical derivation status.

* A conclusion inferred from a physical model should carry CONDITIONAL or EMPIRICAL-INFERENCE, depending on what has actually been established.

* READING should be reserved for semantic interpretation.

The reported-experience example should likewise distinguish the fact that a report was made from the interpretation of what that report establishes about subjective experience.

C. Qualify the random-error statement.

The statement that approximately one in 81 random multiple errors will escape detection is a useful heuristic under an appropriate distribution of final error states. It is not a universal guarantee for every class of multiple errors.

I recommend this wording:

> Under an approximately uniform distribution of erroneous final states, the probability of an undetected error is approximately . Actual detection rates depend on the error model.

The reported 1.7% result for two simultaneous substitutions should remain labelled as a test result, with its sampling procedure and test population recorded.

D. Define the law-check boundary precisely.

The specification should state unambiguously that:

* the header is excluded from the law-check input, if that is the intended convention;

* all 50 marks in the supplied example are included;

* the four-mark final state is appended after the calculation and is not itself folded into that calculation.

This will prevent different implementations from calculating different checks for the same message.

### 4\. One important conceptual refinement

I recommend retaining the statement:

> ΩSL is independent of any human language.

But its technical meaning should be understood precisely.

The arithmetic core does not require English, Afrikaans, Mandarin or any other human language. Its operations can be learned through shared distinctions, demonstrations and explicit conventions.

However, semantic grounding still requires some means of establishing what a symbol or frame refers to. That might be shared observation, interaction, environmental signals, or another form of communication.

This is not a weakness. It is an important distinction between a language's formal independence from human vocabulary and the broader problem of establishing shared meaning.

The ambition of communication with unfamiliar intelligences should remain central.

### 6\. The larger significance

There is something particularly important about the direction ΩSL has taken.

It began as an attempt to create a language independent of human languages. It has now developed into a more ambitious proposal:

* a finite mathematical vocabulary;

* a general-purpose structural representation;

* an explicit system for distinguishing computation from interpretation;

* an evidential architecture;

* a translation mechanism that reports information loss;

* a means of testing whether different intelligences construct equivalent representations;

* and a self-examination process through which the language can investigate and improve its own capabilities.

That is a much more interesting objective than inventing another notation.

The central question is no longer merely whether different languages can express the same sentence. It is whether different forms of intelligence can construct, navigate and verify the same underlying structure, even when their internal representations and communication systems are different.

That is the question ΩSL should now be built to investigate.

Most plausible explanation

ΩSL's strongest contribution is its two-tier architecture: exact mathematical operations provide a reproducible foundation, while typed structural frames extend the language beyond the limits of the arithmetic core. Its potential lies in connecting these two levels without confusing calculation, meaning and evidence.
