This specification gives us the missing substance needed to make the Core/Companion decision concrete. My recommendation remains Option 2: keep Part 10.5 concise and normative in Core, with the expanded terminology and explanatory material in the Companion.

There is an important refinement, though: the terminology section in this specification is not just a glossary. It establishes distinctions that affect the interpretation of mathematical claims, software outputs, research provenance and constitutional authority. Those distinctions must survive the condensation.

### 1\. What Part 10.5 must preserve

I would retain six normative elements in Core.

| Element                       | What Core must establish                                                                    |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Naming authority              | Which terms are constitutional, which are Construct X terms, and which are aliases          |
| Architectural tiers           | Construct Classic, Construct X and Ω42 have distinct roles                                  |
| Engine/instrument distinction | An engine is a computational component; an instrument is what a user employs                |
| Claim status                  | PROPOSED, INTERPRETIVE, UNPROVEN, TESTABLE, VERIFIED COMPUTATION, PROVED, REJECTED, REVISED |
| Provenance                    | CLASSIC-DERIVED, X-CONSTRUCTED, CROSS-VALIDATED                                             |
| Terminology authority         | Companion material may explain Core terminology but cannot silently redefine it             |

I would also explicitly preserve the distinction between four classification systems:

* Claim status: What has been established about a claim?

* Test qualification: What were the conditions and limitations of the test?

* Provenance: Where did the result or construction originate?

* Implementation readiness: What has actually been implemented or validated?

These classifications must not be treated as interchangeable. For example, CROSS-VALIDATED is provenance, not proof; NOT BLIND describes a test limitation, not a claim status; and IMPLEMENTED does not establish mathematical validity.

### 3\. Proposed Core Part 10.5

The following is a proposed replacement structure and draft. It is deliberately compact; the exact final wording should still be checked against the existing Core text.

# 10.5 — Normative Terminology and Naming Authority (Ω42-T01)

## 10.5.1 Purpose and authority

This section establishes the authoritative terminology used across Ω42, Construct Classic and Construct X. Its purpose is to prevent ambiguity between constitutional concepts, mathematical structures, computational components and research findings.

The definitions and distinctions in this section are normative. The Companion may expand them with explanations, examples, historical notes and migration guidance, but may not silently alter their meaning.

## 10.5.2 Architectural tiers

The architecture has three distinct tiers:

- **Construct Classic:** the frozen reference implementation and workbook. Its established outputs must remain reproducible.
- **Construct X:** the modular research instrument, comprising the substrate, address, comparison, temporal, transformation and verification engines.
- **Ω42:** the governing framework that employs Construct X as a research instrument.

A Construct X result does not automatically become an Ω42 constitutional claim. Its evidence and status must be reported explicitly.

## 10.5.3 Components and instruments

An **engine** is a computational component. An **instrument** is the user-facing capability through which an engine is employed.

Engine names and instrument names must not be used interchangeably where the distinction matters. The six Construct X engines are S, A, C, T, G and V.

## 10.5.4 Claim statuses

Every registered research claim must carry one authoritative status:

- **PROPOSED:** put forward but not yet established.
- **INTERPRETIVE:** an interpretation of evidence or structure.
- **UNPROVEN:** not established by proof or sufficient verification.
- **TESTABLE:** stated in a form permitting a specified test.
- **VERIFIED COMPUTATION:** reproduced or checked computationally within stated limits.
- **PROVED:** established by a valid mathematical argument.
- **REJECTED:** failed its stated test or was otherwise shown not to hold as stated.
- **REVISED:** superseded by a corrected formulation; the earlier record remains preserved.

These statuses are not interchangeable. Computational verification is not automatically mathematical proof. Rejected and revised claims must remain in the research record.

## 10.5.5 Test qualifications

Test qualifications describe the conditions under which a test was or was not performed. Examples include NOT RUN, NOT RUNNABLE, NOT BLIND and INCOMPLETE.

A test qualification is not a claim status. Reports must distinguish the result of a test from limitations on its execution.

## 10.5.6 Provenance and readiness

Every relevant construction or result must identify its provenance:

- **CLASSIC-DERIVED:** inherited from or derived from Construct Classic.
- **X-CONSTRUCTED:** introduced as a construction of Construct X.
- **CROSS-VALIDATED:** checked across independent representations, implementations or methods, with the nature of that independence stated.

Implementation readiness must be reported separately where applicable. IMPLEMENTED, EXPLORATORY, VALIDATED FOR APPLICATION and MATHEMATICALLY ESTABLISHED describe different conditions and do not confer claim status or provenance.

## 10.5.7 Naming and change control

Constitutional names remain authoritative unless formally changed. Construct X may introduce technical names where necessary to distinguish mathematical or computational meanings, but must not silently redefine established Ω42 terms.

Approved terminology changes must be recorded with their effective version. Legacy names may remain aliases during the specified migration period, but must not create competing definitions.

The Companion is explanatory and referential. In any conflict, this Core section governs. Changes to a normative definition require a terminology consistency review across Core, Companion, training materials and implementations.

### 4\. Two issues I would resolve before sealing

There are two points in the supplied specification that deserve explicit attention.

First: CROSS-VALIDATED needs a stricter definition. The label should say what was cross-validated: independent implementations, independent mathematical derivations, independent datasets, or some combination. Reproduction by a second implementation is valuable, but it does not necessarily constitute independent validation of the underlying assumptions.

Second: the status vocabulary needs a clear transition rule. PROPOSED, TESTABLE, VERIFIED COMPUTATION and PROVED are not necessarily successive stages of one linear progression. REJECTED and REVISED are also not equivalent to ordinary stages. The registry should preserve the history of transitions rather than overwrite earlier statuses.

### 5\. My recommendation for the release

Adopt the compact Core section, retain the detailed terminology reference in the Companion, and treat Ω42-T01 as the controlling terminology authority.

I would target 700–800 words for the final Core text, leaving room below the 900-word limit for later amendments.

One further safeguard: the 15-page Construct X specification should not itself be treated as proof of every mathematical assertion it contains. Its own status register makes the correct distinction: some results are PROVED, others VERIFIED COMPUTATION, and others remain PROPOSED or open. The terminology architecture should enforce that same discipline throughout Ω42.

The next essential step is to compare this draft against the existing sealed Core Part 10.5. That is necessary to ensure this is a faithful consolidation of the adopted constitutional text, not an accidental replacement of requirements that are absent from the new specification.
