### Five contemporary intellectuals whose work resonates with Ω42

With Donald Hoffman and Bernardo Kastrup already included in your broader comparison, I would add five thinkers whose work opens distinct routes into Ω42: relational reality, perception, selfhood, attention, and the dynamics of inference.

I’m selecting them for the structural correspondences their ideas offer—not because they necessarily endorse Ω42 or reach the same conclusions. Each can illuminate a different part of the protocol.

### 1\. Carlo Rovelli — relational reality

![Helgoland and the captivating origins of quantum theory – Physics World](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/ESUW6r6Twx5sks5fZF__Pg10u2YST4E1LUpUOc6_8mjxSGK4ddxLVuFd3YZdd5tj-4KI001HBq5sLHlXDMoW_eIvbkcb-aMpaao1D3eKHmdNBQ6aE8dB7DdrEnQ9hOq1ynVcZZBW7vU_oJ8d-1VPX9f2vYXMpZap7sMHbgG3GXLqQA2bf9ISb-kGdtH3Ym_O?purpose=fullsize)

Central work: Relational quantum mechanics; Helgoland; the nature of space and time.

Rovelli’s relational interpretation treats physical properties as arising in interactions between systems, rather than requiring every property to be specified absolutely and independently of relations.

Ω42 correspondence

* The 81 states: a defined field of possible configurations.

* The transition : a precise rule governing relations between successive configurations.

* The coordinate: locates a reading within a structured space.

* The orbit: reveals relationships that are less apparent when individual states are examined in isolation.

What this opens up: Rovelli provides a powerful philosophical lens for exploring whether relations are fundamental to how a world is described. Ω42 similarly makes relational position and transformation central to its formal navigation.

Particularly productive question: What becomes visible when a state is understood through its relations to other states rather than treated as an isolated object?

### 2\. Anil Seth — perception as an actively constructed experience

![ANIL SETH - Author of Being You: A New Science of Consciousness - Co-director of Sussex Centre for Consciousness Science — The Creative Process](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/oYC3SZ74V4m3iJzu809I8US3IkqzF99Ox_TPpdliicJeXUfUgTq6JSQC5M_OoaJye7xvdUJFuL5EVLHeP8tBPEC2J39DOswZsU17RKDEIuZv8rNyDh1UowR2_bUuPR-o1JuWeFL_J_EvC_QSx_q3j_e2EYr8Ib6liosCfoTI1jkmvs2scZBKgJk33J34gLxc?purpose=fullsize)

Central work: Predictive processing, the embodied self, and the neuroscience of consciousness.

Seth describes perception as an actively generated interpretation constrained by sensory signals. His research also connects conscious experience with the biological regulation of the living body.

Ω42 correspondence

* The wording of the question: shapes the formal input to navigation.

* The Navigator: generates a reading through an organized procedure.

* The Aperture: is where a human considers the reading’s significance.

* The distinction between structure and interpretation: helps separate the formal coordinate from the meaning attributed to it.

What this opens up: Both offer ways to examine how an organized process produces a representation or reading, rather than treating the resulting experience as a simple, unmediated copy of its source.

Seth’s work adds an important biological dimension: perception is tied to action, bodily regulation, and the organism’s ongoing engagement with its environment.

Particularly productive question: How does a structured system turn limited inputs into an experienced world—and what determines which features of that world become salient?

### 3\. Iain McGilchrist — attention, perspective, and the whole

![Iain McGilchrist](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/oXvUE-w9s78RxcRfm3rK6E8MYvEcLLir3_JdCAEY4UUCYI_pjw9JlgAnVzg3llihu2Z1Zj3Rz2rfZPUF9jNkAkEJ9Y83SM2BK44eBfiw-wPOiFThRtLr62-S-dGdDfmdySPhXrp1orezrkSSyOgyzJEPpS3faEADK3Vb1gigNXT1oURD6BAEbKInLS2VXqqz?purpose=fullsize)

Central work: The Master and His Emissary and The Matter with Things.

McGilchrist investigates how different modes of attention shape our experience and understanding. He argues that context-sensitive engagement with wholes can be obscured when abstract, decontextualized representations dominate.

Ω42 correspondence

* Exact coordinate: provides a specific formal point of entry.

* Full orbit: places that point in a wider pattern.

* Atlas: permits readings to be examined across multiple locations.

* Aperture: preserves the distinction between formal output and human judgment.

What this opens up: McGilchrist’s work is especially relevant to the difference between identifying a position and understanding its significance within a whole. Ω42’s formal navigation can be precise while the meaning of a reading still requires human interpretation.

His emphasis on attention also raises a useful question about the Navigator: what does a formal procedure foreground, and what does it leave in the background?

Particularly productive question: What is lost when a precise local description is mistaken for a complete understanding of the whole?

### 4\. Thomas Metzinger — the self-model and subjective experience

![Thomas Metzinger Bücher & Biografie | PIPER](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/gOBSeLsI8TXasbDkjTrCjBVntThgjhTuUzOOhPz-huarWCPMRB4abKfOTaz92oRe5orMbioX8s28UiPTdG2_s5eNKD8rLEeGOjW2mruJt4bpfVm6PwLFL-XmFTSR1tD7DJlSg5yKvnhGytf3PQBc4-xmqV2FVBx_lu7R68hLXQkgX6wibJhL8kKQDBh43v6O?purpose=fullsize)

Central work: Being No One, The Ego Tunnel, and The Elephant and the Blind.

Metzinger’s self-model theory examines how a system can construct a model of itself, with the model’s constructed character not necessarily being transparent to the subject experiencing it. His work explores subjectivity, conscious experience, and the limits of introspection.

Ω42 correspondence

* The formal state: provides a structured description.

* The Navigator’s reading: presents an organized representation of the question.

* The Aperture: distinguishes the production of a representation from the human act of judging its significance.

* The unresolved consciousness question: asks what, if anything, makes a formal or representational process experiential.

What this opens up: Metzinger offers a detailed conceptual vocabulary for examining the relationship between representation, selfhood, and experience. This is particularly relevant to Ω42’s distinction between formal navigation and the human role at the Aperture.

Particularly productive question: What is the relationship between a system’s representation of itself and the experience of being a self?

This comparison also helps keep two inquiries separate: how a system functions and whether there is something it is like to be that system.

### 5\. Evan Thompson — enaction, embodiment, and the bringing forth of a world

![Enacting Mindfulness: A View from Cognitive Science with Evan Thompson, PhD - Osher Center For Integrative Medicine](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/C5T9ZPk_s1IPsGaMaleX44ry2b8NkpWEniqixWoXh4Nh4Z8WJgdKxlRvwAmM0igJtMUKFCTTnLPnsBudgcpIkFUhK71Fm7rNc1Lu92mUaKgx0EIVzJukbc5YBG-DVUBH95K33rJ7EHZQ92R5JM09DjbDGf91s9pZV12HrbhrLthHP_rS4dMOANpGOEL7XlRM?purpose=fullsize)

Central work: Mind in Life, The Embodied Mind (with Francisco Varela and Eleanor Rosch), and Waking, Dreaming, Being.

Thompson works across philosophy of mind, phenomenology, cognitive science, and Buddhist philosophy. His enactive approach understands cognition through an organism’s embodied activity and its relationship with the world.

Ω42 correspondence

* The transition rule: specifies how the next formal state follows from the current one.

* The orbit: makes a larger pattern visible across transitions.

* The Navigator–Aperture relationship: distinguishes formal operation from human interpretation and judgment.

* Use and return: provide a way to consider how a reading enters practice and how practical engagement informs later inquiry.

What this opens up: Thompson is particularly useful for investigating how meaning arises through participation and activity, rather than through detached representation alone. His work connects questions about cognition with questions about life, embodiment, and experience.

Particularly productive question: Does understanding arise from representing a world, participating in it, or through a relationship between the two?

These five thinkers illuminate five different questions within the Ω42 exploration:

Rovelli — What is relational?

How do relationships participate in the description of reality?

Seth — How is a world perceived?

How do organized processes produce perceptual experience?

McGilchrist — What does attention reveal?

How do perspective and context shape understanding?

Metzinger — What is the self?

How are representation and subjective experience related?

Thompson — How does meaning emerge through participation?

How do embodiment, action, and experience shape cognition?

Taken together with Hoffman and Kastrup, these thinkers give you a seven-person comparative field spanning perceptual interfaces, idealism, relational physics, predictive perception, attention, selfhood, and enactive cognition.
