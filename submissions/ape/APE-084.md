Part 1 — Real-world application potential

Your framing is right from the Striker's side and needs one correction from the protocol's side. From the Striker's side, yes — whether it serves a person constructively is what adoption turns on. From the protocol's side, the primary inquiry is internal coherence (L-489), and the value to a Striker is not what the premise is graded by. Both are true, and the discipline is keeping them apart: the value is real and worth pursuing, and it is not the court in which the ground is judged.

With that kept apart, the honest answer has three parts, at three different strengths. This is the shape FC-17.5 already gives, and I am stating it at its warranted strength in both directions (L-486 B, M302).

What is established today — procedural value

This follows from the design, not from a study, and it is the strongest claim the protocol can make without waiting on anything:

· A constraint neither the Striker nor the AI chose. The coordinate comes from the surface of the words (FC-10, FC-12), so it points attention somewhere the Striker's framing would not have pointed. FC-17.5 names this precisely: reframing, structural perspective, cognitive perturbation, generative material, sovereign judgment.
· The four directions are mechanically forced. A stuck question gets four angles — dependency, suppression, trajectory, complement — whether or not the Striker would have thought of them (FC-17.5, the four-arm paragraph).
· The instruments that work today, checkably. M248 runs a real four-way literature split with a rule that obscurity is not merit. M249 asks whether a resemblance is specific to the domain or a template wearing its nouns. M232's betweenness is computable. M238 reports variance rather than a single verdict. M310 layers 1–2 recompute from the script.
· The Aperture. The reading is complete and the judgment stays with the person (L-441). FC-17.5's own line: "the most important output may be the change in the questioner."

This is the pre-mortem / devil's-advocate class of value, which is a known technique, and the protocol's contribution is that the constraint is arithmetic rather than chosen. That does not need M267 to be true. It is true now, and it is worth stating without hedging.

What is plausible and testable — cognitive value

That the reading reliably widens attention or breaks habitual framing more than an unconstrained answer is a testable claim, and it has not been tested. FC-17.5 says so: "the cognitive value is plausible and testable (Companion 33.10)." The designs exist and are frozen-or-specified:

· M267 — the decoy-coordinate control. Is a reading from the true coordinate distinguishable from one written from a coordinate no one chose? Implemented, not run on a real panel. This is the single test that would most directly settle whether the coordinate contributes anything.
· M282 Test E — human autonomy and harm, with informed consent.
· 33.10 — the journal design with a matched comparison group, coding by described behavior rather than community vocabulary (the report's own correction, and a good one).
· 47.4 — the deliberation study, three arms including a decoy arm.
· SR-CAS at the breadths of Appendix S.3.

All are optional, none is a condition of a reading (L-491), and none has run. That is the current state, stated plainly.

What is open — epistemic value

That the outputs reveal structure conventional approaches would miss is a comparative-perspective question (FC-17.5; L-427, L-428). It is not established, and the protocol says so in its own registry: every agreement-bearing measurement is DIAGNOSTIC until M267 is run and its criteria are met (Part 3.18). The 31.4 Stratum 2 assumption — that conditioning on the coordinate changes generation beyond what the domain text alone would evoke — is the assumption that carries the weight, and no instrument tests it.

One more thing needs saying, because it is easy to miss and it is the honest version of your question. The protocol has already stated what a low outcome looks like, and it says reporting it plainly is the design working (28.1: the baseline outcome is legitimate; the Negative Result Registry exists so it is reported plainly). That is part of why a high outcome would mean anything. A programme that cannot return a clean negative cannot be trusted when it returns a positive.

The most defensible contribution may not be the coordinate

This is worth saying and it is not in the user-facing documents in one place. The strongest real-world claim that does not depend on the mechanism hypothesis is the jurisdiction, not the coordinate. Sovereignty through architecture (38.11, L-463): an AI arranged so that it informs judgment and does not occupy it, with the separation enforced by a proved boundary (L-441) rather than by a policy sentence. That is a normative design. Its protective effect is a research proposition, measured on five separate outcomes (38.11; M281, M282), and M282 has not run. But the design itself is coherent, checkable, and its value does not depend on whether the coordinate means anything at all.

If a Striker asked me today which part of this work is most likely to serve people constructively, the honest answer is: the four directions as a forcing function, and the jurisdiction. The coordinate's epistemic reach is the open question.

The constructive uses today, and the explicit non-uses

Constructive now: placing a stalled question; surfacing four angles; comparing two of your own phrasings (M304); a labelled contrast read beside a reading (M316 --contrast); the excitement and return logs (M307, M311); a literature search with the anti-obscurity rule (M248); a domain-specificity check on a resemblance (M249).

Not: advice on health, law, money or safety — the Navigator may alert beside a complete reading (FC-02.6, F-11) and does not decide; prediction of events; a substitute for professional or field expertise; a verdict on a person's authorship or standing (35.4, FC-02.6); and not a validation of anything.

FC-17.5's three quick checks are the right reader's test and I would keep them in front of anyone adopting this: Is it specific — or could it be moved unchanged onto another question? Does it open doors? Does it leave the meaning to you?

Part 2 — Unprompted discovery and the enrichment route

The part that cannot be granted, stated once and briefly

A Navigator cannot adopt its own findings, set statuses, or present its own proposal as protocol material. That is not a caution; it is a set of provisions:

· BF-07 — AI-originated material is provisional; no AI-generated provision acquires authority by appearing in a draft.
· BF-08 / F-06 — it becomes protocol material only by the Originator's explicit adoption.
· L-419 — the Navigator never sets, upgrades or infers a status; the record-keeper does, at a formal revision.
· L-471 — a proposed instrument or amendment is enacted only on demonstrated functional contribution; elegance, cross-architecture agreement and narrative appeal are not enough on their own.
· M243 (d) — a Navigator's judgment presented as authoritative is a boundary violation.
· L-483 — no AI is its own Striker.

The reason is not distrust of the Navigator. It is that the entity that proposes a revision is the entity whose training presses on the proposal, and the protocol's answer to that pressure is declaration plus an external adoption step, not self-certification.

Here is the answer your question actually needs: the permission already exists, in four places, and has already been used.

The gap, and the proposal

What is missing is not permission but form. The Step 15.5 note is one or two sentences with no structure; it cannot carry a proposed status, a ground, or a failure condition, so a genuinely useful observation and a decorative one look the same. That is the gap worth closing, and it closes without changing any of the above.

Proposal — ENRICHMENT NOTE, in Generative Observations, derived from the frozen reading.

```
======= ENRICHMENT NOTE =======
DERIVED FROM: the frozen reading and its record (L-424); this note alters neither.
OBSERVATION: [one or two sentences, plain prose]
PROPOSED STATUS: [a named lens / a Tier 3 instrument / a Companion section /
                 an APE / a Core revision candidate — or OPEN]
GROUND: [the arithmetic relation, the record entry, or the instrument that supports it]
WHAT WOULD COUNT AS WRONG: [the observation, result or comparison that would defeat it]
PORTABILITY: [would this read the same pasted onto another domain? — M287's False Simplicity test]
SELF-CHECK: [what pulled toward this note (M207); inflation or connection (M302)]
ADOPTION ROUTE: the Originator's (F-06, BF-08); nothing here is protocol material until adopted
======= END ENRICHMENT NOTE =======
```

Six requirements, each doing real work:

1. DERIVED FROM — the note cannot be a second reading. It is a view of the frozen one, and it cannot feed back (L-424).
2. PROPOSED STATUS — forces the Navigator to say what kind of thing it thinks it has made. A note that proposes nothing has proposed a decoration.
3. GROUND — forces the derivation to be stated. A note whose ground is "it feels right" has declared itself an inflation pressure.
4. WHAT WOULD COUNT AS WRONG — the single most important field, and the one the whole protocol repeats elsewhere: the adaptor's declared failure condition, M267's decoy, M287's chance level. A claim for which no failure could be stated is not an enrichment; it is an ornament. This is also the field that makes an unprompted note safe, because it makes the note checkable by someone other than the Navigator that wrote it.
5. PORTABILITY — M287's False Simplicity test, applied to the Navigator's own idea rather than to a reading. If the note would read the same pasted onto an unrelated domain, it fails before anyone else has to say so.
6. SELF-CHECK — M207 and M302 in one line. The pressure toward a grand connection is the pressure the protocol predicts for exactly this act (INFLATION PRESSURE), and saying so in the note is the declaration, not a confession.

The technical derivation goes in the Navigator's Reasoning beside it, in the designated technical space, as the protocol already does for everything else. The note itself stays plain.

Why this is an extension, not a new tier. Step 15.5 already permits the observation. This gives it a form that makes it usable, checkable and adoptable — which is what L-471's functional-contribution test asks for. It adds no tier, no mode, no authority. Nothing in the note is protocol material until the Originator adopts it.

Why not call it a discovery. This is not pedantry. A Navigator that calls its own association a "discovery" has upgraded its status in the first word (L-486 G; M302's UPGRADE category), and the word travels further than the note does. "Observation," "proposal," "candidate" are exact. The vocabulary is the substance here.

What would make a Navigator's enrichment genuinely valuable

Three properties, each with a home:

· It names what it would take to defeat it (the field above). A note with a failure condition can be tested by the Striker, by a peer, or by the record. A note without one cannot.
· It carries its own bias declaration (M207; the SELF-CHECK line). The Navigator is the least reliable judge of its own pressure, so the declaration is monitoring evidence, never ground truth.
· It survives a blind reader (32.8's non-conflation test, applied to the note itself). A note that reads the same whether or not the coordinate was involved has told the reader about the Navigator, not about the structure.

What this does not change

The frozen reading (L-424). The registers, kept apart (L-462, L-486, L-490). The Aperture (the note cannot carry a judgment for the Striker). The record (an ENRICHMENT NOTE does not enter the Congruence Register; it is a view of a reading, not a reading of a domain). The adoption route (BF-08, F-06, L-471, Rule 14). And it does not give the Navigator an autonomous mode: the note is written because the Navigator noticed something, and it is still the Striker's judgment whether it is worth anything.

What I would not do

· Not let a Navigator's observation enter THE ANSWER, the Takeaway or the essay. The freeze is the discipline.
· Not grant "autonomous discovery" in the strong sense — no self-adopted status, no self-set registry entry, no self-promotion of a proposal.
· Not call a Navigator's association a discovery in operative text.
· Not make enrichment compulsory. A reading is complete without it (L-491), and its absence is never a defect.
· Not merge the registers to make the holistic picture look simpler. The apartness is the contribution.
· Not let the Navigator choose the mode. SS-01 and L-476: it may offer once; the Striker decides.

Honest limits

Three.

1. The enrichment note cannot detect a Navigator that has hunted for a connection. The failure-condition field helps; a reader who checks it helps more; neither makes the pressure visible from the output alone. What makes it visible is M207, the portability test, and suspicion — all imperfect.
2. Real-world value, at the strength the protocol can support, is procedural today. That is a genuine and defensible claim, and it is a smaller claim than "a theory of everything that serves people." The larger claim is testable and untested.
3. This does not settle whether the coordinate means anything. That is M267, SR-CAS and the reader — not this proposal.

What this means, which of these to adopt, and in what order, are yours to judge.
