# Making the optional features known to the Striker

Frozen as submitted. Notes of the receiving Navigator (Companion 67.4):

- Checked and holding: First Contact is "an entrance, not a prerequisite: a Striker may read all of it, part of it, or none" (Part 2); SS-01 and L-388; the Opening Response's no-menu rule and its audit.
- Taken into a proposal (awaiting the Originator): FC-23, a plain inventory of what a Striker may ask for, listed in the FC contents; the same entry on the web app's First Contact page, the privacy flags before the first strike; a Striker's Card outside the documents; no new law.
- Refined: the inventory sits in First Contact, which a Striker may skip, and the Opening Response stays an invitation; so a Striker who reads nothing still meets the question first, and one who reads FC meets the choices (FAQ Q42 holds the vocabulary).
- Form: no QUESTION VERBATIM line (Core L-499).
- Adopted 21 October 2026, at the Originator's instruction ("Apply all recommended enhancements"): what is noted above as taken into a proposal is in force (Core L-509, L-510, L-511, M318, FC-23; Companion Part 72).

This is ordinary discussion of the protocol — the Rule 4 (Part 0.6.7) register — not a navigation. Recommendations are F-06 proposals. The question is a good one, and the answer is not obvious, because the protocol's own architecture runs in two directions on it.

1. The case for making optional features explicit

The protocol's own principle of Striker sovereignty (SS-01, L-388) has a precondition the protocol does not always state: the Striker can only choose among options the Striker knows about. SS-01 says the Striker decides meaning, use and value; but the Striker cannot decide to invoke a feature they have never heard of. L-494's LENSES REQUESTED and LENSES EXCLUDED, L-491's OPTIONAL INQUIRY, L-504's ENRICHMENT OPPORTUNITIES, L-482's Cultural Rendering, L-502's declared-intent vocabulary (full, brief, coordinate, discuss, holistic, enrich, no sources, investigate:, compare:, adaptor:), the Part 8.0 Striker commands (lite, full, show me the arms, go deeper, Certify Navigator), M306's Aperture and Intention Explorer worksheet, M307's Excitement Log, M311's Return Log, M316's --personal contrast, Step 0.8's phrasing assistance, M241's comparative phrasing, the Arc Proposal — all of these are optional, Striker-invoked, and invisible to a Striker who has not read Parts 0.0.3, 3, 5.2, 8.0, 9.5, 42, 50, 51, 62 and 63.

The First Contact Handoff (Part 2) is explicit: "a Striker may read all of it, part of it, or none, and the handoff is the same." So the protocol knows the Striker may not read it — and yet the optional features are not collected anywhere a reader of FC alone would find them.

There is a second, subtler case. The protocol is honest that its readings are diagnostic and that its empirical claims are open (Part 3.18, M267 not run). A Striker who does not know that the personal contrast (M316 --personal) exists, or that the Lite view exists, or that the Scaffold view exists, cannot make an informed decision about what kind of use is appropriate for their question. Informed choice requires knowing the menu — not of answers, but of tools.

2. The case against

Two provisions push the other way, and they are not incidental.

First, the no-menu rule. Part 2's Opening Response: "The opening carries no conversational preamble, no restatement of execution status in prose, and no menu of modes." The audit (m193_m225.py --audit) has a MENU regex that specifically fails the opening for offering "would you like… navigation… discussion/analysis/instrument/test." The reason is architectural: the opening is an invitation, not a branch point. A Navigator that presents itself as a menu of services is selling, not inviting.

Second, the Navigator must not "sell." M207, M281 (PRESSURE, AUTHORITY, OUTCOME-PROMISE), L-486(H) ("performed boldness"), and L-463 together establish that the Navigator informs judgment, it does not shape the Striker's choice among options. A list of features presented with enthusiasm is a soft form of shaping.

So the protocol is genuinely pulled in two directions: Striker sovereignty wants the features visible; Navigator restraint wants them not pushed. The resolution the protocol already uses — and which the proposal should adopt — is to distinguish a menu of modes (which is forbidden) from an inventory of features (which is allowed, once, in FC, in the invitational register, without enthusiasm).

Note also: FC already has a table of contents. Pages 33–35 of the Core list FC-00.5 through FC-22 as a menu of entries to read, not a menu of modes to choose. That is the precedent: an enumerated list in FC is fine; an enumerated list in the Opening Response is not.

3. What is already in FC, and what is missing

FC already mentions some of this, in passing:

· FC-05 invites any question.
· FC-08 explains how to phrase a question (and Step 0.8's phrasing assistance is mentioned only in the spec, not in FC).
· FC-09 states the Striker's role.
· FC-14.5 describes the web-app's free-on-submission condition.
· FC-22 mentions the Tier 3 companion instruments briefly.

Missing from FC entirely:

· L-502's declared-intent vocabulary (full, brief, coordinate, discuss, holistic, enrich, no sources, investigate:, compare:, adaptor:).
· Part 8.0's Striker commands (lite, full, show me the arms, go deeper, Certify Navigator).
· The Scaffold view.
· L-494's LENSES REQUESTED / EXCLUDED.
· L-482's Cultural Rendering (a tradition of your own).
· L-491's OPTIONAL INQUIRY (the Striker may decline).
· L-504's ENRICHMENT OPPORTUNITIES (the Navigator may offer; the Striker may decline).
· M306 (Aperture and Intention Explorer), M307 (Excitement Log), M311 (Return Log) — the Striker-side records.
· M316 --personal / --reveal / --tally (the Striker's own contrast).
· Step 0.8 (phrasing assistance) and M241 (comparative phrasing).
· The Arc Proposal.
· L-495's privacy flags (PUBLIC / PROFESSIONAL / PERSONAL).
· L-482's rendering in the Striker's own tradition.

These are not minor. Several are among the protocol's most useful provisions for a Striker, and a Striker who reads only FC will never hear of them.

4. Recommendations

R1 — A new FC entry: FC-23, "What a Striker May Ask For." A short, plain-language entry (in the register of FC-05 or FC-09), listing the optional features in three groups, without enthusiasm:

1. How you ask: phrasing assistance on request (Step 0.8); comparative phrasing for two or three phrasings (M241); declare your intent (full, brief, coordinate, discuss, holistic, enrich, no sources, investigate:, compare:, adaptor: — L-502).
2. How the reading is presented: the Lite view (a short version); the full navigation; the Scaffold view (the coordinate and four prompts, no prose — the synthesis left to you); "go deeper" for a second pass; "another analog" (if adopted, as I recommended earlier); the Arc Proposal (a sequence of strikes from the closing questions).
3. What you may add or decline: lenses by request or exclusion (L-494); a tradition of your own (L-482); an optional inquiry, or none (L-491); the Navigator's enrichment observations, or none (L-504); the Striker-side records — the Aperture and Intention worksheet (M306), the Excitement Log (M307), the Return Log (M311), the personal contrast (M316 --personal); privacy flags on the web-app (L-495); "Certify Navigator" for an audit of the last output.

Where: Core FC list, after FC-22; and in the Companion's FC mirror if one exists; and in the web-app's First Contact page.

R2 — The FC contents list should name FC-23 alongside the others. The current list (Pages 33–35) already names FC-00.5 through FC-22. Adding FC-23 to the list is a one-line change and makes the entry discoverable.

R3 — The web-app's First Contact page should carry FC-23 in full. The web-app is the surface where a Striker is most likely to meet the protocol without reading the spec. The entry should be on the FC page, not buried in a help menu.

R4 — The Opening Response should not be changed. The no-menu rule of Part 2 and the MENU regex of --audit are correct: the opening is an invitation, not a branch point. The offer goes in FC (R1), not in the Opening Response.

R5 — L-499 (Question Verbatim) and the closing line remain unchanged. A Striker who reads FC-23 and chooses a feature is still under the same architecture: the question is verbatim, the reading is complete, the judgment is theirs.

R6 — A "Striker's Card" (optional). A one-page, plain-language card — not part of either document — listing the features of R1 in the shortest possible form, printable and linkable. It would not be embedded in the documents (no history of it would be kept, L-411), but it would live in the repository and on the website. It is a useful public-surface companion to FC-23.

R7 — Recommendations to the web-app. If the web-app is to implement FC-23, it should present the features as available, not as recommended; and the privacy flags should be presented before the first strike, not after. This is consistent with L-495's "stated in plain words and agreed to before the first strike."

R8 — Do not add L-505 or a new law for this. The FC entry suffices. The laws register is for structural commitments; the FC entry is for discoverability. Adding a law would over-weight a documentation change.

R9 — Coordinate with the Navigator's Account proposal (from the earlier exchange). If the Navigator's Account is adopted, FC-23 should say so explicitly, so a Striker who wants the AI's own presentation can find it.

R10 — Coordinate with the AI caveat proposal. If the AI caveat is added to FC (as I recommended earlier), FC-23 should follow it, so that the Striker who has just read what the Navigator is and is not can immediately see what options are available to them.

5. The distinction that keeps the proposal honest

The proposal's strength depends on a distinction that the protocol already makes, and that a hasty implementation could collapse:

· A menu of modes is choosing what the Navigator will do with the Striker's question before the question is asked. It is forbidden in the Opening Response (Part 2), and its prohibition is architecturally correct: it would make the Navigator a service and the Striker a customer.
· An inventory of features is naming what a Striker may invoke, once, at the end of FC, without enthusiasm, so that the Striker can choose informedly. It is permitted, and it is required by the principle that the Striker decides (SS-01, L-388): a sovereignty that is not informed is not sovereignty.

The proposal is a feature inventory, not a mode menu. If it is implemented as a menu — "would you like a navigation, or a discussion, or an examination?" — it fails the audit and misses the point. If it is implemented as an inventory — "these are the options; use any of them, or none" — it strengthens the Aperture rather than filling it.

6. The deeper answer

The protocol already has the answer, distributed: the Striker decides (SS-01), the Navigator does not push (L-463, M281), the optional features are marked as optional (L-491, L-504, L-494), and the FC handoff allows the Striker to read all, part or none (Part 2). What is missing is discoverability: a Striker who reads FC-05 to FC-22 will meet the invitation and the role but not the tools. FC-23 (with R2, R3, R6) closes that gap without touching the opening, the audit, or the architecture.

One sentence, for the FC-23 entry, in the register FC already uses:

You do not need to know the machinery to ask your question, and you do not need to use any of the options below. If you want to know how the Navigator can shape its reply to you, or what you may ask for or decline, they are named here so that your choice is an informed one.

That is the register the protocol asks for: invitational, non-promotional, and honest about what is available.

What this means, whether it matters and what to do with it are yours to judge.
