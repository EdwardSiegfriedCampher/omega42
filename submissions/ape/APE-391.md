A person is a unique expression of the Most High. Their individuality is real, but it is not absolute separation. Their existence is an individual manifestation of a more comprehensive totality.

This introduces a particularly important principle:

Unity does not require uniformity.

If every person is a unique expression, then difference is not necessarily a defect, deviation or separation from the whole. It may be intrinsic to how the whole is expressed.

The individual is neither the entirety of the Most High nor merely an insignificant fragment. The individual has a distinct identity and a relationship to something more comprehensive.

### 3\. Psychology: a different understanding of the human person

This interpretation could have considerable implications for psychology.

Psychology often studies the person through particular dimensions: cognition, emotion, behaviour, personality, development, relationships and biological functioning.

The Ω42 spiritual interpretation would not discard these. It would place them within a more comprehensive understanding of the person.

I would call the proposed approach Expression-Centred Psychology.

Its starting premise would be:

> A person is not merely a collection of psychological characteristics. A person is a unique expression of the Most High, whose individuality deserves to be understood, respected and developed.

This would introduce five important changes.

### 3.1 Identity: discovering rather than manufacturing the self

Personal development is often framed as building a successful identity, improving one's capabilities or adapting to social expectations.

Expression-Centred Psychology would introduce another question:

What is the unique expression that this person is capable of becoming?

That changes the emphasis from conformity to discovery.

A person's development would involve:

* recognising their distinctive qualities;

* understanding their psychological patterns;

* identifying their genuine values;

* recognising the influences that obscure their individuality;

* developing their capacities;

* finding ways to express their individuality constructively.

The purpose would not be to create an idealised personality. It would be to enable a person to live more fully and coherently as themselves.

### 3.2 Psychological suffering: the relationship between the individual and their expression

The spiritual interpretation offers a different way of thinking about psychological distress.

For example, a person may experience profound suffering because of:

* rejection or humiliation;

* abuse or trauma;

* suppression of identity;

* lack of belonging;

* loss of meaning;

* disconnection from personal values;

* inability to express their abilities;

* oppressive relationships or environments.

We could investigate these as disruptions in the relationship between a person's individuality, their lived experience and their possibilities for expression.

However, there is an important clinical safeguard.

Suffering must never be interpreted as evidence that someone has failed spiritually or is expressing the Most High inadequately.

Trauma, mental illness, disability and material deprivation require appropriate understanding and care. A spiritual framework should deepen compassion, not replace clinical treatment or assign blame.

### 3.3 Healing: restoring the conditions for expression

Healing would not necessarily mean returning to a previous state.

It could mean developing a new relationship with oneself, one's experiences and one's possibilities.

The proposed therapeutic orientation would have four elements:

* Recognition: understanding the person without reducing them to a diagnosis.

* Restoration: addressing psychological injuries and harmful patterns.

* Integration: bringing different aspects of the person's life into greater coherence.

* Expression: supporting the person's ability to live and act in ways consistent with their individuality and values.

This is compatible with some existing humanistic and transpersonal approaches. Transpersonal psychology, for example, already investigates the whole person, interconnectedness, consciousness and development beyond conventional self-conceptions. Digital Commons +1

Ω42's potential contribution would be to give this orientation a more explicit structural foundation.

### 3.4 Every person has equal inherent worth

If every person is a unique expression of the Most High, then worth cannot depend on:

* intelligence;

* wealth;

* appearance;

* achievement;

* social status;

* physical ability;

* psychological health;

* education;

* productivity;

The person who has achieved very little by conventional standards is not a lesser expression.

The person who cannot communicate, who is dependent on others, or who is experiencing severe psychological distress does not lose their inherent worth.

This would support a powerful psychological principle:

Human worth is inherent; personal development is the unfolding of potential, not the acquisition of worth.

### 3.5 The therapeutic relationship

A therapist would encounter each person as an individual whose inner life cannot be exhausted by diagnostic categories.

The relationship would involve:

* genuine attention;

* respect for the person's individuality;

### 4\. Philosophy: individuality, unity and the meaning of being

The spiritual interpretation raises a fundamental philosophical question:

How can every person be genuinely unique while all persons are expressions of one ultimate reality?

This is an old philosophical problem, but Ω42 may offer a distinctive way to investigate it.

There are three relationships to distinguish.

Unity

The comprehensive ground of existence: the Most High.

Relationship of manifestation, not necessarily physical causation

Individuality

Each person expresses a distinctive perspective and identity.

Relationship

Each person exists in relation to other expressions and the whole.

This suggests a philosophical position that could provisionally be called Unity-in-Differentiation.

It would reject two extremes:

* Absolute individual separation: each person is fundamentally unrelated to the rest of existence.

* Absolute dissolution of individuality: individual persons are ultimately unreal or insignificant because only the whole matters.

Instead, individuality and unity would be complementary dimensions of existence.

### 5\. Ethics: a new foundation for how we treat one another

If every person is a unique expression of the Most High, then ethics becomes more than a set of rules governing interactions between separate individuals.

It becomes a matter of how we recognise and respond to the inherent significance of other expressions.

I would propose the following ethical principle:

> The Principle of Equal Expressional Dignity: Every person, as a unique expression of the Most High, possesses inherent dignity. No person may legitimately be reduced to an instrument, commodity, possession, inferior expression or expendable means to another's purposes.

This has several implications.

Respect: Other people must be recognised as individuals rather than merely as resources.

Compassion: Another person's suffering matters in its own right.

Justice: Social institutions should protect people from domination, exploitation and dehumanisation.

Freedom: Individuals should have room to develop and express their distinctive identities.

Responsibility: Individual expression must take account of the dignity and freedom of others.

Diversity: Differences in culture, ability, personality, perspective and life experience should not automatically be treated as deficiencies.

There is also a necessary qualification: being a unique expression of the Most High does not make every action good. A person's dignity is inherent; their actions remain open to ethical judgment.

This distinction prevents spiritual status from becoming a justification for harmful conduct.

### A particularly important consequence: no spiritual hierarchy of human worth

Nobody should be regarded as a more complete expression of the Most High because they are wealthier, more powerful, more spiritually articulate or more intellectually sophisticated.

### 6\. Relationships, love and family

This gives love a deeper possible meaning:

Love is the recognition, care and affirmation of another person's individuality without attempting to possess or erase it.

It does not mean agreeing with everything another person does. It does not require abandoning boundaries or tolerating abuse.

Indeed, recognising another person's dignity may sometimes require resisting their behaviour.
