1. The essential distinction: finite recurrence and infinite continuation

The supplied document says:

9. Quantum mathematics: state, measurement, probability and information

A pure state is a ray represented by a normalised vector

\[
|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal H,
\qquad
\langle\psi|\psi\rangle=1.

A mixed state is represented by a density operator

\[
\rho\geq0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho)=1.
\]

The quantum state evolves unitarily under a Hamiltonian \(H\):

\[
i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}|\psi(t)\rangle
=
H|\psi(t)\rangle.
\]

For a time-independent Hamiltonian,

\[
|\psi(t)\rangle
=
e^{-iHt/\hbar}|\psi(0)\rangle.
\]

This is reversible evolution when the Hamiltonian is self-adjoint.

9.2 The Born rule

For a measurement represented by orthogonal projectors \(P_i\), the probability of outcome \(i\) is

\[
p_i=\langle\psi|P_i|\psi\rangle.
\]

For a mixed state,

\[
p_i=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho P_i).
\]

The Ω42 surface provides discrete states and transitions, but it does not yet provide:
