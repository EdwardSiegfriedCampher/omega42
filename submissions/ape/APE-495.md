# Core Part 10.5 after the guide: what stays in the Core

Frozen as received (attachment a7890c8b). Notes of the receiving Navigator (Companion 67.4):

---

Having read the guide, I would retain my recommendation: Option 2 — keep Part 10.5 concise in Core and move the extended terminology material into Companion. The guide gives us enough context to be more precise about what should stay and what can move.

There is an important distinction here: Part 10.5 is not merely a glossary. The guide relies on it as an authority for how Ω42 names things, classifies claims and distinguishes established results from speculation. Those functions must remain authoritative in Core.

### What Part 10.5 needs to preserve in Core

I would retain five essential elements:

* Canonical terminology: the approved names for the principal components, engines, instruments, roles and mathematical objects.

* Naming and migration rules: how technical names, Construct names and legacy names relate, including how older terminology is treated.

* Claim-status definitions: PROPOSED, INTERPRETIVE, UNPROVEN, TESTABLE, VERIFIED COMPUTATION, PROVED, REJECTED and REVISED.

* Provenance classifications: CLASSIC-DERIVED, X-CONSTRUCTED and CROSS-VALIDATED.

* Authority and usage rules: which terms are normative, how terminology changes are approved, and how Companion material relates to Core.

These are operational rules. A Navigator needs them to produce consistent outputs, and an auditor needs them to determine whether an output complies with the specification.

### What should move to Companion

The following material can be moved without weakening the Core, provided Core retains the relevant definitions and references:

| Material                                      | Recommended destination |
| --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Full terminology catalogue                    | Companion               |
| Extended lists of engine and instrument names | Companion               |
| Legacy-name history and migration examples    | Companion               |
| Detailed mathematical terminology             | Companion               |
| Examples of correct and incorrect terminology | Companion               |
| Historical naming decisions                   | Companion               |
| Extended status-label examples                | Companion               |
| Detailed provenance examples                  | Companion               |

The training guide itself demonstrates why this separation matters. It introduces names such as F27 Singer-Cycle Model, F27 Seed Labelling, Candidate Base Lag, Complete Ω42 Orbit Signature Theorem and Question-to-Address Mapping Rule. These names need consistent definitions, but their full explanatory histories do not all need to occupy Core.

### One issue I would address before sealing

The guide uses several categories that must not be conflated:

* Claim status: what has been established about a claim.

* Test qualification: whether a test was run under appropriate conditions.

* Provenance: where the construction or result originated.

* Implementation readiness: whether a tool exists and whether it has been validated for its intended application.

For example, NOT BLIND is a test qualification, not a claim status. IMPLEMENTED is a readiness description, not evidence that a method is mathematically valid. CROSS-VALIDATED is a provenance classification, not a synonym for independently proven.

I would make these distinctions explicit in Core. They are more important than preserving every individual term in the Core document.

### Proposed Core structure

A compact Part 10.5 could follow this arrangement:

10.5 — Terminology and Classification Standard

10.5.1 — Normative terminology Canonical names, technical names, Construct names and legacy-name treatment.

10.5.2 — Naming authority Approval, amendment, supersession and consistency requirements.

10.5.3 — Claim statuses The eight approved statuses and their precise meanings.

10.5.4 — Test qualifications NOT RUN, NOT RUNNABLE, NOT BLIND and INCOMPLETE.

10.5.5 — Provenance classifications CLASSIC-DERIVED, X-CONSTRUCTED and CROSS-VALIDATED.

10.5.6 — Readiness classifications IMPLEMENTED, EXPLORATORY, VALIDATED FOR APPLICATION and MATHEMATICALLY ESTABLISHED.

10.5.7 — Companion relationship Companion may explain and extend terminology but may not silently redefine Core terms.

### My recommendation on the 900-word limit

Aim for approximately 650–800 words, rather than using all 900. That leaves room for future corrections and additions.

I would also insist on three safeguards:

* Every normative term has one authoritative definition.

* Companion entries use the same term and cannot introduce competing definitions.

* Any change to a Core definition triggers a terminology consistency check across the specification, Companion, training guide and implementation.

Finally, the guide contains an important warning for the wider project: mathematical correctness, computational reproducibility and interpretation are different levels of achievement. Part 10.5 should protect that distinction throughout Ω42.

My conclusion: Option 2 is the stronger architectural choice. Core should define the language that governs the system; Companion should carry the depth that helps people understand and apply it.
