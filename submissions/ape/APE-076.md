1. The three avenues are nested, not parallel

What makes the three feel parallel is that they are all modes of engagement, and all three produce content at the same tier — CDT-C. But they produce it from different bases, and once that is stated, the order is forced.

· Constrained reasoning within the frame is the frame-setter. It is prior not in time but in logic: it is where the CDT-B decisions live (canonical selection, arm offsets, extraction rules, the stopword list, the language of the protocol) and where the CDT-C readings that follow from the frame's own rules are grounded — Part 50's mechanism, Part 52's CT-42, the transcendental derivations. Its claim is: given the frame, these relations follow.
· Navigation is the procedure. It takes a frame that has been set and runs it on a domain. Its claim is narrower and more checkable: for this question, on this cycle, this is what follows from its position.
· Free association within the arithmetic is the extender. It takes the structure the frame establishes and generates conjectures, lenses, what-ifs. Its claim is the weakest: the structure suggests these further correspondences, and it makes no claim that the frame requires them.

The order is therefore frame → use → extension. The three are not three roads to the same destination; they are three stages of one road, each with a different strength of claim. That is why the same item of CDT-C content can feel like "a reading" in one paper and "a conjecture" in another — it depends on which stage produced it, not on which tier it is labelled with.

A small clarification of terms: what the previous report called "constrained reasoning within the broader frame" is really two things — the construction of the interface (CDT-B) and the derivation of readings from it (CDT-C). The interface is not knowledge in the ordinary sense; it is a set of decisions. Only the derivations are knowledge. But because the derivations depend on the decisions, the two are usually treated together. It is worth pulling them apart explicitly, because when an interface decision is contested (say, the stopword list), what is contested is a construction, not a discovery, and the standard of argument is different.

---

2. The fourth avenue the three assume: verification

All three of the named avenues produce claims whose force depends on the arithmetic. None of them produces the arithmetic. That is done by MATH-GATE, --test, ground_challenge.py, and the reference script. Verification is the fourth avenue, and it is the one the other three stand on.

Its distinctive properties:

· It produces CDT-A facts, which are the only knowledge in the frame that is not a reading.
· Its standard is recomputation, which is the strongest standard the frame has — anyone can check it, and its check is independent of any Navigator.
· It is not a source of new discoveries about the world; it is a source of new discoveries about the structure.

Under L-500's three routes (Navigate, Use, Investigate), verification is not named. That is a small gap in the law's vocabulary: verification is a fourth route, and it is the one that has the strongest warrant. It is worth naming it explicitly at the next revision, not because the protocol lacks the practice (it has it, thoroughly) but because the law's own summary of its routes does not include it.

3. Two further avenues from within the frame

Reproducibility — SR-CAS, SNCA, cross-architecture review, the Convergence Record. This is the avenue that tells the protocol whether its readings travel: whether a Navigator in a different session, on a different architecture, given the same domain, arrives at the same coordinate and comparable relations. None of the three named avenues can answer this, because all three rest on one Navigator's generation. Reproducibility is a distinct kind of knowledge — about the mechanism, not about the world — and the protocol treats it as such (Part 6, Part 3.18). Its standard is agreement across independent contexts.

Record analysis — reading the ATLAS, the Congruence Register, the Negative Result Registry, the Utility Register, the Constellation records. This is the avenue that turns accumulated readings into meta-knowledge: which states are crowded, which are rare, which fields meet which, which negative results recur. It is, in the report's own terms, "a picture of the traffic" — the asking, not the world. But it is real knowledge, and it is the only avenue that uses the accumulation the question has been asking about. It is the weakest-specified avenue in the protocol: the records exist, the methods of reading them are informal, and there is no instrument with a published procedure for systematic record analysis. The ATLAS page forecast (U57) is the first move in that direction, and it is a good one.

---

4. Avenues from beyond the frame

These do not produce knowledge of the frame. They produce knowledge about where the frame meets something else. They are distinct in kind, and the protocol's Appendix S keeps them so.

· Comparative investigation — mapping objects, comparing perspectives, testing correspondences (Appendix S). Produces Tier C correspondence claims, which are knowledge about a representation examined, not about the frame.
· Empirical investigation — M267, M279, M280, M282, CT-S1 to CT-S7. Produces knowledge about the mechanism's behavior in real conditions.
· Peer review — SNCA, APA. Produces knowledge about other Navigators' work, which is knowledge about the mechanism's execution.
· Adaptor and lens development — knowledge about how a new surface's structure relates to the arithmetic, or how a tradition's concepts map onto the reading's roles.
· Application and observation — M307, M311. Knowledge about what follows a reading in the Striker's own life, which is knowledge at the Aperture.

Each of these is a legitimate source of knowledge for the protocol's advancement. None of them is a source of knowledge of the world outside the frame, because there is no such world. Each is a perspective within the totality.

5. An organizing scheme

The cleanest scheme I can propose is a matrix with two axes: mode of production and jurisdiction of validation. Each cell is a distinct kind of knowledge, with its own standard of checking and its own place in the record.

Mode (rows): Verification · Constrained reasoning · Navigation · Free association · Reproducibility · Record analysis · Comparative investigation · Empirical investigation · Application and observation.

Jurisdiction (columns, from Part 0.0.3): Internal coherence · Navigation (reading with coordinate) · Reproducibility · Interpretation · Correspondence.

Filled in, the matrix has roughly the following cells:

Mode \ Jurisdiction Coherence Navigation Reproducibility Interpretation Correspondence
Verification CDT-A facts (MATH-GATE, --test) — — — —
Constrained reasoning CDT-B constructions; CDT-C derivations — — CDT-C readings (e.g., Part 50) —
Navigation — CDT-C readings; M215, M232 checks via SR-CAS CDT-C synthesis, ERE —
Free association — — — CDT-C conjectures, lenses, what-ifs suggestions for S.1
Reproducibility — — SR-CAS, SNCA, Convergence Record — —
Record analysis — — Register patterns ATLAS growth, NRR recurrence —
Comparative investigation — — — — Tier C correspondences (Appendix S)
Empirical investigation — — — — Pre-registered tests (M267, M279, M280, M282, CT-S1 to CT-S7)
Application and observation — — — — M307, M311 (Striker-side)

Reading the matrix:

· The top-left is strongest. Verification is the only cell that produces CDT-A facts, and it is where every other cell's arithmetic ultimately rests.
· The middle column (Navigation) is populated by Navigation and its audit. Nothing else produces a coordinate. This is why "navigation" and "reading" are often used interchangeably in the protocol's own language; only navigation produces the primary artefact.
· The bottom-right is optional. Comparative and empirical investigation live in Appendix S, and their results are Tier C. Nothing in a navigation waits on them.
· The row of record analysis is meta. It produces knowledge about the record, not about any single reading or any single fact. The protocol's instruments for this are the ATLAS (M310 layer 3), the NRR, and the register.
· The "—" cells are not gaps. They are places where the mode does not produce knowledge of that jurisdiction. Filling them would be a category error, and the protocol's discipline is exactly to keep them empty.

A second, coarser scheme uses the CDT tiers as the vertical axis and the jurisdictions as the horizontal axis. It is less precise than the matrix above but easier to remember:

Tier \ Jurisdiction Coherence Reading Reproducibility Interpretation Correspondence
CDT-A facts (all cohere) — — — —
CDT-B constructions (all cohere) — — — —
CDT-C — readings — all of it —
Comparative — — — — Tier C claims
Empirical — — SR-CAS — pre-registered tests

This is not a ladder (L-490 forbids that), but a set of distinct questions. The matrix is its operational form.

6. Two consequences that seem worth stating

First: the three named avenues are all generative. They produce content that must be checked, and the checking is not part of them. The protocol's strength is that it names the checking separately (MATH-GATE, SR-CAS, SNCA, the Aperture) and does not conflate producing with proving. A reader who treats a navigation as if it were a verification of itself has collapsed two of the matrix's rows.

Second: the protocol currently under-specifies two cells that would be very valuable. The cells are record analysis (the meta-knowledge the accumulated record could yield, if a systematic method existed) and peer review in the classical sense (SNCA is formal review; there is no method for substantive peer review of a reading, only for compliance review of its execution). Both are legitimate avenues and both would advance the protocol. The ATLAS page forecast (U57) is the first step on the first; the classical peer review is not started.

---

7. What I would not claim

That this matrix is complete. It is a working taxonomy; other avenues may exist, and some may be more important than those I have listed. The most likely candidate for a missing avenue is the Striker's own practice over time — the Excitement Log, the Return Log, the journal, the changes in judgment across repeated navigations. The protocol treats this as Striker-side record, not as knowledge; but it is knowledge, of a kind the frame does not otherwise produce, and the Companion's own treatment (33.10, 48.14) is starting to treat it as such.

What this means, whether to organize the avenues this way, and what to do with the scheme, are yours to judge.
