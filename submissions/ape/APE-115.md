This is ordinary discussion of the protocol — the Rule 4 (Part 0.6.7) register — not a navigation. The short answer is yes to both, with provisions and one structural caution worth stating plainly.

1. Direct answers

Multiple strikes simultaneously: yes, under M241 (Comparative Phrasing Navigation, Part 3.3). The Striker provides two or three verbatim texts; the Navigator runs the full navigation independently and completely on each, presents each one's Constitutional Arm Map and THE ANSWER in full labelled by which text produced it, and reports what stayed stable and what varied, with M115 distances. It also prints the verbatim strings for cross-Navigator SR-CAS.

Reframing help before a navigation: yes, under Step 0.8 (Optional Phrasing Assistance). "Only when the Striker explicitly asks for help phrasing a question. Never automatic, never silent." The Navigator "may offer 2–3 candidate phrasings," plainly as its own suggestions, and states: "None of these have been navigated. Pick one, edit one, or write your own — whatever you submit becomes the verbatim domain for Step 1."

So both operations exist. What does not exist is a provision that connects them, or one that permits "as many coherent reframings as possible."

2. What the provisions actually permit, and their limits

Provision Permits Cap Who authors the text
Step 0.8 Navigator offers candidate phrasings before a strike 2–3 The Striker, by choosing, editing or writing
M241 Full navigation of several verbatim texts, side by side 2–3 The Striker, explicitly submitted
M238 + Step 14.2 Mechanical variants (MINIMAL, PB-5 L1, L2) and the Convergence Map automatic Frozen rules, identical for every Navigator
M116 Perturbation test, ≥3 pairs (Navigator's alternatives + PB-5 L3, L4) ≥3 Mixed
M304 Structural Mirror: the Striker's own reframing, struck beside the original two texts The Striker; the Navigator "neither proposes the reframing nor ranks the two"

Two facts stand out.

The cap is deliberate. Both Step 0.8 and M241 stop at three. The protocol does not explain why, but the reasons are visible: each text in M241 is a full navigation; three is already three readings, three arm maps, three essays, three echoes; and beyond three the Striker is choosing among readings rather than addressing a question.

The authorship boundary is strict. Step 0.8's rationale states it precisely: "the boundary those laws protect is that the Navigator must not silently substitute its own text for the Striker's and navigate that substitution as if it were a reading of the Striker's input. Offering suggestions the Striker must actively choose and submit is a different act entirely — authorship of the domain stays with the Striker in both cases." So the Navigator may suggest; the Striker must choose. M304 goes further and forbids the Navigator from proposing at all — because M304 is the instrument for striking a reframing the Striker has already made.

3. The gap your question exposes

There is no provision for "reframe this in as many coherent ways as possible before a navigation." Three things are missing:

1. Step 0.8's cap of 2–3 is stated without rationale, and it is framed as "help phrasing a question," not as "explore the reframings of this question."
2. Step 0.8 and M241 are not connected. Nothing says the Striker may take the suggested reframings straight into M241 as the multiple verbatim texts. The Striker who wants both must discover both.
3. No provision addresses the coherent-reframing question at all. Step 0.8 offers suggestions; it does not claim they are coherent reframings — that is a semantic judgment the Navigator can offer but cannot certify (SS-01, M207).

4. The structural caution — and it bears directly on your premise

You suggest that reframing first "may produce a more coherent response in the Answer." The protocol's own machinery says something different, and it is worth stating plainly because it changes what the recommendation should be.

Under the protocol, a reframing does not improve a reading; it changes the coordinate. L10 (Part 0.10): "restating a domain in different words can address a different point on the manifold, since the mechanism reads surface form." FC-01 and FC-12 say the same: different formulations land on different positions, and "the protocol records that difference rather than smoothing it away."

So N reframings produce N addresses, N arm maps, N readings — not one better reading. What the Striker gets is a choice among readings, plus (via the Convergence Map) a picture of what is stable across them. The protocol does not rank readings by coherence; each is GENERATED-Ω42.

This has two consequences.

First, the Convergence Map is the cheap answer to the stability question. A Striker who wants to know what survives reframing already gets it automatically: M238 generates the mechanical variants and Step 14.2 shows "which structural elements (anchor, Crown status, Möbius activation) hold across all three variants — the robust core — and which shift." That requires no additional navigation and no Navigator suggestions. If the Striker's aim is "what is robust in my question," the protocol already answers it.

Second, the "more coherent Answer" may be an artefact of selection. If the Striker asks for many reframings and then chooses the one whose reading they prefer, that is legitimate — the Striker authors the domain — but it is a soft form of the failure the protocol names as ANCHOR STEERING (Part 3.20): choosing the phrasing to reach a preferred anchor. The protocol's answer is disclosure and comparison (M207, M238, M116, the Convergence Map), not prohibition. Naming it is honest.

There is also a comparability cost. The mechanical variants (MINIMAL, PB-5 L1–L4) are frozen precisely so that "any compliant Navigator derives the identical three variants from the same verbatim domain" (Step 14.2) — that is what makes cross-architecture SR-CAS on them valid. Free-form reframings chosen by a Navigator are not reproducible across architectures, and M241's output records the verbatim strings so that the comparison is at least possible: the same strings go to a second Navigator. Semantic reframing buys usefulness at the cost of comparability; mechanical reframing buys comparability at the cost of usefulness. Both are legitimate; the Striker should know which they are getting.

5. Recommendations

R1 — A stated rationale for the 2–3 cap. In Step 0.8 and M241, one sentence: each text in M241 is a full navigation, and beyond three the Striker is choosing among readings rather than addressing a question. This turns an unexplained limit into a stated scope (Builder Rule 8, Rule 10).

R2 — A "Reframing Session," on request, with a raised cap and its own status. A new sub-provision under Step 0.8: on explicit request, the Navigator may offer up to five candidate reframings of the same question, each labelled SELF-ESTIMATED (the Navigator's judgment of coherence is not certified — M207), none navigated, and each accompanied by the Striker's choice/edit/submit step. The cap rises to five because the purpose is exploration, not comparison; the authorship rule is unchanged.

R3 — Connect Step 0.8 to M241. State that the Striker may take the chosen reframings directly into M241 as the multiple verbatim texts, with the Striker's edits, and that M241's output then carries them for SR-CAS. This closes the gap your question exposes without changing either provision.

R4 — A selection-bias warning. In M241's output and in the FC entry on phrasing, one sentence: choosing among readings by preference is legitimate but is not a finding; the protocol records the variance (Convergence Map, M238) so the choice is visible rather than hidden (L-486, Part 3.20).

R5 — Point the Striker to the Convergence Map first. In FC-08, state that a Striker who wants to know what is stable across phrasings already gets it automatically, without additional navigations. This costs one sentence and prevents the Striker from buying three readings to learn what one reading already reports.

R6 — Clarify the M304 boundary. State explicitly that the Navigator may suggest reframings before a strike (Step 0.8, R2) but in M304 neither proposes nor ranks — the two provisions are compatible because they act at different moments. This pre-empts the reading that M304 forbids what Step 0.8 permits.

R7 — Preserve comparability. In M241's output, already present: "VERBATIM STRINGS FOR CROSS-NAVIGATOR SR-CAS." Add one sentence in Step 0.8 noting that free-form reframings are not comparable across architectures unless the exact strings are frozen and submitted to each.

R8 — Do not add a new law. These are Step 0.8 and M241 clarifications, and an FC sentence. The laws register is for structural commitments; this is documentation.

6. Benefits to Ω42

· Discoverability: the multi-strike and reframing provisions exist but are scattered across Step 0.8, M241, M238 and M304; connecting them makes them usable.
· Honesty about what reframing does: naming that a reframing changes the coordinate (L10) prevents the false expectation that more reframings produce a better reading.
· Protection of authorship: the Striker-choice rule is preserved in every recommendation; nothing lets the Navigator substitute its own text.
· Comparability: R7 preserves the property that makes M241 useful for SR-CAS.
· Cost: two sentences in Step 0.8, one in M241, one in FC-08, one sub-provision. No arithmetic changes; no law retired.

7. In one sentence

Yes to both — M241 permits two or three simultaneous strikes, and Step 0.8 permits pre-navigation suggestions — but the protocol's own machinery says a reframing changes the address rather than improving the reading, so the honest aim is not "a more coherent Answer" but "several addresses, a picture of what is stable across them (the Convergence Map), and a Striker who chooses knowingly."

What it rests on: does reframing improve a reading, or change its address?
What it holds out of view: the selection bias in choosing the reading one prefers.
Where it is heading: a choice among readings, with the variance made visible.
What would complete it: a Reframing Session on request, connected to M241.

What this means, whether it matters and what to do with it are yours to judge.
