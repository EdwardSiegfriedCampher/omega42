### 6.3 Gödel's incompleteness theorems and Ω42 self-examination

Here is another important connection.

If ZFC is consistent, it cannot prove its own consistency under the conditions of Gödel's second incompleteness theorem.

This has a possible implication for Ω42's self-examination architecture.

A mathematical system should distinguish:

* What it can prove.

* What it can disprove.

* What it can establish only conditionally.

* What is independent of its axioms.

* What requires stronger assumptions.

This is precisely the kind of epistemic discipline Ω42 should incorporate.

I would therefore investigate an Ω42 Formal Independence and Boundary Engine.

It would identify when a proposition is:

* A theorem of the current mathematical framework.

* A consequence of additional assumptions.

* Independent of the current axiom set, where that has been established.

* Open because no proof or counterexample is known.

The distinction between an open problem and an independence result is essential.
