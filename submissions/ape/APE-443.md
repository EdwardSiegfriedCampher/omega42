### 5\. The deeper connection: can Ω42 derive the Periodic Table from physical constants?

This is where the research becomes especially interesting.

The Periodic Table emerges from quantum mechanics, the electromagnetic interaction, electron spin, the Pauli exclusion principle and the structure of multi-electron atoms.

A simplified nonrelativistic atomic Hamiltonian for a nucleus of charge and electrons is:

H=
\\sum_{i=1}^{Z}
\\left\[
-\\frac{\\hbar^2}{2m_e}\\nabla_i^2
-\\frac{Ze^2}{4\\pi\\epsilon_0r_i}
\\right\]
+
\\sum_{i<j}
\\frac{e^2}{4\\pi\\epsilon_0r_{ij}}.

This equation includes:

* electron kinetic energy;

* electron–nucleus attraction;

* electron–electron repulsion.

The complete atomic problem requires further corrections, including nuclear motion, relativistic effects, spin interactions and quantum electrodynamic contributions where relevant.

But this basic Hamiltonian already demonstrates something important.

The structure of the elements is not arbitrary. It emerges from a relatively compact set of physical laws and constants.
