### 1. The Crucial Distinction: Navigating vs. Mapping
The protocol explicitly separates two distinct operations to prevent category errors (Core FC-02.8):
*   **Navigating**: Bringing a question, domain, or text (e.g., "How do I resolve this conflict?") and receiving a structural reading generated from its position on the cycle. This measures the *topology of distinction-making* in human language and intent.
*   **Mapping**: Taking an object that exists in its own right (e.g., a crystal lattice, a quantum state, a rhythmic pattern), fixing a measurable description of it *first*, and then testing how much of its intrinsic structure lands on the cycle's structure. 

The protocol mandates that these two operations "can run side by side... but neither may quietly feed the other." You cannot use a physical mapping to retroactively justify a linguistic navigation, nor vice versa, without explicit, pre-registered methodology.

### 2. The Four Questions of Measurement
When any result or correspondence is proposed, the protocol subjects it to four distinct questions (Core Part 0.6):
*   **(A) Internal Validity**: Did the measurement follow Omega42’s frozen rules? (e.g., Was the digital root calculated correctly? Was the anchor derived via the Canonical Position Projection?)
*   **(B) Internal Coherence**: Do the transformations and relations remain mutually consistent within the 24-cycle?
*   **(C) Comparative Correspondence**: Does the external system (dataset, theory, or physical object) produce a corresponding result?
*   **(D) Usefulness**: Does someone find the result illuminating, generative, or useful?

**Crucially, only (A) and (B) are necessary for a result to be a valid Ω42 result.** (C) and (D) are optional comparative investigations. A lack of external correspondence does *not* invalidate the internal coherence of the navigation (Core L-489, L-421).
