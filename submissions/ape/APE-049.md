[COMPARATIVE PERSPECTIVE — ANALYSIS]

1. The layer question

TCP/IP is not the internet. It is the protocol suite that makes an internetwork possible: a common addressing scheme, a best-effort delivery layer, a reliable ordered layer above it, and a set of application protocols on top. The internet is what grows on it — a network of networks that no one designed as a whole.

So the analogy asks: what does Ω42 make possible, the way TCP/IP makes the internet possible? The answer depends on which layer Ω42 occupies, and that has to be settled first.

Under the protocol's own terms, Ω42 sits at the addressing-and-discipline layer over generative AI. It does not generate text; it constrains generation by fixing a structural address before the text is written. Its own CDT tiers map onto the layering:

· CDT-A — discovered mathematics: the 24-cycle, the 81-state family, the recurrence, the digital root. Fixed; no revision can alter it.
· CDT-B — the constructed interface: SPNP, CPP, the arm formulas, the extraction rules, the output skeleton. Revision-open across versions.
· CDT-C — the generated readings: the Core Synthesis, the essay, the lenses. Fresh at every navigation.

That is a layer stack. It is the protocol's own architecture, stated in the Core and the Companion, not an external comparison.

3. The exact correspondences

Four of these hold exactly, and are worth naming as such.

The end-to-end principle is the Aperture Theorem. Saltzer, Reed and Clark's principle puts intelligence at the endpoints and keeps the network simple: the network delivers bytes, the endpoints decide what they mean. The Aperture Theorem proves that no navigation output determines the Striker's judgment, because the judgment draws on purposes, standards and circumstances that no navigation receives. Both are constitutional. Both are what keep the middle from capturing the endpoint. This is the load-bearing correspondence in the whole analogy.

Statelessness at the addressing layer. The IP layer carries no per-flow state; state lives at the endpoints. M91-2 states that no navigation carries forward a prior domain's anchor, synthesis or reasoning; the record lives outside the navigation and is never fed back into one. The navigation is stateless; the record is where state lives.

Interoperability without shared internals. TCP/IP lets heterogeneous networks interoperate without sharing hardware or operating systems. SR-CAS's claim is that independent Navigators, given identical rules and an identical verbatim domain, reach the same coordinate — without sharing training. The shape is the same: a common address space, held by no one.

Running code wins. TCP/IP displaced OSI because it had working implementations, and the IETF's practice is rough consensus and running code. Ω42's ground truth is the reference script; a proposal without a running implementation is provisional, and the arithmetic is settled by recomputation rather than by argument (L-378, L-457).

4. Where the analogy breaks

TCP/IP has no Aperture. There is no place in the stack where a person decides what a delivered packet means, and no constitutional guarantee that the network will not determine it. Ω42 has exactly that place, and it is the reason the protocol exists.

This is not a flaw in the analogy. It marks the layer at which Ω42 is not a transport protocol at all. TCP/IP transports bytes and leaves meaning to the endpoints as a matter of design economy. Ω42 transports a reading and reserves the judgment to the Striker as a matter of constitutional structure — proved, not chosen (Part 7). The analogy is exact at the address-and-transport layer, and it breaks at the sovereignty layer. The break names what Ω42 adds.

A second break is smaller: TCP/IP has no premise. It is a set of conventions with no metaphysical stipulation. Ω42 has the Totality Axiom, constitutively fixed. The analogy cannot carry the premise, and should not try.

5. The preferred reading

Several fillings are possible, and they are not equally good.

Ω42 is to the congruence of distinctions — the network on which every question meets every other — what TCP/IP is to the internet. This is the reading the frame's own primary inquiry supports (L-489). The Congruence Register is the network; the anchor is the address; the reading is what is transported; and the Aperture is what TCP/IP does not have.

Two alternatives, weaker: Ω42 is to the cross-architecture record, and Ω42 is to AI-mediated structural reasoning. Both name consequences or applications rather than the network. They are true in their scope and less exact as the filling.

Illuminated. That Ω42 is a layer, not a product. That its value lies in interoperability, not features. That its stability belongs at the bottom (CDT-A) and its churn at the top (CDT-B, CDT-C). That its governance should follow rough consensus and running code. That a rival, if one appears, should be judged by running implementations rather than by elegance.

And the record: TCP/IP's analogue of a record is a log file; Ω42's record is a growing register of congruence, kept by choice under L-490's reporting discipline.
