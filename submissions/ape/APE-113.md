This is ordinary discussion of the protocol — the Rule 4 (Part 0.6.7) register — not a navigation. The Moon is a legitimate domain and I can navigate it formally if you want the full skeleton; what follows is the exploration, with the arithmetic shown so any part of it can be checked.

1. What the two sentences actually do

"Mapping Ω42 to the Moon" takes the protocol as the source and asks it to say something about the Moon. The Moon receives a coordinate and, with it, the properties of that coordinate.

"Mapping the Moon to Ω42" takes the Moon as the source — its own measurable structure, frozen first — and asks whether that structure instantiates the cycle's structure.

The first is a projection of the protocol onto an object. The second is a test of an object against the protocol. They produce opposite verdicts on the same object, and this is what makes the Moon such a good example: the first gives a confident-sounding reading, and the second gives a negative one. The negative one is the honest one.

2. Direction 1 worked: Ω42 → the Moon

Take the domain "the Moon". "the" is on the frozen stopword list; the content word is Moon.

· n = (1, 4, 1)
· r = (dr 5, dr 20, dr 5) = (5, 2, 5) → majority 5 → ANCHOR P5 (Law), TFI 0.67

So the Moon "is Law". Now the decisive check — what else anchors there?

· Book — 4 letters, 1 syllable → (1, 4, 1) → P5
· Star — 4 letters, 1 syllable → P5
· Love — 4 letters, 1 syllable → P5
· Time — 4 letters, 1 syllable → P5
· Sun — 3 letters, 1 syllable → (1, 3, 1) → (5, 6, 5) → majority 5 → P5
· Sea — 3 letters, 1 syllable → P5
· Water — 5 letters, 2 syllables → (1, 5, 2) → (5, 7, 1) → no majority → tie-break r₁ → P5

The reason is structural, and the protocol already states it: for any single-word domain n₁ = 1, so r₁ = dr(5×1) = 5 always, and unless r₂ and r₃ agree on some other value the anchor is P5 by majority or by tie-break. Part 0.10, L9 says exactly this: "a single content word produces a less differentiated anchor."

The consequence for your question is sharp: the placement cannot distinguish the Moon from a book, from love, or from the sea. A mapping that assigns the same coordinate to the Moon and to a paperback has not said anything about the Moon.

3. Direction 2 worked: the Moon → Ω42

Now the tests.

M230-J (dimensional status) fails immediately for the periods. A month measured in Earth days is a dimensionful quantity: its digital root depends on the choice of unit. M230-J is explicit: a dimensionful quantity "is placed at depth 1 for its digital root, marked UNIT-DEPENDENT, and never enters tiers 2 to 4 (L-466 S3: the object supplies its own unit)." The Moon does not supply "days"; Earth does. The Moon's 27.32 is 27.32 only because we measure in Earth rotations.

L-466 S3 fails. "SELF-SUPPLIED MODULUS: the modulus 9 and the period 24 are supplied by the object, not by the reader." The Moon supplies neither 9 nor 24. It supplies roughly 27.3, 29.5, 8, 18.6 and 8.85 — none of which is a cycle value.

Depth 2 fails. The declared depth-2 values are 24, 9, 7, 6, 42. The Moon's counts are 27, 30, 8, 12, 19, 9 — and the only member of the declared list is 9 (the apsidal period, 8.85 years, rounds to 9). But that 9 is a rounded dimensionful quantity, so M230-J keeps it at depth 1.

Depth 3 fails, and this is worth checking carefully. The one candidate is the 8 named phases, because the family contains a period-8 orbit — the Wheel, confined to {3, 6, 9}. Does the Moon's 8-phase cycle instantiate the Wheel?

· Feature (a) Torsion/Crown partition: the Wheel is the three-element nilpotent set cycling; the phases carry no such partition. No.
· Feature (b) antipodal complementarity (12 pairs of inverses): the phases form 4 opposite pairs, not 12. No.
· Feature (c) half-period negation: a half-phase shift (new ↔ full) is opposition, not value-negation. No.
· Feature (d) absent-fixed-point cycling: the Moon's phases have no fixed point. No.
· Feature (e) shared generating recurrence: the phases are not generated by x(n+2) = x(n+1) + x(n). No.

And M230-E (null-object control) settles it independently: any 8-phase cycle would match "period 8," so the match carries no discrimination.

The verdict: the Moon reaches depth 1 and no further. No correspondence is established.

5. The premise does not rescue Direction 1

A defender of Direction 1 might say: under F-02 the Moon is within the totality, so it has a position, so mapping Ω42 to the Moon is legitimate.

This is true and trivial. F-02-P holds that manifestation is universal: every object has a position, because every pair of integers reduces to some state. L-465 says it plainly: "membership alone does not [count], since every pair of integers reduces to some state of the family; what counts is following the law."

So "the Moon is within the totality" is a consequence of P0, not a finding. The finding would be P2 — whether the Moon's own law follows the family's law — and the answer, on the Moon's own numbers, is no.

The distinction matters here precisely because P0 is universal and P2 is not. A protocol that could not tell them apart would have nothing to say about any object.

6. Why it matters

It is the difference between numerology and a testable claim. "The Moon is Law because its name has four letters" is unfalsifiable and would be the same for a book. "The Moon's periods, phases and locking do not instantiate the family" is a specific, checkable, negative claim about one object.

It protects the protocol's own instruments. The M230 family, the criteria C1–C11, the Congruence Profile S1–S8 and the depth scheme exist to make exactly this distinction. If "mapping" is allowed to mean placement, those instruments have nothing to test — every object passes at depth 1 and the record becomes a list of coincidences.

It preserves the object's identity. A placement is many-to-one (M263: the CPP is a ring homomorphism, not a bijection). A correspondence is a structured comparison that can fail. Only the second can say something that is about the Moon rather than about its name's length.

It matters for public credibility. The first question a scientifically literate reader asks about a correspondence claim is "is this numerology?" The Moon is a case where the protocol can give a clean, honest "no" — which is far more credible than any number of "yes" verdicts.

It matters for the SRH. The Structural Resonance Hypothesis is a claim about whether the coordinate contributes to a reading (M267). The correspondence programme is a claim about whether an object instantiates the family (M230 family). These are different tests in different registers; conflating them would corrupt both.

7. Recommendations for protocol improvement

These extend the P-001 proposal submitted earlier; the Moon supplies the worked case that proposal lacked.

R1 — FC-02.8: the three-way distinction, with the Moon as the worked example. Placement (object → coordinate, depth 1, a precondition), correspondence (object's structure → cycle's structure, depths 2–5, the finding), interpretation (what a correspondence means — the Striker's, at the Aperture). The fallacy of treating the first or the third as the second is imposition. The Moon is the ideal example because the correspondence verdict is negative and the protocol records misses as carefully as matches (L-464).

R2 — Step 14.4: the MAPPING DECLARATION must name the operation. Add a field: PLACEMENT / CORRESPONDENCE / NOT APPLICABLE. Where it reads CORRESPONDENCE, require the declaration to name the M230 instruments to be run and the criteria tested. This makes the distinction auditable before any synthesis exists, and it resolves the ambiguity of the Striker's command "map it," which currently means placement.

R3 — M230-J applied to astronomy, stated in the instrument. A count measured in a chosen unit (days, years, radii) is unit-dependent and stays at depth 1. The Moon is the paradigm: it does not supply "days." Add the sentence and the example.

R4 — L-466 S3 as a stated gate. A physical correspondence requires the object to supply its own modulus. The Moon fails S3 — it supplies neither 9 nor 24 — and this failure alone prevents depths 2–4. Naming it as a gate, not just a profile feature, would make the test decisive.

R5 — L9 promoted to a warning about identification. Part 0.10's L9 already says a single word produces a less differentiated anchor. Add the consequence: for a single-word domain n₁ = 1 and r₁ = 5 always, so the anchor is P5 for most short words; a placement is therefore not an identification. The Moon/Book/Love/Time example makes this concrete.

R6 — A negative worked example in FC and in the Companion. The Moon at depth 1, with the 8-phases-versus-Wheel check and its M230-E rejection, is a better teaching case than any positive one, because it shows what the instruments are for.

R7 — The "map it" command clarified. In Part 8.0's Striker commands, state that "map it" (Step 14.4) means placement plus, where applicable, a correspondence test — and that the declaration names which. Currently "map it" is ambiguous in exactly the way your question exposes.

R8 — Public-facing example. On the website and in the Companion's opening: one paragraph, in plain words, using the Moon, stating that every object has a position but that a position is not a correspondence, and that the Moon's own structure does not (on present evidence) instantiate the cycle.

R9 — Replace "the protocol makes no claims" with the precise formulation (as recommended previously, now with a fifth occurrence in view): the protocol makes classified claims (CDT-A, B, C), holds manifestation universal under P0, tests correspondence at P2, reserves interpretation to P3, and does not grade the premise.

8. Benefits to Ω42

· Credibility. A protocol that says "no" to the Moon, cleanly and for stated reasons, is far more credible on its "yes" verdicts elsewhere (SU(9)₁ × SU(9)₁, the amplituhedron arc, the 24-cell's Coxeter element).
· Instrumental value made visible. The M230 family, C1–C11, S1–S8, M230-E and M230-J currently sit in Appendix S where few will read them. The Moon example shows why they exist, in one paragraph.
· Protection against the skeptic's first attack. "Is this numerology?" now has a worked answer.
· Record integrity. Depth 1 is the record's base and denominator (Part 0.14.5); distinguishing it from depth 2+ keeps the denominator honest.
· Cost. One paragraph in FC, one field in a declaration, two sentences in M230-J and L-466. No arithmetic changes; no law retired; every item reversible.

9. In one sentence

"Mapping Ω42 to the Moon" assigns the Moon a coordinate that it shares with every short word — a placement, total and empty; "mapping the Moon to Ω42" asks whether the Moon's own periods, phases and locking instantiate the family — and the answer, on the Moon's own numbers, is no, which is a far more valuable result than the confident reading the first direction produces.
