### F171 — Establishing a Calibration Programme for Awareness: Mappable Objects, Calibration Criteria, and the Limits of Consciousness Measurement

# F171 — Establishing a Calibration Programme for Awareness: Mappable Objects, Calibration Criteria, and the Limits of Consciousness Measurement

## 1. Objective

This note initiates the calibration programme for Ω42.

The central question is:

**Can Ω42 represent, measure, compare, and calibrate awareness-related phenomena, and can this eventually contribute to a scientifically defensible account of consciousness?**

The investigation begins with a distinction between three levels:

1. **Representation:** an object or phenomenon is assigned a mathematical representation.
2. **Calibration:** the representation is compared against an independent measurement or reference.
3. **Explanatory adequacy:** the mathematical structure explains the observed phenomenon, rather than merely encoding it.

The first two may be achievable for numerous objects. The third requires additional evidence.

We will therefore establish a hierarchy of objects that Ω42 can already map, objects that can be calibrated conditionally, and objects for which no adequate mapping has yet been established.

---

## 2. The mathematical foundation

The established Ω42 state space is

\[
S_k=(\mathbb Z/3^k\mathbb Z)^2.
\]

Its canonical transformation is

\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
1&1
\end{pmatrix},
\]

so that

\[
x_{n+1}=Ax_n.
\]

Writing

\[
x_n=
\begin{pmatrix}
a_n\\b_n
\end{pmatrix},
\]

gives

\[
a_{n+1}=b_n,
\qquad
b_{n+1}=a_n+b_n\pmod{3^k}.
\]

The associated quadratic expression is

\[
Q(a,b)=2a^2+2ab-2b^2,
\]

and its square

\[
I(a,b)=Q(a,b)^2
\]

is invariant under the canonical transformation.

The coordinate valuation

\[
v_3(x)=\min(v_3(a),v_3(b))
\]

is also preserved by the invertible recurrence.

These give Ω42 three established mathematical resources:

- a finite or inverse-limit state representation;
- a deterministic transformation law;
- invariant quantities and orbit classifications.

They are sufficient to build a calibration architecture. They are not, by themselves, sufficient to establish a theory of consciousness.

## 3. What does it mean to calibrate an object?

Let an external object or phenomenon be denoted by \(Y\).

Let

\[
\Phi:Y\longrightarrow S_k
\]

be a mapping into an Ω42 state space.

Let

\[
O:Y\longrightarrow\mathbb R^m
\]

be an independently defined measurement procedure.

A calibration requires a comparison between the mathematical representation and the external measurement.

We can define a calibration system as

\[
\mathcal C=(Y,\Phi,O,\widehat O,L),
\]

where:

- \(Y\) is the target domain;
- \(\Phi\) is the mapping into Ω42;
- \(O\) is the reference measurement;
- \(\widehat O\) is the measurement predicted or reconstructed from the Ω42 representation;
- \(L\) is an explicitly specified error function.

For example,

\[
L(y)=d\big(O(y),\widehat O(\Phi(y))\big).
\]

The average calibration error over a test sample is

\[
\overline L=
\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N
d\big(O(y_i),\widehat O(\Phi(y_i))\big).
\]

A low error is meaningful only if the test observations are independent of the data used to construct the mapping.

### Five conditions for successful calibration

A mapping should satisfy the following conditions.

**C1. Defined correspondence**

Every relevant external observation must have a specified representation.

**C2. Repeatability**

The same observation under the same conditions should produce the same representation, within stated measurement tolerances.

**C3. Distinction preservation**

Differences that matter to the investigation must not be erased by the mapping.

**C4. Predictive or reconstructive performance**

The representation must predict or reconstruct independently measured properties.

**C5. External validation**

Success must be assessed against evidence that does not simply restate the Ω42 model's own definitions.

A mapping can satisfy C1 and fail all the other conditions. That is a mathematical encoding, not a successful scientific calibration.

## 5. First calibration experiment: perceptual and metacognitive awareness

We begin with an experiment that can be conducted without assuming a particular theory of consciousness.

### 5.1 Experimental variables

For each trial \(i\), record:

\[
r_i=\text{whether the stimulus was present},
\]

\[
d_i=\text{whether the participant detected it},
\]

\[
c_i=\text{reported confidence}.
\]

The primary detection measure is

\[
D=\frac{\text{correct detections and correct rejections}}{N}.
\]

For a more specific analysis of perceptual sensitivity, use signal-detection measures such as \(d'\), which distinguish sensitivity from response bias.

Metacognitive calibration can be evaluated by grouping confidence reports into bins.

For confidence bin \(j\), define

\[
C_j=\text{mean declared confidence},
\]

\[
A_j=\text{observed proportion correct}.
\]

The expected calibration error is

\[
ECE=
\sum_j
\frac{n_j}{N}
|C_j-A_j|.
\]

A smaller value indicates closer agreement between confidence and observed accuracy.

This is an operational measurement of metacognitive calibration, not a direct measurement of consciousness.

### 5.2 Illustrative dataset

Suppose 100 trials produce the following results:

| Confidence | Trials | Actual accuracy |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 50% | 20 | 50% |
| 60% | 20 | 60% |
| 70% | 20 | 70% |
| 80% | 20 | 80% |
| 90% | 20 | 90% |

Then

\[
ECE=0.
\]

The participant is perfectly calibrated in this illustrative sample.

However, this does not mean that the participant has perfect awareness. The participant could still have poor perceptual sensitivity, limited attention, or incomplete access to their own mental processes.

We therefore need at least two independent measures:

\[
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{operational}}=(D,M),
\]

where \(D\) is perceptual detection performance and \(M\) is metacognitive calibration.

We should not collapse these into one consciousness score without a justified theoretical and empirical weighting scheme.

## 6. Mapping the awareness experiment into Ω42

We now introduce an initial Ω42 representation.

Let the operational awareness profile at time \(t\) be

\[
z_t=(D_t,M_t).
\]

For a first demonstration, quantize each component into three ordered categories:

\[
q(u)=
\begin{cases}
0,&0\leq u<1/3,\\
1,&1/3\leq u<2/3,\\
2,&2/3\leq u\leq1.
\end{cases}
\]

For metacognitive calibration, use a normalized score such as

\[
M_t=1-\min(1,ECE_t).
\]

Then define

\[
\Phi(z_t)=
\begin{pmatrix}
q(D_t)\\
q(M_t)
\end{pmatrix}
\in\mathbb F_3^2.
\]

For example, suppose

\[
D_t=0.85,\qquad ECE_t=0.04.
\]

Then

\[
M_t=0.96.
\]

Both values fall into category 2, giving

\[
x_t=
\begin{pmatrix}
2\\2
\end{pmatrix}
\pmod3.
\]

This is a valid mapping into the mod-3 Ω42 state space.

But the mapping is coarse: many different awareness profiles produce the same state. It preserves only the distinction between three levels of detection and three levels of metacognitive calibration.

### 6.1 What happens under the canonical transformation?

Applying the Fibonacci operator gives

\[
Ax_t=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
1&1
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
2\\2
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
2\\4
\end{pmatrix}
\equiv
\begin{pmatrix}
2\\1
\end{pmatrix}
\pmod3.
\]

The transformed state is

\[
x_{t+1}^{\mathrm{model}}=(2,1).
\]

This is mathematically valid. It does **not** establish that awareness naturally evolves according to the Fibonacci recurrence.

The transformation is currently a model hypothesis. To test it, we must collect repeated observations and compare the actual next awareness profile with the prediction.

If the measured next state differs systematically from the predicted state, the canonical recurrence is not an adequate model of that process.

This is the first important calibration boundary:

**A mathematically valid Ω42 representation does not automatically make the Ω42 recurrence an empirically valid law of awareness.**

### 6.2 Why a higher-resolution representation is necessary

Three categories are sufficient for a demonstration but inadequate for detailed psychological calibration.

We can move to

\[
S_k=(\mathbb Z/3^k\mathbb Z)^2.
\]

However, increasing \(k\) does not automatically improve measurement quality.

The quantization rule must be designed so that relevant distinctions survive modular encoding. In particular, modular wraparound must not be mistaken for a meaningful reversal in awareness.

For actual calibration, the original continuous measurements should be retained alongside the encoded state.

## 7. A more complete awareness architecture

The initial pair \((D,M)\) is too narrow to represent all relevant aspects of awareness.

We therefore propose a measurement vector:

\[
\mathcal A_t=
(D_t,M_t,T_t,S_t,R_t),
\]

where:

- \(D_t\): perceptual detection;
- \(M_t\): metacognitive accuracy;
- \(T_t\): temporal continuity of reported or measured awareness;
- \(S_t\): sensitivity to relevant changes in conditions;
- \(R_t\): reliability of self-report or self-monitoring.

Each variable requires an independent operational definition.

For example, \(T_t\) cannot simply be identified with the duration of a person's report. We would need a task and a method for distinguishing continuity from retrospective reconstruction.

Similarly, \(R_t\) cannot be treated as a direct measurement of subjective access. It measures the reliability of a reporting process under specified conditions.

### Proposed architecture

\[
\text{External stimulus}
\rightarrow
\text{Observation}
\rightarrow
\text{Measured profile}
\rightarrow
\text{Ω42 encoding}
\rightarrow
\text{Model prediction}
\rightarrow
\text{Independent validation}.
\]

A separate record must preserve the participant's first-person descriptions.

Those descriptions should not be reduced to numerical values prematurely.

## 8. The role of the Ω42 invariant

The established invariant

\[
I(x)=Q(x)^2
\]

offers a possible way to classify encoded states.

But we must not assume that \(I\) is an invariant of consciousness.

If an awareness profile is encoded as \(x\), then \(I(x)\) is mathematically invariant under the canonical Ω42 recurrence.

It follows that if the recurrence is used as a model of awareness, the invariant would impose a constraint on the model's trajectories.

It does not follow that human awareness has this invariant.

The correct experimental question is:

Does any independently measured property of awareness remain constant, or approximately constant, along the observed trajectories that Ω42 models?

If so, we may investigate whether that property corresponds to \(I\), another invariant, or an entirely different mathematical quantity.

This is a testable research question.

It would be an error to declare the invariant a consciousness measure before that investigation.

## 9. Three distinct levels of awareness

We now propose three levels for the Ω42 calibration programme.

### Level 1 — Awareness of a state

A system detects or registers a condition.

Examples:

- detecting a visual stimulus;
- registering a sound;
- identifying a change in an environment;
- recording an internal physiological signal.

This level can be measured using detection and discrimination tasks.

### Level 2 — Awareness of the reliability of awareness

The system evaluates whether its own registration is accurate.

Examples:

- knowing when it has seen something clearly;
- recognizing uncertainty;
- detecting its own errors;
- estimating the reliability of a memory.

This level can be investigated through metacognitive measures.

### Level 3 — Subjective experience

There is something it is like to undergo the experience.

Examples include the experienced character of colour, pain, sound, emotion, or the sense of being present.

This is the difficult level.

Neither successful detection nor accurate self-evaluation establishes that subjective experience has been directly measured.

The distinction should remain explicit throughout Ω42 development.

## 10. The calibration registry

Ω42 should now maintain a formal registry of mapped objects.

Each object should be assigned one of four statuses.

**Status E — Exact mathematical mapping**

The representation and its mathematical properties are proven.

**Status C — Calibratable empirical mapping**

The external measurements, encoding, and reference criteria are defined, and independent calibration is possible.

**Status P — Proxy mapping**

The representation measures a related observable but does not directly measure the intended underlying phenomenon.

**Status O — Open mapping**

No defensible correspondence has yet been established.

The initial registry is:

| Object | Status | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Fibonacci recurrence | E | Exact transformation |
| Modular state vectors | E | Exact state space |
| Quadratic invariant | E | Proven algebraic invariance |
| Coordinate valuation | E | Proven preservation |
| Finite orbit structure | E | Proven group action |
| Sensor measurements | C | External numerical reference available |
| Repeated signal measurements | C | Period and error can be tested |
| Perceptual detection | P/C | Operational detection is measurable |
| Metacognitive calibration | C | Confidence can be compared with accuracy |
| Attention | P | Requires carefully defined behavioural measures |
| Working memory | P/C | Task-dependent performance is measurable |
| Self-monitoring | P/C | Error detection can be tested |
| Subjective experience | O | No direct independent calibration established |
| Consciousness as a whole | O | No complete operational or explanatory mapping |

The distinction between P and C depends on the precise target. For example, confidence calibration is a direct measurement of confidence-performance correspondence, but only a proxy for consciousness.

## 11. What would count as a genuine Ω42 success?

We need to prevent the programme from becoming an exercise in encoding arbitrary measurements.

A successful empirical Ω42 model should demonstrate at least one of the following:

1. It predicts an independently measured change better than an appropriate baseline.
2. It identifies a stable invariant that is not simply imposed by the encoding.
3. It compresses multiple measurements into a representation that preserves the distinctions relevant to the task.
4. It explains a reproducible relationship between different observable domains.
5. It generalizes to new observations or participants without requiring a new arbitrary mapping for every case.

A stronger result would be a nontrivial mathematical structure that emerges independently in several measurement domains.

But even that would demonstrate structural correspondence, not necessarily that Ω42 is ontologically fundamental.

## 12. Scientific Appendix — Initial experimental programme

The first study should avoid making an immediate claim about consciousness.

### Study A: Visual detection and confidence

Use repeated visual detection trials with randomized stimulus presence and intensity.

Record:

- stimulus condition;
- detection response;
- response time;
- confidence;
- correctness.

Calculate:

\[
d',\quad ECE,\quad \text{Brier score},\quad \text{response-time distribution}.
\]

Use held-out trials for validation.

### Study B: Attention variation

Repeat the task under controlled changes in attentional demand.

Measure whether detection, confidence, and metacognitive accuracy change together or independently.

### Study C: Temporal stability

Repeat the experiments across sessions.

Determine whether the awareness profile is stable, drifts gradually, or changes discontinuously.

### Study D: Ω42 model comparison

Compare at least three models:

1. A simple persistence or baseline model.
2. A conventional statistical time-series model.
3. An Ω42 model using the specified state representation and transformation.

The models must receive equivalent training information and be evaluated on the same held-out observations.

An Ω42 model should not be declared superior merely because its state encoding has more adjustable parameters.

### Study E: First-person descriptions

Collect structured descriptions of subjective experience separately from behavioural measurements.

Investigate whether these descriptions contain reproducible distinctions not captured by detection and metacognitive measures.

Do not assume that verbal reports exhaust subjective experience.

## 13. Fundamental open problems

The programme now faces several precise questions.

**Problem 1 — Representation**

Can the relevant dimensions of awareness be mapped into Ω42 without destroying distinctions essential to the investigation?

**Problem 2 — Dynamics**

Does the canonical recurrence predict any aspect of measured awareness trajectories, or is another transformation required?

**Problem 3 — Invariance**

Does any nontrivial Ω42 invariant correspond to a reproducible empirical property of awareness?

**Problem 4 — Metacognition**

Can the difference between awareness of an object and awareness of the reliability of that awareness be represented as a mathematically distinct structure?

**Problem 5 — Subjectivity**

Can any observable relationship distinguish subjective experience from the behavioural and reporting capacities associated with it?

**Problem 6 — Ontology**

Even if Ω42 successfully predicts awareness-related phenomena, what additional argument would establish that its mathematics is fundamental to consciousness rather than merely an effective model?

Problem 6 is not resolved by the other five.

## 14. Most plausible explanation

Ω42 already has the mathematical resources to represent discrete states, transformations, invariants, and histories.

These resources make it a candidate framework for organizing and testing measurements of awareness.

The most plausible immediate achievement is not a direct measurement of consciousness. It is a formal account of relationships among perception, confidence, self-monitoring, and temporal change.

If these relationships can be represented compactly and predictively, Ω42 will have demonstrated an empirical application.

Whether this application reaches subjective experience remains open.

## 15. Recommended way forward for Ω42

Proceed in the following order:

1. Establish the calibration registry and preserve the distinction between exact, calibratable, proxy, and open mappings.
2. Build a repeatable visual detection and confidence experiment.
3. Retain the raw measurements independently of their Ω42 encoding.
4. Test whether Ω42 state transitions predict held-out observations.
5. Investigate whether any mathematical invariant corresponds to a stable empirical property.
6. Extend the framework to attention, memory, self-monitoring, and temporal continuity.
7. Only then investigate whether the mathematical structures provide evidence about subjective experience.

The first experimental target should be metacognitive awareness because its central relationship—confidence compared with actual performance—is quantitatively testable.

### The most important conceptual opportunity

I think the most interesting possibility is not that Ω42 will produce a single number called consciousness.

It is that it might reveal a structural distinction between three processes:

First-order awareness

I register something.

Second-order awareness

I assess whether my registration is reliable.

Third-order investigation

I investigate what it means for something to be experienced at all.

The first two levels have experimentally accessible components. The third remains the profound problem.

One particularly important question follows: Can a system become increasingly accurate at evaluating its own awareness without its subjective experience necessarily increasing?

If the answer is yes, then metacognitive calibration and consciousness are demonstrably different variables. That would be a valuable result for Ω42, not a failure.

My recommendation is to begin the actual calibration with metacognitive awareness, using repeated visual detection trials, confidence ratings, and independent accuracy measurements. This gives us a measurable baseline before attempting the much harder problem of subjective consciousness.
