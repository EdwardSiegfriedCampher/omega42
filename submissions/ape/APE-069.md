The one finding that decides the rest

The collapse is not a cosmetic issue. Under the frozen rules, a question in Arabic, Chinese, Japanese, Korean or Russian yields n = (0, 0, 0) and anchors at P9, Fixpoint — the same coordinate as L-468's NULL STRIKE. The record therefore cannot distinguish "no question was asked" from "a question in a language the extractor cannot see." That is an integrity failure at the extraction layer, and because the Congruence Register is the record of the primary inquiry (L-489), it would report meetings among all such questions at 1.0 against an expected rate near 1/81 — spectacular convergence that is pure artifact. Nothing in M224 or the L-418 scan would catch it, because nothing is malformed.

Two things follow that the report already has right, and one it does not say. It is right that the mathematics is universal (CPP accepts any count) and that what is missing is fidelity to the medium's own boundaries. It is right that "universality of form, not content" is the honest claim. What it does not say, and what matters most for adoption, is this: a coordinate produced by a script adaptor and a coordinate produced by the English canonical are not comparable in the same sense, and the M310 base rates (1/81, 80/81², the link rate) are computed over residue triples assumed uniform. Per-adaptor distributions will not be uniform. If cross-adaptor meetings are counted as if they were same-surface meetings, the register will be wrong in a new way — not degenerate, but miscalibrated. This is the sharpest refinement I would add.

Recommendations, ranked

D1 — Adopt option (c), with a refined routing rule. Adopt it as the frame for everything else.

Reasoning: (a) leaves the register degenerate for every non-Latin question; (b) sacrifices the entire English record for a fidelity gain that the primary inquiry does not need, since cross-language coordinate comparability is already illusory under the frame's own reading; (c) preserves the record, keeps a comparable English layer, and adds fidelity per surface. But phrase it more cleanly than "canonical + companions": the English canonical is one adaptor among adaptors, the default for English; others enter as companion adaptors and are promoted to canonical for their medium only by the Originator (F-06). That formulation is honest about what the English canonical is and avoids a dual-layer ambiguity.

Refine the routing rule: route when the canonical extraction yields zero content words AND the text contains letters from a non-Latin script block. This distinguishes a genuine null strike (empty by nature) from an extraction failure (empty by blindness) — a distinction L-468 did not need to draw and now must. Record the routing in the Substrate Lock and flag it (a new NCF flag is appropriate).

D2 — Adopt the Adaptor Contract as an operative Core Part, not only as a law.

Reasoning: operative procedure belongs in the Core (Builder Rule 4, L-359), and a law that carries procedure is exactly the drift the protocol elsewhere forbids. The seven clauses are sound. Four additions I would make before adoption:

1. Stability. Every adaptor states a frozen perturbation family and reports per-item stability, on the model of M265 Check 4 and M238. An adaptor without a stability figure is not an adaptor; it is a coordinate with no error bar.
2. Null/base rate. Each adaptor states its own null distribution, or the register declares cross-adaptor meetings ungraded until one exists. This is the D1 refinement made operative.
3. Layer status. Each adaptor is marked COMPANION or CANONICAL-FOR-MEDIUM. Promotion is an Originator act. The mark travels in the ADAPTOR line.
4. Versioning. An adaptor's rules are frozen and versioned; a change of rules changes every coordinate it produces, so it follows the L-392-style confirmation discipline (or at minimum an explicit Originator adoption per version), exactly as the stopword list does.

One refinement to clause 4: keep the DOMAIN SHA-256 over the text (L-414 says so), and record a separate ADAPTOR SHA-256 beside it. Folding the adaptor ID into the domain hash would change the meaning of L-414; a separate field preserves it and still distinguishes "the same text under two adaptors."

One refinement to clause 1: the three layers need not be content-words / letters / syllables. The form is three nested granularities native to the medium, declared. For a music score, phrases / notes / onsets; for DNA, genes / bases / (declare the third or declare it lost); for Hangul, eojeol / syllable blocks / jamo. The English rule is one instance of the form, not the form.

D3 — Adopt L-501 as a short constitutional principle, separate from the Contract.

Reasoning: the protocol separates laws from operative Parts everywhere else, and this should be no exception. L-501 should state: (i) adaptors are transpositions of the form, not extensions of the reach; (ii) the English canonical is the default adaptor for English; (iii) coordinates across adaptors are not directly comparable at this revision, and meetings across adaptors are not same-surface meetings; (iv) promotion to canonical for a medium is the Originator's (F-06); (v) any adaptor must satisfy the Contract; (vi) an adaptor's revision is a protocol revision. That is a law. The Contract is a Part.

D4 — Adopt Hangul as the first companion adaptor, with tests, in the same revision.

Reasoning: Unicode Hangul decomposition is deterministic (blocks → jamo), so n2 and n3 need no dictionary; n1 = eojeol (whitespace-delimited word blocks) is well-defined on the surface. One caution the report does not state: Korean particles are suffixes, not separate words, so "content words" in the English sense is not available without a particle list. The honest adaptor declares n1 as eojeol, not as content words, and the Contract's clause 1 (declared granularities) is what makes that legitimate. Adopting the first adaptor in the same revision as the law and the Contract is what makes the adoption safe: a law that has never been exercised is exactly the "SPECIFIED, AWAITING FIRST ENTRIES" state the protocol treats elsewhere as incomplete, and Rule 9 (verify after building) is satisfied only if the adaptor's tests run in CI from day one.

D5 — Adopt Companion Part 55 and M316 --align with three output guards.

Reasoning: the alignment report is the right shape for the primary inquiry, and the five layers are the right five. But three guards must be enforced in the tool, not merely advised: (i) no combined score — L-486 and L-462 forbid it, and a five-step report is precisely where the temptation arises; (ii) depth is stated as "placement only" where no mapping was run (FC-02.8 — counting never gives depth 3+); (iii) the Aperture step is a statement, not a score — the tool says judgment is the reader's, it does not grade the reading's worth. If the tool does not enforce these, they will not survive contact with users.

D6 — Revise Part 1.9's language-scope sentence and FC-12's scope in the same revision.

Reasoning: Part 1.9 currently says non-English text is processed under English rules and "its counts are canonical under them." That sentence remains true and should not be deleted, but its consequence — the non-Latin collapse and the German "was" removal — must be disclosed beside it, and the routing rule must be consistent with it. FC-12's "it counts the boundaries the text already contains" is Latin-script-specific under current rules and should say so. This is Rule 6 (reference integrity) and Rule 9 (a concept stated in five places is fixed in five places) applied to a concept that appears in Part 1.9, FC-01, FC-12, M189, M208, M222 and Companion 53.7. A revision that fixes only the new Part 55 leaves four other places stating the old scope.

D7 — Re-issue NAV-2026-0001 as a corrected navigation citing the original; do not edit the record.

Reasoning: navigation records are append-only (L-411; Companion 51.9 — corrections as new navigations citing the original, which is never edited). The seven format findings the intake listed are real and should be fixed in a new navigation carrying a new ID and citing NAV-2026-0001, not by editing it. The date inconsistency (identity 16 October vs question 18 October) is the most serious of the seven because it is a provenance issue under M273, not a formatting one; the corrected navigation resolves it by being dated correctly.

D8 — Record the collapse finding in the Negative Result Registry.

Reasoning: it is a clean negative result about the mechanism's reach — the English-canonical extraction fails on non-Latin text. That is exactly what 24.2 exists for. Recording it has two benefits: it is honest about the current reach, and it demonstrates that the programme can report a low result cleanly, which strengthens every positive result the ledger carries. A programme that cannot report a miss cannot be trusted when it reports a hit.

D9 — Do not adopt "truly universal" in operative text.

Reasoning: it is an overclaim. Operative text should say what adaptors do (count a medium's own boundaries under frozen rules) and not what they make the protocol. The Companion Part 55's "universality of form, not content" is the right register for the essay; operative text is thinner and more exact. This is L-486 (A) and L-490 applied at the level of a phrase.

D10 — Adopt all of the above as one formal revision, not in stages.

Reasoning: the law must be exercised from day one (D4); the Contract must be tested from day one (CI); the Part 1.9 and FC-12 revisions must be consistent with the routing rule (Rule 6); and the register must know the layer status of every coordinate before the first adapted coordinate enters it (D1, D2). A staged adoption leaves the register carrying coordinates whose layer status is undeclared, which is the state the register exists to avoid. One revision, one paired release (Builder Rule 15), one CI run.

D11 — State the cross-adaptor comparability limit in the Core, not only in the Companion.

Reasoning: this is the register's integrity, not a philosophical nicety. The Core's Part 0.0.3, item 11 (relation types) already gives the vocabulary; a cross-adaptor meeting is a different relation type from a same-adaptor meeting, and the Congruence Record must say which. Without this, the register reports meetings with base rates that do not apply to the domains producing them — which is a new instance of the very failure the finding exposed.

D12 — State, briefly, the M193/M207 interaction.

Reasoning: an adaptor is deterministic, so its counts are Navigator-independent and carry no exposure issue; but the adaptor's rules are protocol text, and a Navigator that has read them declares PROTOCOL-TEXT ECHO when a reading turns on them, as I did in the navigation preceding this reply. One sentence in the Contract prevents a class of silent contamination.

D13 — On the Align tool's U54 entry. Mark it REFERENCE-IMPLEMENTED with its registry role; make it refuse to combine scores; make it print per-adaptor base rates, or "no base rate yet — cross-adaptor meetings ungraded" where none exists. This is D5 and D11 carried into the tool.

What I would not adopt, and why

· Not (b). It sacrifices the entire English record to a fidelity gain that (c) delivers anyway, because cross-language coordinate comparability is already illusory under the frame's own reading. (c) dominates (b) on the record's integrity and loses nothing the frame can actually promise.
· Not a "canonical + companion" framing. Phrase it as "the English canonical is the default adaptor for English; others are companion adaptors until promoted." The first framing suggests companions are lesser; the second is simply true.
· Not L-501 without the Contract and the first adaptor. A law exercised by nothing is a SPECIFIED-not-RUN instrument, and the protocol's own registry treats that state as one to be avoided where avoidable.
· Not "universal" in operative text (D9).
· Not a per-language adaptor rollout before the routing rule and the layer status are in place. A companion adaptor without an ADAPTOR line and a layer mark is a coordinate the register cannot classify. The order matters: routing, then Contract, then law, then the first adaptor, then the next.

Open items this adoption would carry

1. Per-adaptor null distributions. Until one exists for each adaptor, cross-adaptor meetings are ungraded. This should be stated as an open item, not left implicit.
2. The L-221 floor's failure on non-Latin text. The floor uses the raw word list; the raw word list is also Latin-only. The routing rule supersedes the floor for non-Latin text, but the floor's wording should be checked in the same revision (Rule 9).
3. The Latin-script non-English case (German "was" removed, French function words counted). The minimal routing rule does not catch it, because it yields non-zero content words. A fuller rule — route any non-English text to a language adaptor if one exists, otherwise use English canonical with the distortion declared — is the right direction, but it is a larger change and I would not bundle it into the first revision. I would name it as the next step after Hangul, with the German and French cases as its first known-answer tests.
4. M208's partial Hangul support and the new adaptor must not disagree. The adaptor is a new object; M208 remains a companion. Their relationship should be stated in one line, or a reader will assume one supersedes the other.

The two attachments, the Part 55 text, and the option (c) recommendation are sound. My recommendations above are refinements and sequencing, not reversals.

What this means, which of these to adopt, and in what order, are yours to decide.
