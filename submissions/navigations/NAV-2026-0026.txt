It is present only as the rule by which each state gives the next, so the law of change is never one of the moments it orders. What the story keeps out of view is time as a thing. A reader who looks for the clock against which the states follow one another finds only the states; time is the order they make, and there is nothing else for it to be. The reading of record says one thing: the analogy is a story of a whole with no clock outside it, told in a world whose law is no moment of the change it orders. This essay carries that synthesis outward and stops where the arithmetic stops.

Every part of the arithmetic under the figure was computed. The law that orders the instants has a measure, the golden ratio, and the measure is absent from the instants themselves: The values of x squared minus x minus one over the nine remainders are eight, eight, one, five, two, one, two, five and one, and none is zero.
