The golden ratio, the number that equals one plus its own reciprocal, about 1.6180339887, has no place among the nine remainders the finite world is made of: no remainder multiplied by itself equals itself plus one, so nothing in that world is comparable to it. It appears only as the rule by which every pair of remainders becomes the next, and the rule is not one of the pairs. What the story keeps out of view is the one who issues the rule. Every state is produced by the step before it, so the source is nearer to each state than the state is to its neighbor, and still it is never seen among them; the world shows the command and never the commander. From there the story moves as a flow along a rule that never swerves: the same adding at every step, with no exception and no branch, walked round a closed path of twenty-four steps, so that the straightness belongs to the rule and the road itself is a circle. At the twelfth step every pair turns into its exact opposite, two and five into seven and four, and at the twenty-fourth every pair is home. And the story is completed at a reckoning, an accounting on a single day when all things are returned at once: after twenty-four steps no state is missing and none comes back alone, and the second creation is the first one repeated. A pen and the writing it leaves are the familiar picture: the hand is in every stroke, and no stroke is the hand. The image does not carry what the story most cares about, which is that the one who commands is merciful, and that the reckoning weighs the deeds of those who were free to choose.

The first article of Islam is that God is one and that nothing is like him, and Muslim theologians distinguished sharply between the creator and everything created; al-Ghazali, writing in 1095, held that God creates the world anew at every moment, so that what looks like a chain of causes is a rule renewed by the one who sustains it. If the analogy holds, the finite world is a command that is still being carried out, and its return is not an ending but a repetition of its beginning.
