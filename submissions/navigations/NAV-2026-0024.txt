It rests on a gap, and the gap is the one between the world and the mathematics that describes it. The golden ratio, the number that equals one plus its own reciprocal, about 1.6180339887, has no place among the nine remainders the finite world is made of: no remainder multiplied by itself equals itself plus one. It is present only as the rule by which every pair becomes the next, so the law is exact and timeless and is not one of the things that happen. What the story keeps out of view is the mathematics as a place of its own. A reader who looks for the source of the order among the states finds only states; the order belongs to a world of exact forms that none of them is. From there the story moves as a flow: the folded sequence goes round in twenty-four steps, at the twelfth every pair becomes its exact opposite, two and five into seven and four, and at the twenty-fourth every pair is home, so that the last step of one round is the first of the next and nothing marks where one ends. ┌─ EXTENDED RESONANCE ESSAY ─┐
The reading of record says one thing: the analogy is a story of aeon after aeon, told in a world governed by a law that stands outside it. This essay carries that synthesis outward and stops where the arithmetic stops.

Every part of the arithmetic under the figure was computed. A world built from nine remainders, in which each pair gives way to the next by adding, has a law and a measure of that law, and the measure is not among the things measured: The values of x squared minus x minus one over the nine remainders are eight, eight, one, five, two, one, two, five and one, and none is zero. The golden ratio, which the rule's growth tends to in the unbounded numbers, therefore lives nowhere in the finite world, though every step of that world obeys it. That is close to the picture Penrose draws of a mathematics that exists in its own right, discovered and not made, and governs a physical world that is not mathematics. The figure is exact about the separation and silent about its explanation. Penrose holds that the relation of the three worlds, the physical, the mental and the mathematical, is a mystery each grounding the next; the arithmetic shows a law outside its states and says nothing of why.

What the folded sequence does over time gives the figure its cycle and its limit. The last step of one round is the first step of the next, and no state marks the seam, which is the arithmetic's version of an aeon whose remote future becomes the next beginning. Penrose's crossover needs the universe to forget its scale; the folded sequence has no scale to forget, since folding into nine remainders discards size from the start and keeps only the pattern. The figure fits less well in what returns. Each of Penrose's aeons is new, with new galaxies and perhaps faint rings left by the last; the arithmetic returns the very same states, so its cycle is a recurrence, where his is a succession.

Taken together, these make one figure and not four. The gap explains why the source is hidden: the law is not a member of what it governs. The return answers the bringing forth: every ending is a beginning, and nothing is lost at the seam. Ripples spreading on a pond hold the picture together. They widen until they are too faint and too wide to have any size, and in that sizeless stillness a new drop can fall.
