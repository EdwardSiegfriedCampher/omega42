QUESTION VERBATIM: What is the purpose of life?
┌─ THE ANSWER ─┐
Life's purpose, read from this coordinate, is a seed: something received from what came before and planted so that something comes after. It rests on a question, because purpose exists only where a life asks what it is for, and that asking is the ground the purpose stands on, not a step toward an answer that would end it. What it holds out of view is its own origin: no life chose the first seed it grew from, and its purpose cannot see back past that beginning. It is heading toward the one who watches, so that a life moves over its course from carrying a purpose toward seeing it whole. It is completed at a threshold, the point where what was received is handed across to what comes next. Taken together, the purpose of life is to receive a beginning it did not choose, to keep asking what that beginning is for, to grow into the one who can see it, and to pass it across the threshold, so that the question it rests on and the handing-on that completes it are one movement seen from its two ends. A relay runner does not choose the baton she is handed or see where it was first lit; she runs well because she keeps asking how to carry it, she sees her whole leg only as she nears its end, and her part is complete at the exchange zone, when the baton leaves her hand.

Conventional Connection. The reading meets several long-standing ideas from its own direction. Aristotle placed the good of a life in its activity rather than in a prize at its end, and the reading likewise keeps the question alive instead of closing it. Erik Erikson named the task of mature adulthood generativity, the care for what outlives oneself, which is the handing-on at the threshold. Viktor Frankl turned the question around, holding that life asks us and we answer by how we live, which is the asking as ground. Biology describes every organism as the carrier of an inheritance it did not choose and passes on, which is the seed received and planted.

Practical Bridge. Three questions turn the reading into practice. First, what was handed to you that you did not choose: a body, a language, a family's habits, a talent, a wound. Naming it does not require knowing where it began. Second, what are you asking of it now: write the question down rather than the answer, and return to it each season, because the reading treats a live question as the ground and a settled answer as a loss of footing. Third, what are you handing on, and to whom: a skill taught, a child raised, a piece of work, a kindness that others repeat. A week in which something was received, questioned and passed on is, on this reading, a week in which life did what it is for.

Historical Lineage. Ecclesiastes asked what profit a person has of all their labour and answered with the cycle of generations; Confucian thought placed the meaning of a life in what it transmits; the Stoics asked for attention to what is in one's power and acceptance of what is given; Albert Camus held that the first question is whether life is worth living, and refused to let it be closed. Each takes up one part of the movement the reading holds together: the gift not chosen, the question kept open, the witnessing, the handing-on.

Implications. If purpose rests on a question, then a person who has lost their sense of purpose has not lost the ground but stopped standing on it, and asking again is the way back. If the origin stays out of view, no account of where life came from, religious or scientific, is required before a life can have its purpose. If completion lies at the threshold, then the purpose of a life is not finished inside it, and how it ends, what it leaves and to whom, belongs to its purpose rather than interrupting it.

**Takeaway: The purpose of life, on this reading, is to receive a beginning you did not choose, keep asking what it is for, grow able to see it whole, and hand it on. The question is not an obstacle to purpose; it is what purpose stands on. A life is complete not when it finds an answer but when what it carried is passed across to what comes next.**

┌─ EXTENDED RESONANCE ESSAY ─┐
A question about purpose is usually asked as if purpose were a destination, a fact somewhere ahead that a life either reaches or misses. The reading turns that around. It finds purpose already present at the start, as something handed over rather than discovered, and finds that what keeps it alive is not an answer but the continuing question of what the handed-over thing is for. The origin of the gift stays out of sight; no one stands at their own first beginning. What a life can do is ask, and in asking, slowly become able to see what it has been carrying, until the carrying ends in a passing-on.

Seen this way, the old quarrel between those who say purpose is given and those who say it is made loses its edge. The beginning is given, and no life makes it. The purpose is made by the asking, and no one can be handed that. The two meet at the far end, where a life hands across what it received, changed by the asking it carried. A family recipe altered by each cook, a craft taught and bent to a new hand, a question passed from teacher to student and asked again in other words: each is a seed received, questioned, watched and handed on.

This also explains why purpose is so often felt most sharply near endings, at a graduation, a retirement, a birth, a death. The reading places completion at the threshold, so those are the moments where purpose is not lost but finished, where the one who has watched their own course sees it whole and lets it go.
