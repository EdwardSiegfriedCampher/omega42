Research initiative O42-IIRA-01 · Information Integrity

Examine the information you build your world from.

Was it made by a person or an AI? Is it accurate? Where did it come from, how has it changed, and how did it spread? Omega42 brings mathematics, evidence and an AI Navigator to these questions, and keeps them apart, because the origin of a text is not its truth.

The first distinction

Origin is not truth

Three questions are kept separate: origin (human, AI, both or unknown), accuracy (are the statements true?), and intent and use (to inform, mislead, impersonate?). An AI-written article can be accurate; a human-written one can deceive; a genuine photograph can carry a false caption; a real video can be cut to remove its context.

What it gives you

Six things you can do with it

Before you share

Check a viral claim

Split a post into its individual claims and see which are supported, contradicted or unverified, instead of a thumbs-up or thumbs-down for the whole thing.

Count sources, not echoes

See through repetition

Ten sites repeating one article are one source. Find out how many independent confirmations a story has.

Old news, new caption

Test the timeline

Compare when an event happened, when the content was made and when it first appeared, and catch old footage presented as new.

Research and study

Audit a paper or a headline

Check that references exist and say what they are cited for, and measure the gap between a study and the headline about it.

Unconventional sources

Read the unusual fairly

Compare channeled texts, ancient accounts or anomalous reports for provenance and consistency, separating what a source says from what independent evidence shows.

Know what is known

Calibrated uncertainty

Get a profile of what is established and what is open, with the reasons, instead of a confident verdict or a single trust score.

Investigate information

Prepare an investigation for your Navigator.

Paste a claim, an article, a transcript or a link, choose the kind of investigation, and copy the prompt into any AI with the Core and the Companion attached. The prompt is built on your device; nothing is sent anywhere. The investigation itself is carried out by the AI you use, with its usual limits.


    

  






  
The Information Integrity Engine (O42-IIE-01, proposed)

  
Six modules, used alone or together

  

    
1Synthetic content analysisIndicators of AI, human and hybrid production, read as evidence, never as proof.

    
2Provenance and source reconstructionA graph of copies, edits, quotations and translations back to the earliest available source.

    
3Claim verificationEach claim linked to its evidence, its sources, the claims it depends on and the claims that contradict it.

    
4Propagation analysisHow a story spreads, whether apparently independent sources share one origin, whether corrections reach the same audience.

    
5Temporal and contextual analysisEvent, creation, publication and redistribution times compared.

    
6Reliability assessmentEvidence quality, independence and competing explanations, with uncertainty stated. Example: three origins at 0.5, 0.3 and 0.2 carry 1.49 bits of uncertainty, a measure of what is not yet known, not a truth score.

  

  

    
An integrity profile, not a score
Origin evidence · factual support · source independence · temporal consistency · provenance · propagation, each with its finding and reasons. A single number such as "92/100 trustworthy" hides the reasons and suggests a precision that does not exist.

    
Where Omega42's own mathematics enters
Three open experiments: mapping information features into the 81-state space (does it preserve meaningful distinctions?), reading successive versions of a claim as a recurrence (can progressive distortion be detected?), and similarity across apparently independent sources (shared errors, sequences, omissions). Each counts only if it beats ordinary baselines in a registered test.

  






  

    
Safeguards

    
So the checker does not become a source of error

    
    
      
  • Never declare an author to be an AI because a detector gives a high score.
    • 
      
  • Never call a person or organization deliberately deceptive without evidence of intent.
    • 
      
  • Never count agreement between AI systems that share a model or sources as independent verification.
    • 
      
  • Never confuse a source's reputation with the truth of a particular claim.
    • 
      
  • Never treat absence of evidence as proof that something did not happen.
    • 
      
  • Keep the material, the steps and the evidence so every conclusion can be challenged; protect privacy.
    • 
      
  • Challenge misinformation without suppressing disagreement, unconventional research or unpopular ideas.
    • 
    

  

  

    
How it will be tested

    
Five hypotheses, registered before any run

    

      
H-IIE01Structural patterns help tell some AI-generated content from human writing, beyond existing detectors and simple baselines.

      
H-IIE02Provenance graphs expose hidden common origins of apparently independent publications.

      
H-IIE03Recurrence and transformation patterns identify progressive distortion of a claim.

      
H-IIE04Claim-by-claim analysis beats document-level assessment.

      
H-IIE05Combined content, time, source and network analysis improves detection of coordinated manipulation out of sample.

    

    
The benchmark must include unusual, formulaic, technical, translated and non-native human writing, so distinctive people are not mislabeled as machines; development and held-out test data are kept apart.
