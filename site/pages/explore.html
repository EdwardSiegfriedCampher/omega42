How Omega42 works

Two engines. One exploration.

Your browser or any Python computes the exact coordinate of your question. An AI Navigator, guided by the Core and the Companion, explores what that position opens. You can reproduce every number and examine every step of the reasoning.

The path of a reading

Four steps, always in this order

1Strike. You give the question in your own words.
2Place. The counts fix a position on the loop and its four directions.
3Read. An AI Navigator writes one complete finding from them, sealed before anything is compared.
4Judge. You decide what it is worth. Nothing in the reading can do that for you.

Human–AI collaboration

Understanding the method

Every reading is written by an AI acting as Navigator. Like any author it can miscount, misquote or be out of date, and its training shapes its wording. That is why the numbers are built to be recomputed and the reading is built to be examined: recompute what can be recomputed, weigh the rest yourself (Core L-506). Readings are not medical, legal or financial advice.

The parts are known: counting, the Fibonacci rule and AI models. What is new is the configuration: a position anyone can recompute, a reading an AI writes under it, a judgment kept for you, and a prepared test of whether the position adds anything.

Placing and testing

Every object has a position

Give the Moon to Ω42 and it gets a position, as everything does: "the Moon" lands where "book", "sun" and "water" land. Testing the Moon against Ω42 is a different act: take the Moon's own measured structure, fixed first, and ask whether it follows the loop's law. For the Moon it does not, since its periods are counted in Earth's days and it supplies neither the nine nor the twenty-four. What a correspondence would mean, where one is found, is yours. Counting reads no word order, so the direction of a mapping is declared, never computed (Core L-510).

How you ask

Your question first, then other framings

How a question is asked decides where it lands. In a full navigation your own wording is read first and sealed; then the AI shows it from three to five other framings, fixed before it computes where any of them lands, and says what holds across them and what moves. Which framing answers what you meant is yours to say (Core L-511). Ask for the Navigator's account to hear the protocol explained by the AI in its own words, its own view marked as its own (Core L-509).
