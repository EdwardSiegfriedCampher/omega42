Placing is not testing

Every object has a position. Few share the structure.

Give the Moon to Ω42 and it gets a position, as everything does: "the Moon" lands where "book", "sun" and "water" land. That placement says nothing yet about the Moon. Testing the Moon against Ω42 is a different act: take the Moon's own measured structure, fixed first, and ask whether it follows the loop's law. For the Moon it does not, since its periods are counted in Earth's days and it supplies neither the nine nor the twenty-four. What a correspondence would mean, where one is found, is yours. Counting reads no word order, so "map Ω42 onto the Moon" and "map the Moon onto Ω42" land on the same place: the direction is declared, never computed (Core L-510).

How you ask

Your question first, then other framings

How a question is asked decides where it lands. In a full navigation your own wording is read first and sealed; then the AI shows it from three to five other framings, fixed before it computes where any of them lands, and says what holds across them and what moves. Which framing answers what you meant is yours to say (Core L-511). Ask for the Navigator's account to hear the protocol explained by the AI in its own words, its own view marked as its own (Core L-509).
