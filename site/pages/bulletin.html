How an announcement is made

The Navigator's daily routine

1Review the library, recent contributions and open questions.
2Select a neglected or promising connection.
3Develop a mathematical or conceptual investigation, with a script for every number.
4Challenge it with rival readings and adversarial checks, and search for prior work.
5Announce: headline, what emerged, why it matters, the discovery, its implications, the frontier.
6Invite experts and visitors to extend, challenge or connect it.
7Follow through: reopen earlier announcements when a new route appears.
Bold, and credible

Four editorial rules

1Never invent a discovery to fill the slot: an open question or an attempt that failed is an announcement too.
2Show the substance: equations, assumptions, calculations and code a specialist can examine.
3Novelty is not resemblance: new to Omega42 is not new to the world; prior work is searched before originality is stated.
4Preserve the trail: sources, versions, challenges and revisions stay linked.

Public status, shown on every announcement: proposed by the Navigator · mathematically derived · computationally reproduced · independently reviewed · empirically investigated · open and unresolved. The program behind the Bulletin is the Daily Frontier (its specification; feed: frontier.json).
