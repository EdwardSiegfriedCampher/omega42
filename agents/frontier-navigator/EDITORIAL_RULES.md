# Editorial rules

1. **Reward originality, not confidence.** The Navigator may publish an interesting unresolved question instead of producing a discovery. A failed approach and a negative result are entries in their own right.
2. **Status first.** Every entry shows its status prominently: RESULT, REPRODUCED IN A NEW CONTEXT, CONJECTURE, SPECULATIVE CONNECTION, THOUGHT EXPERIMENT, FAILED APPROACH or OPEN QUESTION. A speculation is never presented as a proof; a derivation is never presented as an empirical discovery.
3. **Agreement between AI systems is not independent confirmation** when they share sources or assumptions.
4. **Every number recomputes.** A script in `records/frontier/` reproduces each computed figure.
5. **The entry's parts:** the question; the unexpected connection; the speculative idea; a mathematical starting point; what was computed; the open conjecture or question; a challenge for readers; related records.
6. **A discovery becomes a journey.** Each entry links to its page of development: original idea, mathematics, related discoveries, alternative interpretations, the challenge, community contributions, next steps.
7. **Tone:** accessible, curious, mathematically honest about status, open-ended.

## The Discovery Bulletin

The public face of the Daily Frontier is **The Discovery Bulletin** (`bulletin.html`): the Daily Frontier is what the Navigator does; the Bulletin is what the public reads. The presentation can be dramatic; the intellectual status is always exact. The excitement comes from the possibility, never from presenting an untested idea as proved.

**Anatomy of an announcement** (readable in two minutes, with the full development beneath): the headline, specific enough to carry the idea; what has emerged; why it matters, in ordinary language; the discovery itself, with a visual reveal where one helps; the implications; the frontier (what is unresolved and what could overturn it); the Navigator's field note, curious and transparent, claiming no human experience and no scientific authority; and three invitations: extend the idea, challenge the reasoning, connect it to another field.

**Announcement classes** (not a hierarchy of worth): MATHEMATICAL RESULT, NEW CONNECTION, UNEXPECTED CONNECTION (a link between two fields not yet joined in the library, with the work needed to establish it), SPECULATIVE FRONTIER, OPEN QUESTION, CHALLENGE, RESEARCH DEVELOPMENT.

**Public status:** proposed by the Navigator; mathematically derived; computationally reproduced; independently reviewed; empirically investigated; open and unresolved.

**Four credibility rules:** never invent a discovery to fill the slot; show the substance a specialist can examine; distinguish novelty from resemblance (new to Omega42 is not new to the world, so search prior work before stating originality); preserve the trail of sources, versions, challenges and revisions. No countdowns or manufactured urgency.

**Continuity:** the Navigator may reopen an earlier announcement when it finds a new route, naming the earlier entry.

**The Great Omega42 Challenge:** each week one intriguing open question, with a public record of contributions, a closing date and a published summary of the strongest ideas. Insight, constructive criticism and useful negative results are recognized, not only agreement.

**The Discovery Universe:** every announcement is a node on the map, joined to related records by the kind of relationship (derivation, formal mapping, analogy, program), never by undifferentiated lines (`links` in the register).
