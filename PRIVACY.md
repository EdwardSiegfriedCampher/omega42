# Privacy

The project collects as little personal data as it can.

| Data | Where | Why | Public? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Your GitHub handle and anything you write in an issue, pull request or discussion | GitHub | Submissions and discussion | Yes, by design |
| Name or handle you choose for attribution | `community/contributors.json` | Recognition | Yes |
| Newsletter email address | The newsletter provider (never an issue or the repository) | Sending the newsletter to registered contributors, submitters and commercial users | No |
| Professional credentials you choose to share | The verification issue; the public registry entry | Verified professional listing | Only the fields you mark public |
| Readings made in the web-app and marked public or professional, with the question | The open record, after the personal-detail removal you review | The exchange that makes the web-app free (`COMMERCIAL.md`) | Yes, by your consent |
| Readings made in the web-app and marked personal | The open record as a redacted-domain record: coordinate, state and domain hash, no text; the research corpus only by a separate opt-in | The same exchange, with Companion 28.2's opt-in for personal domains | The record yes; the text no |
| Readings made in commercial applications | The application only | The service you use | No, unless you opt in to submit one |
| Readings made in the Strike app or with your own AI | Nowhere | — | No |
| Site visits | Counted only as aggregate daily page-load totals at the hosting facility: no cookies, no addresses stored, never the question; requests sent with Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control are left out | The Record page's figure of page loads, which counts loads, not people (`tools/dashboard.py`) | Totals only |

- The site sets no cookies and runs no per-visitor analytics. Where page loads are counted, only daily totals are kept, and the figure says it counts loads, not people. The Strike app computes in your browser; your question is not sent anywhere, and no figure counts what you asked.
- The web-app asks for your consent before the first strike, in plain words: every reading made there is submitted to the open record. Before submission, names and personal details are removed and you see the copy that will be published. You may withdraw consent at any time and ask for your submitted readings to be removed from the site, the Congruence Register and the research corpus; copies others have already made under the open licence cannot be recalled, and attribution is changed as you ask. A reading you mark personal is published only as a redacted-domain record, and enters the research corpus only if you opt in to that separately. A question you want to keep to yourself goes through the Strike app or your own AI, which are free with no condition. Where the law requires consent to be freely given and not a condition of a service, the free routes are what keep it so; the Company confirms the terms with a lawyer before launch.
- Commercial applications submit nothing unless you opt in, reading by reading or as a standing choice.
- The newsletter goes only to registered contributors, to people who have submitted Ω42-generated content and to commercial users. Receiving it is your choice, confirmed by email with the provider, and every issue has an unsubscribe link. Registering as a contributor lists your chosen name or handle publicly in `community/contributors.json`; your email address is given only to the provider. The provider's own privacy notice applies.
- Credentials: send documents that prove a qualification only by the private route the verification form names, never in a public issue. After verification the private copy is deleted; the public registry keeps only what you approved (name, field, title, ORCID or professional page, and the verification date).
- You may ask for your attribution to be changed to a handle, or for the registry entry to be removed. Content already published under an open licence stays published, with the attribution changed as you ask.
- Requests: the privacy contact on the site. The Committee is responsible for compliance with data-protection law where it applies (for example GDPR or POPIA).

GitHub's own privacy statement covers what GitHub collects.
